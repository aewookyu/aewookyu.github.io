Xie Zheng looked at Fan Changyu. “You know that constable surnamed Wang. Go find him at once and have him bring the yamen runners to guard the city gates. They must not let the rioters into the city.”

Fan Changyu was puzzled. “If the rioters get into the city, they should be looking for trouble with the county magistrate and those yamen runners. Why are we helping the magistrate keep them out?”

There was an indescribably cold look on Xie Zheng’s face. “They’ve already thrown their lives away to rebel. Do you still think all they want is justice? What they want now is power and wealth! Every household in this city is richer than those farmers. Any one of them could become the object of their deepest hatred. Take one more step, and they could become rebel troops who burn, kill, loot, and commit every evil under the sun. If you don’t want to see this county town looted bare, then do as I say.”

Hearing him put it that way, Fan Changyu’s heart grew heavy for a moment. She pressed her lips together. “Head Constable Wang has already been dismissed by the county magistrate. His word doesn’t carry any weight at the yamen now.”

Xie Zheng furrowed his brows, but still said, “Just go and pass on the message. Tell him the county magistrate has been stripped of his authority. Have him take the yamen runners to the city gates and set up a defense. If they encounter the rioters, pacification comes first. Promise them that the authorities will return all the grain they seized, and that none of them will be held accountable for their crimes.”

“But what if the authorities refuse to return the grain?”

“For now, stabilize the rioters. I’ll think of a way to deal with the rest.” His gaze was calm and steady, somehow making his words easy to trust.

Fan Changyu thought about it, but still had some misgivings. “Didn’t you say they’ve all rebelled because what they’re after is wealth and glory? Will this really be enough to pacify them?”

Xie Zheng glanced at her. “The rioters are willing to fight to the death because they have no way back. Promise not to punish them, then return their grain, and they can go back to farming as they did before. Those with ambition will continue stirring things up and refuse to yield, but those who only wanted to farm honestly and were driven to this point will begin to hesitate.”

Fan Changyu finally understood. He wanted the rioters to lose their unity and throw themselves into confusion first.

For a moment, the Yan Zheng before her felt like a stranger. It seemed she had never truly understood him at all.

Noticing her gaze, Xie Zheng asked, “What is it?”

Fan Changyu shook her head and asked, “How are we getting out?”

The soldiers were still stationed in the alley behind Yixiang Restaurant. If they went out through the mouth of the alley, the soldiers posted outside would inevitably see them. If they knocked the soldiers unconscious and left, it would not be long before someone discovered them lying there, and their whereabouts would still be exposed.

To make matters worse, the other end of the alley was sealed off. It was extremely narrow, meant only to drain rainwater dripping from the eaves between the two houses, and barely wide enough for a single person to pass through. Damp and deprived of sunlight year-round, its walls were covered in slick green moss, and the slightest misstep could send someone slipping.

Xie Zheng glanced at the high wall blocking off the end of the alley and said to Fan Changyu, “Use my shoulders to climb over.”

Fan Changyu sized up their heights and nodded. “All right. Once I’m over, I’ll find a ladder for you.”

When Xie Zheng crouched down at the foot of the wall, she braced one hand against it and stepped onto his broad shoulder with one foot.

With their combined height, Fan Changyu was finally able to reach the top of the wall. She planted both hands on the wall and hauled herself over. As she looked into the courtyard, she saw a room with its window wide open. A man was standing at the desk inside, brush in hand, writing something. Suddenly, he raised his sharp gaze toward her.

Fan Changyu snatched up a loose roof tile from the wall and hurled it at one of his acupoints with lightning speed.

Shock flashed across the man’s face. Before he could utter a word, his entire body crumpled forward onto the writing desk.

Only after throwing the tile did Fan Changyu belatedly realize that the man looked somewhat familiar. She simply could not remember where she had seen him before.

Hearing the commotion inside, Xie Zheng asked, “There’s someone on the other side?”

Fan Changyu nodded. “En. I knocked him unconscious. There happens to be a bamboo ladder in the courtyard. Wait here—I’ll bring it over.”

She leapt down from the wall as she spoke, nimble as a cat.

The bamboo ladder was just the right length to reach the top of the courtyard wall. After Fan Changyu climbed up, she passed it over to the other side, allowing Xie Zheng to make his way into the courtyard as well.

He stepped inside and glanced at the man Fan Changyu had knocked unconscious. A flicker of something strange crossed his eyes. “He’s the owner of the bookshop.”

Why was this residence belonging to the Zhao family conveniently right next door to Yixiang Restaurant?

The suspicion that arose in his mind made him take another look at the unfinished letter on the writing desk. When Zhao Xun collapsed, the brush in his hand had dragged heavily across the page, smearing much of the writing with ink, but the general gist could still be made out.

Xie Zheng’s eyes suddenly turned cold.

When they left, whether intentionally or not, his sleeve happened to knock over the inkstone. Thick black ink splashed across the desk, staining the unfinished letter—and Zhao Xun’s sleeve and half his face along with it.

After hearing that the man was the owner of the bookshop, Fan Changyu was already feeling somewhat guilty. Seeing Xie Zheng knock over his inkstone nearly made her heart leap into her throat. “I… I hit your bookshop owner, and then you knocked over his inkstone too. Won’t he hold it against you?”

She remembered that Xie Zheng had been writing model essays at the bookshop. Hadn’t that forty taels last time included an advance payment for future essays as well?

Xie Zheng paused slightly, evidently not expecting this to be what she was worried about. Some of the coldness in his expression faded. “It’s fine. He may not remember you, and he doesn’t know I was here.”

Fan Changyu thought about it and realized he was right. She herself had nearly failed to recognize him. He was a wealthy merchant who met countless people every day; surely he would not remember her either. She immediately breathed a huge sigh of relief.

Zhao Residence was a two-courtyard house, yet there was scarcely a servant to be seen anywhere in the household. Fan Changyu and Xie Zheng easily slipped out through the side gate.

Fan Changyu thought to herself that the whole detour they had just made was only because both the front entrance and rear alley of Yixiang Restaurant had been placed under guard by the soldiers. She could not help saying, “Yu **Zhanggui** and all the workers at the restaurant have already been thrown into the cells by that corrupt official. Why are they still sending people to watch Yixiang Restaurant? Could it be they’re only after Yu Bao’er?”

Xie Zheng’s expression was dark and unreadable. He merely said, “It’s not impossible.”

Fan Changyu’s expression immediately turned indignant. “Those corrupt officials are truly vicious!”

They were going so far as to make an example of a child just to intimidate everyone else?

Xie Zheng did not respond. Instead, he said, “I left the boy for the time being with the old man who drives your cart.”

Fan Changyu had rented that old man’s ox cart for a month to deliver goods before, so he was someone she could more or less trust.

But having the old man take care of a little young master from a wealthy family would still easily arouse suspicion. Fan Changyu said, “When I go to Head Constable Wang’s house, I’ll take Bao’er with me.”

Xie Zheng nodded.

When the two went their separate ways, he looked at Fan Changyu as though he wanted to say something to her, but in the end, he said nothing.

It was Fan Changyu who noticed his hesitation and asked in confusion, “What is it?”

The sky was overcast, making Xie Zheng’s eyes look darker than usual. “If the rioters get into the city, just make sure you keep yourself safe.”

After a pause, he added, “Don’t trust anyone too easily.”

Fan Changyu’s heart gave a sudden leap. She looked up at him. “Are you leaving?”

There was something deeply strange about him suddenly saying these things to her.

Xie Zheng was momentarily at a loss. His expression turned somewhat unpleasant as he said, “Though I’m hardly someone worthy of trust either, you can still trust me for now.”

After he left, Fan Changyu stood rooted to the spot for a while before finally going to the old cart driver to pick up Yu Bao’er and heading for Head Constable Wang’s home.

When Head Constable Wang heard about the rioters, he was likewise shocked. After pacing back and forth across the room several times, he said to Wang Furen, “Bring me my constable uniform.”

When Wang Furen went into the inner room to fetch the uniform, Head Constable Wang looked at Fan Changyu and said, “Your husband has quite a keen eye and knows a great deal. He’s no ordinary man, is he…”

Fan Changyu said, “His family used to run an escort agency. Perhaps he’s simply seen and experienced more than most.”

Head Constable Wang murmured that that explained it. After changing into his constable uniform, he went out first to find the men who had once served under him.

Wang Furen saw him out the door, worry written all over her face.

Fan Changyu had no idea what Xie Zheng’s next move was. Sending a dismissed constable out to do this was risky.

But once the rioters entered the city and began looting, with no way left to retreat, their ambition and greed would swell with it, like beasts that had tasted blood and could no longer stop. They had to put down that beast before it tasted blood.

After thinking for a moment, she asked Wang Furen, “*You*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="您，the formal 'you'"></sup> said earlier that *you* have maps of the county yamen and the County Magistrate’s residence?”

Wang Furen hesitated, then nodded. “I do. But, lass, what do you have in mind?”

Fan Changyu said, “From what my husband said, it sounds like the grain requisition has gotten so out of hand that the county magistrate has most likely been stripped of his authority. Why don’t we rescue the county magistrate? Whatever else happens, we should at least get Wang **Shu**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="uncle"></sup>’s position as a constable restored. That way, it’ll be easier for him to do what needs to be done.”

No matter who was secretly holding the reins of power at this point, in the eyes of ordinary people and the yamen runners, the county magistrate was still the highest-ranking official in Qingping County.

Wang Furen did not know whether the girl was simply born daring, but even now, when her heart was still pounding with worry, the girl was already thinking up something even more audacious.

Thinking of her husband, who was about to go stop the rioters, she steadied herself and said, “That’s too dangerous. I’ll go with you.”

Fan Changyu thought about it. “There’s a way that isn’t quite so dangerous, but I’ll still need **Shenzi**’s help.”

A flicker of movement crossed Wang Furen’s face.

At Yixiang Restaurant.

A carriage approached the rear alley of Yixiang Restaurant and stopped not far from the entrance. No one emerged from inside, and the guards at the rear gate began quietly watching the carriage.

Two of them exchanged a glance and were just about to go over to take a look when a black shadow suddenly darted out from the other end of the alley. A cudgel swung down hard against the backs of the remaining two guards’ heads, knocking both of them unconscious on the spot.

At Head Constable Wang’s house, Fan Changyu changed into a boy’s clothes and blackened her face with soot until her original features were impossible to make out. She kicked off the paper seal plastered across Yixiang Restaurant’s rear door and ran inside.

The two guards who had been about to investigate the carriage immediately shouted, “An accomplice to the murderers has broken into Yixiang Restaurant to destroy the evidence!”

They hurried in after her to seize Fan Changyu, but she was already waiting for them behind the door.

The moment one stepped inside, she hurled the cudgel at him and knocked him unconscious. The constable behind him drew his blade and swung at Fan Changyu, but she sidestepped the blow and kicked him straight into the slop vat in the rear courtyard. The vat was deep enough that the man folded right into it, and for a long while, he could not even manage to scramble back out.

A moment later, Fan Changyu emerged from the building with something wrapped in her cloak and held against her chest, then hurried out of the courtyard.

The constable in the vat screamed hysterically, “The thief is getting away! The thief is getting away!”

The commotion had already alerted the guards stationed at Yixiang Restaurant’s front entrance. A group of men dressed in constable uniforms—but clearly lacking the bearing of true constables—split into two groups and came racing in from either end of the alley. All they caught sight of was a small figure who seemed to be carrying a child in his arms, hurrying toward the carriage waiting at the mouth of the alley.

Before the soldiers and yamen runners could catch up, the carriage had already sped away.

Snow drifted thickly through the air. The driver wore coarse cloth and a bamboo hat that concealed his face, but the way he snapped the whip made it obvious that he, too, was a trained fighter.

Some of the soldiers who had come around from the front moved to block the carriage. The driver suddenly flicked a second whip from his hand. It was over one *zhang*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="1 zhang ≈ 3.3 meter"></sup> long, and a single strike was enough to tear flesh. With a sweep to either side, the soldiers who had closed in were left sprawled by the roadside, groaning in pain.

The leader of the soldiers shouted, “It must be the accomplice from the restaurant who took that little brat and ran! Quickly, call for reinforcements!”

A signal arrow shot into the gray sky, and before long, the county yamen dispatched another contingent of soldiers.

The people in the carriage were none other than Fan Changyu and Wang Furen.

Wang Furen knew every street and alley in the county like the back of her hand. After a few turns, she had already shaken off the soldiers behind them. Before Fan Changyu jumped down from the carriage, she said, “I’ll have to trouble *Shenzi** to lead these soldiers on a chase for another half an hour. After that, don’t bother with them anymore. Just get yourself away safely.”

Wang Furen lifted the brim of her bamboo hat slightly and asked, “Half an hour—will that be enough time for you?”

Fan Changyu said, “My husband should be heading for the county yamen. I’ll go to the magistrate’s residence from here. With all the soldiers out in full force to capture Yu **Zhanggui**’s son, we should be able to find the magistrate somehow.”

Naturally, Yu Bao’er was not in the carriage either. The thing Fan Changyu had wrapped in her cloak and carried out of Yixiang Restaurant earlier had been nothing more than a small quilt.

Wang Furen only reminded her, “Be careful in everything you do!”

Fan Changyu replied, “**Shenzi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="auntie"></sup> too.”

The carriage slowed down. Once they reached a deserted spot, Fan Changyu jumped off and then took a winding route through several alleys toward the County Magistrate’s residence.

<br><br>

When Fan Changyu reached the County Magistrate’s residence, she discovered that Mother Song was there as well.

She crouched in the shadows and watched as Mother Song stood at the magistrate’s gate with a very young maid, carrying bags and bundles of gifts. She wore a fawning smile as she said, “Yan **Ge’er** is about to head to the capital for the examinations and has been thinking of **Da Xiaojie**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="eldest (大/Da) young lady (小姐/xiaojie) - the eldest daughter of an influential househole)."></sup>. So, you see, he had me buy all these little things to bring to **Da Xiaojie**…”

The steward at the gate said, “Song **Juren** is most thoughtful.”

He instructed the young servant behind him to accept the hairpins and jewelry Mother Song had reluctantly spent money on, yet he made no invitation for her to come inside and sit for a while.

Mother Song’s smile was nearly stiff with strain. She had been *eating closed-door soup*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="吃闭门羹 - an idiom for being refused entry or coldly turned away."></sup> for several days in a row, and after reluctantly spending so much silver on gifts, she was unwilling to leave without earning at least some favor from the magistrate’s family. She said, “The other day, **Furen** praised the pattern of my shoes and said it was pretty. I came today especially to invite **Furen** to have tea with me, and I thought I might as well bring **Furen** the shoe pattern.”

The steward merely said, “**Furen** has caught a chill and has yet to recover. If Song **Furen** has anything to give her, just hand it to **Laonu**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="老奴(Lǎo nú) - lit. this old servant."></sup>.”

Mother Song had originally thought the county magistrate’s family was somewhat beneath her. Once Song Yan passed the metropolitan examination, she had thought, being merely the daughter of a county magistrate, the girl might not necessarily be worthy of her son. It was only because they were living in the same county and would inevitably need the magistrate’s assistance from time to time that she had gone out of her way to cultivate a close relationship with the magistrate’s wife.

Previously, when the magistrate’s wife had been eager to settle the marriage between their children, Mother Song’s little schemes had been clicking away in her head. She dangled the promise of one day becoming the wife of a Juren, then the wife of a Jinshi, before the magistrate’s wife and daughter, keeping them hooked while never actually agreeing to the engagement.

进士 (jìnshì) title for scholar who passed the palace examination; highest level of degree in chinese history.
</div>

Whenever the magistrate’s wife pressed her too hard, she would put on a tearful act and bring up Song Yan’s recent broken engagement. She would say that Song Yan was a filial son who had been willing to shoulder the reputation of being heartless and unfaithful for her sake, breaking off his engagement with the butcher’s Fan family. Who could have known that the Fan family was now practically telling everyone they met that the Song family had wronged them? If Song Yan were to arrange another engagement so soon, it would surely make the Fan girl even more resentful. If she went around spreading malicious rumors, it would inevitably affect Song Yan’s prospects in office. In any case, the two families were bound to become in-laws sooner or later, so why rush things now?

The magistrate’s wife had been taken in by this line of reasoning. Ordinarily, the two women would drink tea and watch opera together, and the magistrate’s wife had always treated her warmly.

During the New Year festivities, Song Yan had happened to cause a scandal with the Fan family at the lantern festival, and for a time, Mother Song had felt she could scarcely hold her head up in public.

She was afraid the magistrate’s wife would now think less of her son. Though she had initially viewed this match merely as a stepping stone, the incident suddenly made Mother Song uneasy. What if her son failed to pass the palace examination and could not go to the capital to take up an official post? Looking across the whole of Qingping County, there was still no marriage more prestigious than one into the magistrate’s family. That was why, on the second day of the New Year, she had brought gifts to the magistrate’s residence to pay her respects.

Who could have expected that she would be *eating the closed-door soup*?

That day, Mother Song returned home so furious she nearly spat blood. Afraid of disturbing her son’s studies, she did not dare tell Song Yan what had happened. But she secretly resolved that she had to repair relations with the magistrate’s family, and for the past two days, she had kept sending gifts to their residence.

When she could no longer get anywhere through the magistrate’s wife, she tried going through the magistrate’s daughter instead. Yet even after sending gifts all the way up to today, she still could not even get through the magistrate family’s front gate.

Mother Song felt as though her dignity had been torn off and thrown onto the ground for someone to trample beneath their feet. By the time she left, she could not even force a smile onto her face. Her expression was livid, and only after turning the corner did she dare spit viciously onto the ground several times. “What an ungrateful wretch! She’s nothing more than a magistrate’s daughter. Does she really think my Yan **Ge’er** is begging to marry her? She has the nerve to accept my gifts, yet she can’t even be bothered to invite me inside for a cup of tea?”

With her back turned, Fan Changyu pretended to browse the wares at a street stall, hearing every word Mother Song said. She cast a faint sideways glance at Mother Song’s retreating figure. Although she had long since stopped caring about the Song family, seeing Mother Song’s ugly behavior still left her able to sigh inwardly at one thing: evil begets its own consequences.

She thought that the magistrate’s family had better have seen through what kind of people this mother and son were. That had to be why they were refusing to entertain them.

She circled around to the rear wall of the magistrate’s residence, climbed up a tree growing beside the wall, and pulled herself over into the compound.

Head Constable Wang had served as a yamen runner for more than a decade and had worked under several county magistrates, so he was thoroughly familiar with the layout of this residence. After looking over the map Wang Furen had given her, Fan Changyu could roughly make out the arrangement of the house as well. She surmised that this area should be the kitchen.

Keeping close to the base of the wall, she slipped quietly forward. After passing through a *chuihua gate*, she happened to see the steward entering the compound and quickly ducked behind the corner of a wall.
垂花门 (chuihua men) — literally “hanging-flower gate”, a  traditional inner gate within a Chinese residence, often leading from one courtyard section to another

The steward pleaded with a man who looked like a guard, holding out the things Mother Song had sent. “**Junye**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="军爷 (Junye) — A respectful form of address for a soldier or military man."></sup>, these are all gifts from the our **Xiaojie**’s future **Guye**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="姑爷 is a household term for a daughter’s husband."></sup>. Please, show a little mercy and let me take them to her.”

A steward of the county magistrate’s residence actually had to ask a guard for permission to carry out his duties?

Something was clearly wrong. Fan Changyu pricked up her ears and listened.

The guard merely let out a cold laugh. “Throw these into the side room with the things from before. If even a word of this gets out, you can forget about keeping your heads!”

The steward was clearly frightened into submission and dared not say another word.

Fan Changyu suddenly realized that the people controlling the magistrate’s residence were certainly no ordinary lot. She made her breathing even quieter and more measured.

She noticed that not a single patch of snow had been cleared from the courtyards throughout the entire residence. She could not tell whether the magistrate’s family had been placed under control and the servants were deliberately neglecting their duties, or whether someone had expressly ordered that the snow not be cleared.

After all, with snow covering the ground, no matter how lightly someone tried to tread across the courtyard, their footsteps would inevitably make a sound as they crushed through it.

Fan Changyu was deep in thought when she suddenly heard footsteps approaching from behind.

She turned around and found herself face-to-face with a young maid carrying a tray.

The maid had just opened her mouth to scream when Fan Changyu closed the distance and struck her sharply at the neck, knocking her unconscious. She caught the tray with one hand and steadied the maid with the other. After glancing around, she nudged open the door of a nearby room with her foot and carried the maid inside.

A short while later, Fan Changyu emerged in a maid’s uniform, carrying the tray openly as though she had every right to be there.

When she turned the corner, the guards beneath the eaves glanced at her. Fan Changyu kept her head lowered as she passed and headed in the direction the steward had gone earlier.

She had studied the map beforehand, and with her good sense of direction, she had little trouble finding the steward’s quarters based on the layout of the residence.

When she pushed the door open, the steward was sitting in a chair, lost in his own misery. The sight of Fan Changyu nearly scared him to death. He toppled right out of his chair, baring his teeth in pain as he scrambled back up, yet still trying to put on the airs of a longtime steward. He pulled a stern face and barked, “Which courtyard are you a maid from? How dare you!”

Fan Changyu figured that if even the county magistrate had been placed under guard, then the order dismissing Head Constable Wang could certainly not have come from the magistrate himself. For all she knew, the magistrate was counting on Head Constable Wang to come save his life.

So she said, “I’m with Constable Wang.”

The anger on the steward’s face froze. A moment later, he looked as though he might burst into tears from sheer relief. “Constable Wang is indeed a seasoned hand. He saw at a glance that something was wrong at the county yamen these past few days…”

Seeing that he looked ready to spend half the day pouring out his grievances, Fan Changyu frowned and cut him off, asking only what she wanted to know, “What happened here?”

The steward’s eyes filled with tears. “A few days ago, didn't the Jizhou Prefectural Office issue an order to requisition grain? A contingent of soldiers carrying military waist tokens from Jizhou Prefectural Office came here to oversee the grain requisition. When my master heard that the order was to collect one *shi* of grain from every person, he pleaded with them, saying that this would drive the common people to the brink of desperation. But the officials sent from above used the grain requisition order to pressure him, telling my master he had no choice but to comply.

“My master had no choice but to order the grain requisition. But the soldiers sent out to collect it beat farmers to death in the countryside. My master was afraid that once word reached He **Daren** of Jizhou Prefectural Office, he would lose his official cap, so he wanted to go to the Prefectural Office in advance and plead guilty. Instead, those soldiers from the Prefectural Office placed him under guard.

“They claimed to be men of Wei Xuan, the Northwestern Military Commissioner, and said that everything was to be done according to their orders. Now that He **Daren** has been dismissed by the Military Commissioner, they accused my master of obstructing the grain requisition and placed him under guard in his own residence. Even **Furen** and **Xiaojie** are forbidden to leave or receive any visitors.”

Fan Changyu’s brows drew together even more tightly. She had heard the name Wei Xuan before. The tragedy that had occurred during the grain requisition in Taizhou was the result of him allowing the men under his command to run rampant.

For a moment, Fan Changyu was uncertain as well. If Wei Xuan truly was as brutal and lawless as people said, and really intended to force the grain requisition through by such means, then what would happen if Head Constable Wang managed to pacify the rioters at the city gate for the moment, only for Wei Xuan to turn around and lead troops to slaughter those people afterward?

After thinking for a while, Fan Changyu said, “How about we tie up the high-ranking official Wei Xuan sent here and have the magistrate return the requisitioned military grain to the people?”

If they tied up the man in charge, he would have no way to order the killing of the common people.

The steward’s lips trembled. He did not even have time to process the second half of what she had said; the first half alone nearly scared the wits out of him. “T-Tie him up? There are more than a dozen soldiers stationed in this residence, and every one of them is highly skilled. The entire county yamen is under their control too. How are we supposed to tie him up?”

Fan Changyu said, “If you can’t beat them, can’t you just drug them or something?”

The steward could not help looking Fan Changyu up and down, wondering to himself whether this really was someone Head Constable Wang had sent to help.

Kidnapping military men from the Jizhou Prefectural Office—what sort of crime was that? If those men came back to settle the score later, there would not be enough heads in this entire residence to satisfy them!

He waved his hands frantically. “No, no, that won’t do! How is my master supposed to answer to those military men afterward?”

Fan Changyu knew the idea was a little underhanded herself. But the magistrate had been in office in Qingping County for three years. Though he had not committed any great atrocities, neither had he done anything particularly good for the common people. Right now, this was the only way. It was only the magistrate they would be throwing under the bus, and if they could make use of him, why not?

She said, “People in Majia Village have already been beaten to death by the soldiers, and those soldiers have driven the people in the surrounding villages to revolt. There are already several thousand rioters gathering to storm the county yamen. Do you think your master won’t be the one they push out to take the fall when they get here? And as the steward of the magistrate’s residence, do you think those rioters won’t hold you responsible too?”

The steward’s lips began trembling again. After weighing the matter for a moment, he said, “There’s no drug like that in the residence. Besides, those men are extremely cautious. They always make the servants taste anything meant for them first.”

Now Fan Changyu was at a loss as well.

Seeing this, the steward said somewhat sheepishly, “Though we do have croton seeds. The main kitchen is making white fungus and lotus seed soup right now.”

A short while later, Fan Changyu headed for the front courtyard carrying a tray, accompanied by a young servant carrying a wooden bucket.

On Fan Changyu’s tray was a white porcelain bowl. Inside was a large snow pear<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="雪梨 — A crisp, pale-fleshed Asian pear."></sup> with its top cut off and the flesh hollowed out. White fungus and lotus seed soup had been poured into the hollowed pear, which was then covered with its cut-off top and simmered over low heat.

Even through the porcelain bowl, the fragrance of white fungus soup mingled with the sweet, fresh scent of pear.

Fan Changyu could only marvel at how wealthy households could come up with such novel ways of preparing food.

The soup in the wooden bucket carried by the young servant was merely ordinary white fungus and lotus seed soup.

Of course, all of the soups had croton seeds added to them.

The steward approached the guard beneath the eaves with a broad smile. “The weather is bitterly cold, so Furen took pity on all you **Junye**s. She had the kitchen prepare some white fungus and lotus seed soup for **Junye**.”

The guard had a faint scar at the corner of his eye. He gave a disdainful snort through his nose, carrying himself with an air of superiority, though it was obvious that he was rather pleased by the deference shown to him.

The steward seemed long accustomed to his cold expression. He had the young servant ladle out a bowl of the soup and drink it first, showing the guard that there was nothing wrong with it. Only then did the guard say, “All right. Leave it here.”

The steward pointed to the tray in Fan Changyu’s hands. “This was specially simmered for the lord inside.”

The guard glanced at Fan Changyu. She kept her head slightly bowed, and at first glance, she really did look like a gentle, demure, and pleasing young maid. The smile on the guard’s face turned even colder. “Just give it to me.”

The steward fawned, “That lord has come all the way here, and Qingping County is such a small place with nothing decent to offer our guest. Just let this girl take it in.”

They insisted on having Fan Changyu go inside for no other reason than this: while croton seeds could cause diarrhea, they would not be enough to incapacitate everyone in the residence in such a short time. If Fan Changyu went in to deliver the soup, she would have a chance to get close to the officer in charge. If she could subdue him, everything that followed would be much easier.

The guard’s mocking expression did not fade. Perhaps having thought of something, he gave Fan Changyu another once-over and said, “I’ll go in and ask the lord.”

He knocked and entered. Inside, a young man was leaning on one elbow as he played a game of Chinese chess against himself. “**Shizi Ye**, the people here insist on sending a beautiful maid in to bring *you* some soup.”

The one who had ambushed and killed the Jizhou soldiers and spent the past several days posing as grain-requisition officers while controlling the entirety of Qingping County was none other than Sui Yuanqing, the son of Prince Changxin, the rebel prince of Chongzhou.

Prince Changxin had two sons. His eldest had been frail and sickly since childhood, so the position of Shizi eventually fell to his younger son.

In the early years, Prince Changxin had kept a low profile and bided his time, while Sui Yuanqing had been known outside merely as a wastrel. It was not until Prince Changxin rebelled that Sui Yuanqing began to distinguish himself on the battlefields of Chongzhou. His methods were so ruthless that he even came to be known as the “Little Marquis Wu’an.”

Hearing his subordinate’s report, Sui Yuanqing gave a cold snort as well and tossed the chess piece in his hand back into the chess bowl. “Wei Xuan is notorious for his brutality and lust. There’s no reason the men under him should turn out to be paragons of virtue. Fine. Let her in. What tricks could a mere county magistrate possibly have up his sleeve?”

The guard acknowledged the order and was about to leave when Sui Yuanqing asked, “Have the scouts sent back any word? Has Wei Xuan arrived with his men?”

The guard replied, “We haven’t received any word yet.”

Sui Yuanqing unconsciously furrowed his brows. With Wei Xuan’s explosive temper and utter incompetence, how could that fool possibly have heard that Qingping County had failed to deliver the requisitioned grain and not immediately led his troops over?

Could something have happened in the Jizhou Prefectural Office?

The rioters from Qingping County were already almost at the county seat. If that fool Wei Xuan did not come, Sui Yuanqing’s carefully arranged stage would not be allowed to go to waste.

He tapped his long fingers against the tabletop and said, “First, transport the money and grain we extorted from the merchants and common people of Qingping County out of here. Send a thousand men to wait at Banpo hillside outside the city. If that fool Wei Xuan doesn’t come, we’ll kill a few rioters in his place.”

The guard was puzzled. “Those rioters intend to defect to our side in Chongzhou. Why does **Shizi** still want to kill them?”

Sui Yuanqing scoffed. “There’s no need to kill them all. Just put on enough of a show to make people throughout the realm lose all faith in the imperial court. If we don’t kill these rioters, how many of them will merely vent their anger and then truly go to Chongzhou to join the army? Only by driving them to the brink will they genuinely choose this path of rebellion.”

The scholar they had deliberately allowed to escape had carried the following message to the Jizhou Prefectural Office: the imperial troops had forcibly requisitioned grain, leaving the common people with no means of survival. When the people tried to go to the Jizhou Prefecture authorities to seek justice, the soldiers slaughtered them all.

By then, no matter how the Wei faction tried to clarify matters, people throughout the realm would only be inclined to believe the scholar’s account. After all, the Wei faction’s reputation had been disreputable for more than a day or two, while behind the scholar’s blood-tear accusations lay the lives of more than ten thousand people from Qingping County.

Things grounded in actual facts always make it easier for people to empathize with them—and easier for them to believe.

The guard hurriedly said, “**Shizi** is truly brilliant.”

Sui Yuanqing paid no attention to the flattery and asked, “Have you caught that little brat yet?”

The guard’s heart tightened. “Half an hour ago, someone broke into Yixiang Restaurant and injured our men. It seems they escaped carrying a child. **Shuxia**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="this subordinate"></sup> has already sent men after them. We should have word soon.”

Sui Yuanqing merely said, “Don’t hurt the child. He is, after all, my **Da Ge**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="eldest brother"></sup>’s flesh and blood.”

The guard ventured another question. “And the woman in the jail…”

Sui Yuanqing raised a pair of cold, steely eyes. “My **Da Ge**’s concubine. What to do with her can be decided by my **Da Ge** himself once we bring her back. Let her suffer in the jail for a couple of days first. Just make sure no one humiliates her.”

The guard acknowledged the order.

After he withdrew, someone entered carrying a tray.

The moment Sui Yuanqing heard those light yet remarkably steady footsteps, the corners of his mouth curled upward coldly.

He lifted his eyes toward the maid. Although he had expected the county magistrate, in an attempt to curry favor with him, would not send someone too shabby, he still showed a flicker of surprise at seeing such a striking beauty in this remote backwater.

Especially her eyes. They were neither bright as stars nor lively as a deer’s. At first glance, what struck one most was that they were beautiful yet guileless—the kind of guileless that made you worry that if you brought her home as a maid, she would only be bullied and pushed around by the others.

Perhaps Fan Changyu had simply grown accustomed to being subjected to Xie Zheng’s sidelong glances. When a strange man suddenly fixed her with such a scrutinizing gaze, she did not feel frightened at all. She merely carried the tray over with steady hands.

Fan Changyu set the bowl of soup on the table. As she reached to take back the tray with one hand, the man said with a faint smile, “You’re quite bold.”

Fan Changyu thought he had realized there was croton in the white fungus soup. Her palms broke out in a clammy sweat, and she thought to herself that this man was clearly cut from the same cloth as Yan Zheng. He might not be as handsome as Yan Zheng, but he was just as sharp—and clearly not easy to fool.

As the saying went, it was better to strike first.

She immediately swung the tray up as though she meant to bring it down over his head. The man’s gaze instantly turned cold, and he thrust out a long arm to intercept it.

But the tray was only a feint.

Fan Changyu drove her foot straight into his abdomen. Sui Yuanqing’s face flashed with astonishment as the pain immediately doubled him over, and Fan Changyu had already brought her other hand down hard against the back of his neck.

An ordinary person would have been knocked unconscious by a blow like that. Sui Yuanqing, however, still had enough strength to overturn the low table and use it to block her. Clutching the back of his neck, he staggered to his feet and darted toward the door with surprising speed.

Fan Changyu had not expected the man’s neck to be that tough. The guards outside heard the commotion of the overturned table and immediately rushed toward the room, “**Jiangjun**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="general. The guard avoided using Shizi Ye here."></sup>?”

Fan Changyu had already anticipated that she might not be able to subdue the man at close range. She immediately pulled out the thin cord she had prepared earlier, its end already tied into a slipknot, and flung it toward Sui Yuanqing’s neck.

Winter clothes were thick enough that the cord, tucked inside her sleeve, had been virtually impossible to spot.

When the guard at the door burst into the room, he saw Fan Changyu with a cord looped around their Shizi’s neck, pulling hard backward. The cord tightened instantly. Sui Yuanqing had one hand braced across his throat, gripping the cord as he struggled against Fan Changyu’s pull, his face flushed a deep red—whether from lack of air or fury, it was impossible to tell.

Sui Yuanqing’s arm strength was astonishing. By all rights, one hard yank on the cord should have sent that overconfident woman flying toward him like a broken kite. Yet she merely staggered once before instantly regaining her footing and matching him force for force, pulling with the strength of a wild bull.

Even Sui Yuanqing’s neck could not withstand the force of both her hands. She dragged him toward her like a dead dog, then hauled him upright and pressed a sharp knife to his throat. Half of his handsome face was contorted by suffocation; the other half was twisted with fury at the thought of cutting the woman behind him into a thousand pieces.

He snarled viciously, “You’d better hope you never fall into my hands. Otherwise, I’ll skin you alive and hang your corpse from the city gate tower!”

Fan Changyu was currently holding him hostage under the county magistrate’s authority, so she was utterly unfazed. She jabbed the sharp boning knife in her hand into his thigh, leaving a shallow, bloody puncture. “Then we’ll see whether you can skin me faster than I can stab you.”

The wound Fan Changyu inflicted was not deep, but it had still pierced flesh and drawn blood. Sui Yuanqing did not so much as make a sound.

The guards outside, however, were terrified. They were worried about him, of course, but they were even more shocked that Sui Yuanqing had actually been captured by a woman.

The guard who had entered the room earlier was one of his personal guards, named Mu Shi. He immediately shouted at Fan Changyu, “Don’t you dare hurt our General!”

Fan Changyu said, “Do as I say, and I won’t hurt him.”

Mu Shi and the others looked to Sui Yuanqing for instructions. Through clenched teeth, Sui Yuanqing forced out, “Do as she says.”

Then, in a voice low enough for only the two of them to hear, he threatened her, “**Laozi** will remember you.”

How had he ever thought this woman was guileless at first glance?!

Fan Changyu thought to herself, why does this man only hold a grudge against her? Why doesn’t he blame the county magistrate for this? She’s doing this on the magistrate’s behalf, too!

After thinking it over, Fan Changyu pressed the boning knife a little deeper against the skin beneath his clothes and called out to the guards outside, “Release our county magistrate at once!”

Mu Shi looked toward the steward, his eyes seeming to say he would gladly tear him apart on the spot.

The steward was trembling all over, practically on the verge of fainting.

A moment later, the county magistrate, who had been imprisoned for days, finally walked out of the room. The moment he stepped into the courtyard and saw the scene before him, he nearly fainted on the spot.

He would rather have stayed locked in that room for another year than walk out only to be confronted with a scene like this!

A faint smile played at the corners of Sui Yuanqing’s mouth as he asked, “My men have released the county magistrate. Can you let me go now?”

Perhaps afraid Fan Changyu would worry about retaliation, he suddenly adopted the air of an elegant young nobleman. “Don’t worry. Even if I intend to capture you, I’ll wait until you’ve made it safely out before I do. I won’t make my move now.”

Just then, a soldier came running in through the main gate. “Report! Rioters have gathered outside the county-city gates. All the prisoners in the county yamen have been released. They seized the military grain that had been requisitioned and are transporting it to the city gates, saying they intend to return all of it to the rioters!”

Sui Yuanqing’s face twisted with rage, yet he smiled as he asked Fan Changyu, “You people who devised this plan certainly thought things through.”

Fan Changyu ignored him. Whatever was happening at the county yamen was most likely Yan Zheng’s work.

The man in her hands was a *hot potato*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="something difficult or dangerous to handle"></sup>. If she actually killed him, she would have murdered a high-ranking official, and she would probably have to spend the rest of her life taking Changning with her to hide out in some bandits’ den.

But if she let him go, she certainly wouldn’t have any peace in the future.

She looked at the county magistrate. “**Xianling Daren**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="Xianling means county magistrate"></sup>, the common folk in the countryside of Qingping County have risen up because of the requisition of military grain. *You* will have to give them an explanation to quell their outrage.”

As she spoke, her eyes flicked meaningfully toward the man she was holding hostage.

The county magistrate’s face had gone white the moment he heard that the rioters had reached the county-city gates. Once the rioters entered the city, they would surely kill a few corrupt officials, and as the magistrate of Qingping County, he would undoubtedly be the first one offered up as a sacrifice.

Once he was dead, the higher authorities would still demand an explanation—and they would probably dump the blame on him anyway. After all, his record in office was nothing remarkable, and a dead man made the perfect scapegoat.

When the county magistrate caught Fan Changyu’s meaningful look, he might have been timid as a mouse when it came to those above him, but anyone who could survive in officialdom was bound to be shrewd. He immediately understood what she meant.

After considering whether it was feasible, his heart suddenly bloomed with delight.

Yes. He did not dare do anything to these people himself, while the rioters needed someone to answer for what had happened. Why not simply push this group forward and let them give the rioters an explanation?

The county magistrate puffed out his belly, which looked as though he were eight months pregnant, the fat on his face trembling slightly. Without even looking at Sui Yuanqing, he said, “The order to requisition the grain was a military command brought by you generals. Since matters have come to this, One will have to trouble you generals to go to the city gates and give the people an explanation.”

What the rioters did with these men was their business.

Sui Yuanqing merely gave a cold laugh. “Fine. Then let’s go to the city gates and give them an explanation.”

Mu Shi caught his gaze and understood at once. The anger on his face receded as well.

They had a thousand men lying in ambush on Banpo hillside outside the city gates. The moment a signal arrow was fired, the troops on the hillside would come charging down. Wiping out the whole of Qingping County would be well within their means!

Outside Qingping County, a force of soldiers flying the banners of Jizhou Prefectural Office came winding along the official road in a grand procession. At their head rode the veteran general He Jingyuan. Clad in heavy armor, the scholarly elegance he usually carried was all but concealed, replaced by an air of stern authority.

Age had caught up with him, however. His beard and hair were streaked with gray, and after barely sleeping these past few days, he looked visibly worn.

Zheng Wenchang rode half a step behind him and said, “Perhaps that scholar exaggerated things. How could a mere county magistrate of Qingping County dare to fleece the people under the pretext of requisitioning grain? I could have brought the troops here and looked into it on *your* behalf. There was no need for *you* to make the journey yourself.”

He Jingyuan shook his head, his gaze old yet commanding. “Qingping County has a salt lake. For something like this to happen at a time when grain is being requisitioned, I fear the circumstances are not so simple.”

No sooner had he finished speaking than a scout came galloping toward them from ahead, urging his horse on with a cracking whip.

“Report! Ten li<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="approx. 5 km"></sup> ahead, at Banpo hillside, we have spotted a force of Chongzhou troops lying in ambush among the mountain woods!”

Even Zheng Wenchang broke into a cold sweat upon hearing the scout's report.
