In Lin’an Town.

After finishing selling the pork, Xie Zheng frowned as he wiped his hands clean with a handkerchief.

He then raised his eyes to glance at the sun. It was already nearing noon, and his handsome brows drew together even more tightly.

Lin’an Town wasn’t far from the county seat. What had taken her so long?

Xie Zheng shut the shop door. As he passed through the marketplace, he noticed a Hu merchant selling various animal pelts and leather goods at a stall. His gaze fell upon a pair of bracers.
<div class="author-note">
  <p>胡商 (húshāng) — “Hu” was a historical Chinese term used broadly for peoples from regions to the north and west of China, particularly those perceived as culturally or ethnically non-Han. A Hu merchant in fiction usually refers to a foreign or Central Asian trade.</p>
  <p>护腕 (hùwàn) — Bracers / wrist guards. These are protective coverings worn around the forearms or wrists, often made of leather. It usually serves both practical and protective purposes.</p>
</div>

Seeing him eyeing the bracers, the Hu merchant called out, “**Gongzi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="young master"></sup>, looking to buy some bracers? These are made of deer skin, a truly fine item. Though for **Gongzi**… they might be a little small. I have some made from muntjac<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="獐子 (zhāngzi): Muntjac, a small deer native to Asia."></sup> skin over here that are larger. Why not take a look?”

As he spoke, he picked up a pair several sizes larger from beside him and held them out to Xie Zheng.

But Xie Zheng didn’t take them. Instead, he picked up the deer-skin bracers and examined them. He raised one hand and gave it a gentle squeeze, as though gauging the size against a memory. After a moment, he said to the Hu merchant, “I’ll take these.”

He paid for them and picked up the bracers, preparing to leave, when he heard several people inside a nearby teahouse sighing heavily.

“Those poor souls from Majia Village. Dozens of lives lost! Those damned officials are truly beneath contempt!”

“Let’s just hope that scholar managed to escape. If he did, perhaps he can expose all the vile things those corrupt officials have done!”

Xie Zheng stopped and turned to look in their direction. Seeing that he seemed interested in what the men were discussing, the Hu merchant sighed and explained, “It’s the tragedy at Majia Village. There was a scholar in the village who couldn’t bear the government’s brutal grain requisitions any longer. He planned to lead the entire village to the Jizhou Prefectural Office and kneel in petition, begging them to leave the farmers some grain for seed.

“Fearing the authorities would discover their plans, the entire village set out last night. But this morning, they were found dead along the official road. Every villager had been hacked to death. The scholar’s whereabouts are unknown. No one knows whether he was captured alive and taken back, or managed to escape.”
<div class="author-note">
官道 (Guān dào) — Official road. An officially maintained public road, often used by government officials, military personnel, and travelers.
</div>

A cold glint flashed through Xie Zheng’s eyes. He asked, “The people in that village were killed by the authorities?”

The Hu merchant said, “That’s what everyone suspects. After all, they were nothing but dirt-poor farmers. Even bandits, when robbing travelers on the road, would target the wealthy. Surely they wouldn’t go out of their way to lie in wait just to kill dozens of poor folk for the sake of sharpening their blades?”

“Come to think of it, there’s something truly strange about the whole thing. The moment the people of Majia Village were killed, every road leading to Jizhou Prefectural Office was blockaded by bandits. What are the odds of that happening? Isn’t it obvious they’re afraid someone will go to the Prefectural Office to lodge a complaint? The farmers from the villages neighboring Majia Village have already taken up arms, saying they’re going to defect to the rebel prince in Chongzhou.”

The Hu merchant shook his head repeatedly as he spoke. He was not a native of Great Yin, and traveled far and wide solely to make a living trading in pelts and leather goods. But as one of the common people himself, he could not help lamenting when he heard of such a tragedy.

Xie Zheng, meanwhile, drew his long brows together. As someone who held power, he naturally saw that something was amiss.

The tragedy at Majia Village seemed almost as though someone were deliberately driving the people of Qingping County into rebellion.

If that scholar was still alive and had managed to escape, he would surely expose the massacre to the authorities in the Jizhou Prefectural Office—and perhaps even to the capital.

The authorities had driven the people to the brink of starvation in their grain requisitions. When the commoners knelt before the county magistrate to plead their case, he refused to heed them. They then planned to kneel before the prefectural authorities instead, only to be massacred halfway there. Anyone who heard of such a tragedy could not help wishing to grind those officials’ bones to dust.

A tragedy capable of stirring public fury and driving the people of Qingping County into rebellion would, without a doubt, deal another military blow to the imperial court.

Thinking of the massacres that had emerged from the grain requisitions in Taizhou Prefecture, the chill in Xie Zheng’s eyes deepened.

It seemed that someone had been deliberately fanning the flames behind every tragedy caused by the grain requisitions.

And the only ones who stood to benefit were undoubtedly the Chongzhou rebels.

In the Jizhou Prefectural Office.

Wei Xuan sat in the seat of honor, watching the men below tally the grain and provisions requisitioned from the various commanderies and counties with an expression of mounting impatience.

Before long, one of his personal guards reported, “**Jiangjun**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="general"></sup>, the grain requisitioned from Qingping County has yet to arrive.”

Wei Xuan’s already foul mood plummeted to rock bottom. He kicked over the low table before him and cursed, “A mere county magistrate dares defy my military orders?”

He rose with his sword in hand. “Someone! Muster the troops! I’m going to Qingping County to requisition the grain myself!”

Just then, another scout came rushing in. “Report—an urgent dispatch from Yanzhou Prefecture, *eight-hundred-li emergency dispatch*!”
<div class="author-note">
八百里加急 - an exceptionally urgent military communication, sent by relay riders who changed horses at courier stations along the route. The same one mentioned in Chapter 29.
</div>

Wei Xuan’s expression darkened. Yanzhou Prefecture was nothing but a poverty-stricken backwater nestled against the Yan Mountains, a place where Xie Zheng’s former subordinates had been banished. What could possibly warrant an urgent dispatch from there?

He unfolded the letter, and the moment he saw the familiar handwriting, the blood throughout his body seemed to surge backward.

His personal guards had no idea what had suddenly made their general look so stricken. But in the next instant, they watched as Wei Xuan abruptly drew his sword and hacked the low table he had kicked over clean in half, his eyes bulging with rage. “He’s not dead! He deliberately waited until now to show himself. Isn’t it obvious? He saw that I’d suffered a defeat and wants to use this to humiliate me!”

One of his trusted aides picked up the sheet of paper Wei Xuan had thrown to the floor. Upon seeing the forceful, unrestrained handwriting and the three characters “Xie Jiuheng” in the signature, he too was utterly shaken.

The Marquis Wu’an of Great Yin was surnamed Xie, named Zheng, and styled Jiuheng.
<div class="author-note">
字 (style/courtesy name) - A traditional Chinese personal name adopted upon reaching adulthood, used in formal or respectful contexts.
</div>

The courtesy name had been given to him by Grand Tutor Tao. Grand Tutor Tao had said that the character Zheng carried too much ferocity, and feared Xie Zheng might rush headlong after quick success. He had therefore chosen Jiuheng as his style name, to temper him. Where others need only deliberate *three times before acting*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="From the saying 三思而后行 (sān sī ér hòu xíng), “think three times before acting,” from the Analects. "></sup>, Xie Zheng would do well to weigh matters nine times over.
<div class="author-note">
  <p>征 (zhēng) — can mean “to go on a military campaign,” “to conquer,” or “to levy“. </p>
  <p>九衡 (Jiǔhéng) - 衡 (héng) means “to weigh,” “to measure,” or “to deliberate,” while 九(Jiǔ) means “nine”. </p>
</div>

And all these years, Xie Zheng had indeed lived up to Grand Tutor Tao’s hopes. He had never acted rashly on the battlefield. Though he had made his name at a young age, his steadiness was no less than that of a veteran general.

The personal guard was Wei Xuan’s trusted confidant, and naturally knew of the plot Wei Yan and his son had devised against Marquis Wu’an on the Chongzhou battlefield.

He immediately said, “Marquis Wu’an has been lying low until now. He must have been secretly building up his strength, waiting to avenge what happened that day. In his letter, he tells *you* to withdraw to Huizhou and hold the northwestern gateway against foreign enemies. That may well be a scheme. We cannot afford to linger in the northwest! The Prime Minister’s transfer order should be arriving soon. **Jiangjun**, returning to the capital ahead of it is the wisest course!”

Wei Xuan seized the guard by the collar and snarled, “You think I’m afraid of him?”

The guard knew that Wei Xuan was always eager to compete with Marquis Wu’an, and could not stand anyone suggesting he was inferior to him. But at this moment, he could no longer afford to tread carefully around his temper. “**Jiangjun**, please don’t let wounded pride dictate *your* actions! The northwest is already in such turmoil, and the seventy thousand troops remaining in Huizhou were all trained by Marquis Wu’an himself. When he died, they believed he had perished at the hands of the Chongzhou rebels, and followed *your* orders only to avenge him. But now that Marquis Wu’an is still alive, we in the northwest are nothing more than fish on Marquis Wu’an’s chopping block!”

Wei Xuan knew full well that everything his personal guard had said was true. But the more clearly he understood it, the more furious he became. He had been overshadowed by this man since childhood. Xie Zheng was a thorn driven into his eye.

Until that thorn was pulled out, he would never know a moment’s peace.

In the end, however, Wei Xuan had no choice but to swallow his pride for now. With two thousand personal guards in tow, he stormed out of the Jizhou Prefectural Office in a fury.

When He Jingyuan heard about it at his residence, he let out a long sigh, feeling both relieved and ashamed.

Relieved that the famed “Killer General” was merciless only toward foreign enemies, while still retaining compassion for the people of Great Yin.

Ashamed that he, as the local governor entrusted with the welfare of the people, had allowed Wei Xuan to drive the people of Jizhou Prefectural Office to such desperate straits.

Zheng Wenchang asked him, “**Daren**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="My Lord; a term of address to an official by his subordinates or the common people."></sup>, what should be done with the military provisions that have been requisitioned?”

He Jingyuan said, “Return the grain meant for seed to the farmers. Spring is about to begin. We cannot delay next year’s planting.”

Zheng Wenchang acknowledged the order.

He Jingyuan asked, “I heard one county failed to send in its grain. Do you know which county it was?”

Zheng Wenchang answered, “Qingping County.”

Hearing that place name again, He Jingyuan’s gaze darkened. “County Magistrate Cui Shoude has *the gall of a rat*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="鼠胆 - spineless man."></sup>. How would he dare refuse to requisition the grain? There must be something strange about this. Take some men and look into it.”

Zheng Wenchang had just begun to clasp his hands in salute when a guard suddenly hurried through the door. “**Daren**, something terrible has happened! A scholar is beating the grievance drum outside the prefectural office, composing verses to denounce the authorities for forcibly requisitioning military grain and slaughtering farmers in the fields. The whole city is already in an uproar!”
<div class="author-note">
击鼓鸣冤 — “Beat the grievance drum” is a traditional method of petitioning the authorities directly. A person could beat a drum outside a government office to demand that officials hear their grievance.
</div>

Both He Jingyuan and Zheng Wenchang were startled.

Zheng Wenchang hurriedly clasped his hands and said, “After receiving **Daren**’s order, I had men keeping watch on the soldiers Wei Xuan sent to requisition the grain. We never discovered them killing anyone or looting the grain.”

He Jingyuan merely instructed the guard, “Bring him here. I’ll question him myself.”

The guard acknowledged the order and left.

In Qingping County.

Without a doubt, Wang Furen rejected Fan Changyu’s suggestion of kidnapping the county magistrate. She said helplessly, “There are more than a hundred yamen runners at the county office all told. How could we possibly kidnap the magistrate?”

Afraid of frightening Wang Furen, Fan Changyu lowered her head and said nothing. But what she was thinking was: So what if there are that many of them? They can’t possibly follow the magistrate all twenty-four hours of the day. There has to be a time when he’s alone.

Wang Furen was about to say something else when a commotion broke out farther down the street.

A squad of government soldiers, fierce as wolves and tigers, marched past with a group of people *bound tightly from head to toe*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="五花大绑 — A traditional expression for someone being bound extremely securely, often with ropes wrapped around the body in multiple places."></sup>. Fan Changyu recognized their clothing and cried out in shock, “Aren’t those the workers from Yixiang Restaurant?”

Wang Furen’s heart gave a lurch as well. “The magistrate is making his move this soon?”

Fan Changyu didn’t see Yu Qianqian among the people being escorted. She hurried forward and squeezed into the crowd of onlookers to get a better look.

The commoners around her were also murmuring among themselves.

“How did all the cooks and workers from Yixiang Restaurant get arrested?”

“I heard someone died after eating food from Yixiang Restaurant. The family carried the coffin to the entrance of Yixiang Restaurant and caused a scene. The authorities sealed the place while investigating the case and took all the workers back for questioning.”

Fan Changyu rose onto her toes and peered into the procession of soldiers escorting the prisoners. At last, she spotted Yu Qianqian walking in the middle with her hands bound behind her back.

Yu Qianqian saw her too. Without drawing attention to herself, she shook her head, signaling Fan Changyu not to come over. Then she silently mouthed two words.

Fan Changyu recognized the words from her lip movements.

Bao’er.

Fan Changyu carefully scanned the procession but saw no child among them. She realized that Yu Qianqian must have hidden Yu Bao’er somewhere. By silently mouthing Bao’er to her, Yu Qianqian was asking her to look after the child for her.

Wang Furen had already caught up. Afraid Fan Changyu might act rashly, she kept a firm grip on one of her hands and lowered her voice beside her ear, “No matter how close you are to that shopkeeper, don’t go over there now. If you attract the soldiers’ attention, you might end up bringing trouble upon yourself.”

Fan Changyu understood this as well and forcibly restrained herself from doing anything.

Once the squad of soldiers had left, Wang Furen looked at Fan Changyu and said, “If you need a map of the county government office or the magistrate’s residence, I can get one for you.”

Fan Changyu knew that given the Wang family’s circumstances, Wang Furen was already taking a considerable risk by offering this much. She thanked her and said she would come to collect it when needed, then hurried toward Yixiang Restaurant.

Yu Qianqian had been arrested at Yixiang Restaurant itself. There was a chance Yu Bao’er had been hidden somewhere inside.

When Fan Changyu reached the main street, she could already see from a distance that a seal had been affixed across the imposing entrance to Yixiang Restaurant. She went around to the back alley and found that the courtyards where Yixiang Restaurant’s attendants lived had been sealed as well.

She glanced at the rear courtyard wall of Yixiang Restaurant and was just about to climb over it when an arm suddenly shot out beside her and dragged her into a narrow, secluded alley between the outer walls of two courtyards.

Fan Changyu immediately grabbed the other person by the collar. She was already tensing her arm to slam him into the ground when the familiar bitter scent of medicine mixed with the fragrance of dried tangerine-peel candy reached her nose, and the force in her hand abruptly slackened.

She called out, “Yan Zheng?”

Xie Zheng lowered his eyes, signaling her not to make a sound. His phoenix eyes swept coldly toward the mouth of the alley, and Fan Changyu instinctively grew wary as well.

The footsteps of a squad of soldiers approached from afar. They stopped outside Yixiang Restaurant’s back entrance. “The magistrate has ordered that Yixiang Restaurant is to remain sealed until the murder case is resolved! To prevent the destruction of evidence, guard this building closely!”

Fan Changyu whispered, “Yu Bao’er wasn’t taken by the soldiers. I’m worried that Yu **Zhanggui** hid him somewhere inside.”

The two of them were standing extremely close, close enough that they could clearly hear each other breathing. To keep the soldiers outside from hearing them, she spoke in an exceedingly low voice.

Xie Zheng felt as though there were insects crawling inside his ears. He frowned and straightened, putting some distance between them.

“I got here a quarter of an hour before you. I already went inside and brought him out.”

Fan Changyu breathed a sigh of relief. Only then did she think to ask, “Weren’t you at the meat shop? How did you end up coming to the county seat?”

Xie Zheng’s gaze turned slightly cold, “After I finished selling the pork, I noticed you hadn’t returned for a long time, so I came to see what was happening.”

Fan Changyu replied, “I’m fine. It’s just that Yu **Zhanggui** has run into trouble.”

After telling him about the conclusions she and Yu Qianqian had reached, she added, “I was planning to kidnap the county magistrate and rescue Yu **Zhanggui**. You take Yu Bao’er and Ningniang somewhere safe and hide. If I get arrested, I’ll have to trouble you to look after Ningniang.”

Xie Zheng frowned at her. “Who gave you that stupid idea?”

Fan Changyu was baffled by his response. After thinking it over, she decided he must be angry just as he was about to leave, she was putting herself in danger, and even added the two children to his care. “I thought of it myself. And I was only saying there was a possibility I might get caught. I’d obviously make my move when there weren’t many people around the magistrate. How could I possibly get caught…”

Xie Zheng scoffed. “There are already quite a few farmers in the countryside who have rebelled. They’re preparing to storm the county office and then join the rebel prince. Do you really think the magistrate, if he were the mastermind, would put himself in such danger?”

Fan Changyu understood what he meant. Even kidnapping the county magistrate would not save Yu Qianqian.

She thought for a while but could not come up with another plan. She looked at Xie Zheng with a pair of clear, guileless eyes. “Then what do we do? If we find out who the mastermind is, would kidnapping him help?”

Hearing that she still had not abandoned the idea of kidnapping someone, Xie Zheng was so exasperated he almost laughed.

He said, “Kidnapping anyone will be useless. Someone is deliberately sowing discord, intending to drive the people of Qingping County into rebellion and then lure the Jizhou troops here to suppress the ‘rebels’. Once that happens, the rumors that the imperial court’s grain requisitions drove the common people to revolt will become an established fact.”

Qingping County had failed to send the requisitioned grain to Jizhou Prefectural Office. Given Wei Xuan’s temperament, he would inevitably charge into Qingping County himself. Once he clashed with the rebels, there was every chance Wei Xuan would order his men to massacre the entire county.
