Because of the conscription, the already quiet Lin’an Town had grown even quieter. The market was no longer as lively as it once was.

The New Year had passed as well. Apart from weddings and funerals, hardly any household was slaughtering pigs anymore, so Fan Changyu suddenly found herself with plenty of free time.

Business at the meat market was poor too. Quite a few butcher shops had temporarily closed. Qingping County was not far from Lucheng City, and many people were growing anxious. Some wealthy merchants who had access to better information had even begun selling off their properties and fleeing south.

For the past two days, Fan Changyu had been staying home looking after the two children. Although she had not received much education herself, she had at least learned to recognize all the characters in texts such as the Three Character Classic and the Thousand Character Classic. She had planned to teach Changning and Yu Bao’er how to read.

Unexpectedly, Yu Bao’er already knew quite a number of characters at his young age. When he gripped a charcoal stick and wrote on the ground, the characters he produced actually looked surprisingly proper.

Changning was superior to Yu Bao’er in practically every other regard; when she took him out to play, he was the one who listened to her.

But upon discovering that this seemingly dull-witted fellow was better at reading and writing than she was, Changning was deeply dissatisfied. She no longer felt like playing and instead clung to Fan Changyu, pestering her to teach her characters.

Yu Bao’er, on the other hand, was eager to help. “If you want to learn, I can teach you.”

Changning tugged at the hem of Fan Changyu’s clothing and gave a derisive snort through her nose. “I’m not learning from you. I have **Ah Jie** to teach me. Once **Ah Jie** has taught me every character she knows, I’ll also have **Jiefu** teach me. I’ll know even more character than you!”

Fan Changyu was in the middle of opening the Three Character Classic to teach Changning that day’s five characters when she suddenly heard her mention Yan Zheng. For a moment, her mind went blank.

So many days had passed already. She wondered whether the conscripted soldiers had reached Lucheng City yet.

Several tens of thousands of men had been recruited this time. Carpenter Zhao had gone as both a veterinarian and a carpenter, and if Yan Zheng had been assigned to the infantry, the chances of Carpenter Zhao encountering him would be pitifully slim.

If he had been selected for the cavalry instead, the odds of Carpenter Zhao finding out where he was would be somewhat better.

Changning noticed that Fan Changyu had been holding the book without saying anything for a long while and gently tugged at her sleeve. “**Ah Jie**, what’s wrong?”

Fan Changyu gathered her thoughts and said, “Nothing. Come, let’s start by learning these five characters today…”

The four treasures of study (rushes, ink, paper, and inkstones) were precious, so Fan Changyu did not hand them over to the two children to waste. Instead, she used charcoal sticks and had them write on a clean stone slab.

While Changning bent her head over her writing practice, Fan Changyu slowly opened the Four Books, which Yan Zheng had carefully annotated.

She began with the Analects. Since Yan Zheng had previously taught her two passages from it, and the entire text was filled with detailed explanations, she found it surprisingly easy enough to read.

At noon, someone knocked on the Fan family's courtyard gate.

Fan Changyu went to open it and, seeing Yu Qianqian standing outside, hurried to warmly usher her inside.

Yu Qianqian wore a dark-colored cloak. Though she had a smile on her face, she looked somewhat haggard. She said, “Changyu **Meizi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. younger sister."></sup>, I'm afraid I'm in a real hurry today, so I won't come in. I've come to take Bao’er home.”

The moment Yu Bao’er heard his mother's voice, he ran out from the courtyard, wrapped his arms around Yu Qianqian's leg, and looked up at her with a delighted cry of “Mom!”

Yu Qianqian stroked the child's head before saying to Fan Changyu, “I’ve really put Changyu **Meizi** to a lot of trouble, looking after Bao’er all this time.”

Fan Changyu quickly assured her it was nothing.

Not seeing Xie Zheng, Yu Qianqian asked, “The military conscription took place two days ago, did your husband go to Lucheng  too?”

Fan Changyu confirmed that he had, then invited Yu Qianqian inside to sit once more, but Yu Qianqian politely declined again.

Looking at Fan Changyu, Yu Qianqian hesitated briefly before saying, “Changyu **Meizi**, to be honest, all the wealthy merchants in Qingping County have already been calling in favors and transferring their assets south. I've sold off both Yixiang Restaurant establishments at a reduced price as well. I've already made arrangements with the people at the city gates, and come the You hour<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="between 5 p.m. to 7 p.m."></sup>, my family will be leaving the city for Jiangnan. We don't even know whether Lucheng can hold. Changyu **Meizi**, come with me to Jiangnan. If you're worried about your husband, you can always return to Qingping County after the war is over.”

Fan Changyu finally understood why Yu Qianqian had seemed so rushed when she arrived. After a moment's hesitation, she gently declined. “Thank you for the kindness, **Zhanggui**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="manager/shopkeeper"></sup>, but there are still many things at home I haven't arranged. If I leave in such a hurry, and the authorities issue another order for taxes or grain requisitions, those nine households in this alley tied to me through collective liability will be the ones to suffer.”

The ten households living next to one another were not permitted to relocate at will. Even if they wished to move, they had to go through the authorities to process household transfer documents, and the procedure was extremely cumbersome.

After several deaths had occurred in Fan Changyu's household, she had planned to take Changning elsewhere to lie low, but that would have required disposing of property and processing official documents, among other things. The process dragged on for several days, so long that even by the time the authorities had formally closed the case, she still had not managed to complete the paperwork. Later, when she decided against hiding out elsewhere and living a life constantly on the run, the plan was temporarily shelved.

Yu Qianqian naturally understood how difficult it was for ordinary households to obtain such documents at a critical juncture like this, with the city lockdown order still in effect. Merchants like them had only managed to get their paperwork approved by offering considerable benefits to the officials, under the pretext of leaving with their trading caravans to purchase goods.

She squeezed Fan Changyu’s hand firmly and said, “I’m only a merchant. I can’t take anyone else with me, but if you’re willing to leave with me, just come to the city gate at the You hour today.”

Fan Changyu nodded. “I understand **Zhanggui**’s kindness.”

It was just that she truly couldn’t leave right now. Quite apart from the cumbersome household relocation procedures, Carpenter Zhao had already been taken away for conscription, leaving only Auntie Zhao behind. Fan Changyu couldn’t abandon her either.

Auntie Zhao was practically half a grandmother to both her and Changning.

Seeing that she couldn’t persuade Fan Changyu, Yu Qianqian did not press the matter further. She lowered her head toward Yu Bao’er and said, “Bao’er, say goodbye to Changyu **Gugu**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. paternal auntie"></sup> and Changning **Meimei**.”

Yu Bao’er knew Yu Qianqian had come to take him home, but he hadn’t expected them to be leaving Qingping County altogether. He turned to Fan Changyu. “Goodbye, Changyu **Gugu**.”

Then he glanced at Changning, who was clutching the hem of Fan Changyu’s skirt, and said, “I’ll teach you how to read in the future.”

Changning was thoroughly indignant. “I’ll definitely know more characters than you!”

The two children were too preoccupied with bickering to feel the sadness of parting. Seeing them, Fan Changyu and Yu Qianqian couldn’t help but burst into laughter, and the sorrow of their farewell lightened considerably.

Fan Changyu held Changning’s hand as she escorted Yu Qianqian and her son to the carriage outside the alley.

Yu Bao’er was already about to climb into the carriage when he suddenly came running back, took off the jade pendant hanging around his neck, and held it out to Changning, “This is for you.”

Fan Changyu hurriedly stopped him and said to Yu Qianqian, “This is too valuable.”

Yu Qianqian merely smiled gently, “Let Ningniang keep it. This child is terribly lonely. Whenever he meets a playmate, he can never bear to part with them, and always gives them his favorite possession. It’s simply a token of his affection.”

Only after Fan Changyu nodded did Changning accept the jade pendant.

She tugged at the hem of her clothes and looked at Yu Bao’er, “But I don’t have anything to give you.”

Yu Bao’er pointed at the straw grasshopper hanging from her little pouch. “I want that.”

Changning had practically been raised by Fan Changyu, and in many ways she was a very straightforward child. She had never seen jade before and had no idea that the pendant was valuable, only that its pale, lustrous surface was very pretty.

But she also really liked her straw grasshopper. Changning hesitated for a moment, then decided that Yu Bao’er really did seem to like it, so she untied it and gave it to him.

She said, “This is a grasshopper Zhao **Shu** made for me before he was taken away. Zhao **Shu** has gone to the army, so no one will make grasshoppers for me anymore. You have to keep it safe. If you don’t want it anymore someday, bring the grasshopper back and trade it for your little pendant.”

“I’ll keep it safe,” Yu Bao’er promised.

He was still too small to climb into the carriage by himself, so when Yu Qianqian lifted him up, one of her wide sleeves slipped down slightly, and the pair of broad jade bangles on her wrist slid down with it.

Fan Changyu noticed what looked like marks left from having been bound around Yu Qianqian’s wrist.

She guessed that they had been left from Yu Qianqian’s time in prison, and her brows furrowed as her heart ached for her.

When Yu Qianqian turned back to say goodbye to Fan Changyu, she saw Fan Changyu staring at her wrist. The smile on her face faltered slightly, and she instinctively used her sleeve to cover the marks before continuing, “Then we’ll be on our way.”

Fan Changyu didn’t notice anything amiss and merely smiled, “Have a safe journey.”

Once Yu Qianqian had climbed into the carriage as well, the driver finally urged the horses forward and drove away.

As Fan Changyu walked home with Changning, she noticed that the girl kept her head lowered, occasionally kicking little stones along the road with the tip of her shoe. She seemed rather downcast.

When Fan Changyu crouched down, she saw that Changning’s eyes were already red. “Are you sad to part with Bao’er?”

Changning nodded, then shook her head. Looking rather sad, she said, “Sunsun is gone, **Jiefu** is gone, Zhao **Shu** is gone too. Ningniang misses them…”

Fan Changyu pulled her little sister into her arms and gently patted her back again and again. For a moment, her own heart felt somewhat melancholy.

She said, “When the war is over, they’ll come back.”

Several hundred li away, in Lucheng City.

He Jingyuan had just arrived at the city gates with the newly conscripted soldiers when he encountered a contingent of troops from Yanzhou Prefecture. The man leading them spotted him from a distance and smiled as he cupped his hands in greeting. With a pair of fox-like eyes that looked utterly crafty, he called out, “He **Daren**.”

Seeing the man dressed entirely in white, with such a handsome face that he looked like the *Xiao Zhu* on an opera stage, He Jingyuan could not help but frown.
<div class="author-note">
小生 — Traditionally the term for a young male role in Chinese opera, especially the refined, handsome scholar-type character.
</div>

The Lucheng City commander who had come to receive He Jingyuan said, “This is Gongsun **Xiansheng**, the military strategist under Marquis Wu’an. He arrived in Lucheng not long ago, saying that Yanzhou’s defenses were weak and that he wished to borrow troops from Lu City.”
<div class="author-note">
先生 (Xiānshēng) means Master, but it is usually used for a person of scholarly or learned manner.
</div>

He Jingyuan’s expression immediately darkened. “Fifty thousand rebel troops are surrounding Lucheng right now. How could Lucheng possibly spare troops to send to Yanzhou?”

Cold sweat broke out on the Lucheng commander’s forehead. “They say this Gongsun **Xiansheng** is *a genius of the most uncanny sort*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="鬼才 — Literally “demonic/ghostly genius.”"></sup>. **Mojiang**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. This subordinate general"></sup> have no idea what he has up his sleeve either. **Mojiang** already used Lucheng’s siege as an excuse to refuse, but Gongsun **Xiansheng** said he only wanted a thousand men from among the soldiers *you* just conscripted.”

At this, He Jingyuan was even more baffled.

In the short while they had been speaking, Gongsun Yin had already strolled leisurely over to He Jingyuan, clad in that white robe of his that gave him the air of an otherworldly Daoist. “**Mou**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="a humble way of referring to oneself without giving one’s name"></sup> has come in the hope that He **Daren** will lend **Mou** a hand.”

Gongsun Yin served Xie Zheng, but held no official military position in the army. Nevertheless, with his mind full of schemes, no one dared look down on him.

Although He Jingyuan was the Governor of Jizhou and dealt directly with the imperial court in matters of civil administration, when it came to military affairs, he had to follow Xie Zheng’s orders.

Thus, when Gongsun Yin reached him, He Jingyuan dismounted and said cautiously, “Yanzhou is in danger. Jizhou should naturally come to its aid, but given Jizhou’s current circumstances, One imagines Gongsun **Xiansheng** has seen the situation for yourself…”

Gongsun Yin smiled. “**Mou** has come precisely on **Houyé**’s orders to relieve Lucheng of its siege.”

He Jingyuan was even more confused when Gongsun Yin did not mention borrowing troops at all. “What does this mean?”

Gongsun Yin said, “The incident of Wei Xuan beating people to death while requisitioning grain in Taizhou has caused a tremendous uproar. There may well have been rebels fanning the flames behind the scenes. It is only because Jizhou has a salt lake that the rebels chose to besiege Jizhou. But if they fail to take Jizhou after a prolonged siege, it is entirely possible that they will turn around and attack Taizhou instead.

“**Houyé**’s intention is for Yanzhou to deliberately show weakness, giving **Mou** an excuse to come to Jizhou and request reinforcements. If the rebels see that Jizhou is still able to spare troops to send to Yanzhou, they will inevitably begin to question Jizhou’s true military strength and will not dare act rashly in the short term.”

He Jingyuan asked, “Will Prince Changxin really be so easily fooled by this scheme? What if he turns around and attacks Taizhou instead?”

The smile on Gongsun Yin’s face did not falter. “**Houyé** has already sent someone else to Taizhou to request troops.”

He Jingyuan fell silent at that, mulling over the plan.

Both Taizhou and Jizhou Prefectures had been embroiled in the scandal of grain requisitioning, leaving the people disheartened and their support scattered. But the rebels had chosen to attack Jizhou for no other reason than its salt lake.

Marquis Wu’an had sent men from Yanzhou to seek reinforcements from both Taizhou and Jizhou. There was no doubt that this was effectively telling the rebels that Yanzhou was now the easiest target to take, while Jizhou and Taizhou were still capable of lending troops to Yanzhou—clearly suggesting that the military strength of these two prefectures far exceeded Yanzhou’s.

Under ordinary circumstances, Prince Changxin might have worried that this was a trap. But right now, Jinzhou, north of Yanzhou, was engaged in battle with Beijue, so it was entirely plausible that Yanzhou’s defenses were weak.

In the end, He Jingyuan summoned his deputy general. “However many troops does Gongsun **Xiansheng** requires, see that they are assigned to Gongsun **Xiansheng**.”

Gongsun Yin gave him a deep bow. “**Mou** thanks He **Daren**.”

He Jingyuan said, “If the siege of Lucheng can be lifted and Jizhou can be kept secure, then it is He **Mou** who should be thanking **Xiansheng** and **Houyé**.”

Gongsun Yin exchanged a few more polite words with him before departing.

Half an hour later, Gongsun Yin, dressed in white as pure as snow, returned with the thousand newly conscripted soldiers to the encampment where the Yanzhou troops he had brought were stationed.

The moment he stepped into the tent, he could no longer keep the gloating off his face. Looking at the man reclining on the *daybed*, whose face and the corners of the eyes still mottled with bruises, he raised his brows and asked with exaggerated amusement, “Yo? Who in this world could possibly beat you up like this?”
