The room was pitch-black with the lamp extinguished. Fan Changyu lay on the inside of the bed, practically pressed against the wall. She squinted at the person lying beside her.

Yep. Xie Zheng was practically sleeping on the very edge of the bed.

She closed her eyes again, too lazy to care whether he was comfortable. She had already made it clear several times that she harbored no improper intentions toward him, and she had left him more than enough room. Yet after getting into bed, he had said nothing and still chosen to sleep clinging to the edge.

With how he was avoiding her, wasn't he practically acting as though he was afraid she'd covet his good looks?

Fan Changyu turned onto her side, facing the wall, and thought, with that foul temper of his, even if he grew into a *male immortal*, she still wouldn't want him!
<div class="author-note">
仙男 (Xiān nán) - the masculine counterpart to the idea of an ethereally beautiful immortal, an otherworldly handsome man.
</div>

The “male immortal” Xie Zheng was lying with his eyes closed, merely pretending to sleep. When the person on the inner part of the bed<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="in Chinese historical bed, it usually is attached to the wall, so with two people lying on it, one would be on the inner part of the bed, and the other the outer part.)"></sup> suddenly turned over, the corner of the quilt he had been clinging to was instantly pulled away with her.

The chill of the night seeped through his thin clothing and burrowed straight beneath his skin. Xie Zheng lifted his eyelids and glanced inward.

Fan Changyu's figure formed a small mound beneath the thick quilt, most of which was spread across the middle of the bed.

If he wanted to cover himself with the quilt, he'd have to shift inward a little—but that would inevitably disturb Fan Changyu.

Her breathing was very light. Clearly, she wasn't asleep yet.

Xie Zheng withdrew his gaze and closed his eyes again.

There had been a year when he led troops beyond the frontier and encountered an avalanche. He had survived three days buried beneath the snow. A little chill like this was hardly worth his notice.

There was at least three chi<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="roughly one meter"></sup> of distance between them, but perhaps because the thing beneath them was a bed, he still couldn't shake the unease in his heart.

Even biological siblings of opposite sexes were not permitted to share sleeping quarters once they had come of age, let alone a man and woman with no blood relation whatsoever.

In this world, the only people who could share a bed and pillow so intimately were husband and wife.

And the woman sleeping soundly beside his bed at this very moment was none other than her.

Xie Zheng was so thoroughly tormented by these jumbled thoughts that he couldn't feel the slightest hint of sleep. When he heard Fan Changyu's breathing grow deep and steady, an inexplicable irritation welled up inside him. He simply sat halfway up, leaned against the headboard, and began contemplating the situation at hand.

After sleeping for some time, Fan Changyu had shifted into a flat-on-her-back position.

Hearing the movement, Xie Zheng cast a casual glance over.

She truly had a remarkably deceptive face. When asleep, that face of hers looked gentle and harmless in every way.

Yet even when she was up to no good, she wore that same honest, guileless expression.

Sui Yuanqing… He must have been fooled by that very appearance, too, right?

At the thought of that man, Xie Zheng's gaze grew several shades darker.

He couldn't quite put a name to the feeling. He had thought this wildflower growing in the wilderness, the one that had caught his eyes, was his alone, yet someone else was coveting it, too.

It was as though someone had singed his heart with a candle flame. It didn't hurt, but it left him burning with discomfort.

He stared unblinkingly at Fan Changyu sleeping in the darkness, his eyes concealing their depths in the night until they became all the harder to read.

Perhaps sensing his gaze in her dreams, Fan Changyu muttered unhappily, “Not interested…”

  <p>我还不稀罕呢！— “As if I wanted it!”</p>
  <p>or</p>
  <p>我不稀罕你的同情。— “I don't need your pity.”</p>
  <p>But in FCY’s sleeping mumble, it probably mean “As if I wanted you…” 😆</p>

Xie Zheng couldn't make out what she had said. He furrowed his brows and asked, “What?”

Fan Changyu mumbled another indistinct reply, not a single syllable clear enough to understand. Xie Zheng had no choice but to lean closer and listen carefully.

The chill emanating from his body made Fan Changyu instinctively edge away even in her sleep. As she turned over, her lips brushed lightly past his ear, and Xie Zheng's entire body stiffened.

Someone was too close; an unfamiliar presence enveloped her. After everything she had been through, Fan Changyu remained somewhat alert. Her eyelashes fluttered as she began to wake, but before her eyelids could open, Xie Zheng lightly pressed a pressure point beside her neck with his cool fingers. She sank back into a deep sleep.

Xie Zheng rose. Without even lighting a candle, he made his way to the table by the faint light of the snow reflecting in through the window and poured himself two cups of cold tea.

After finishing the tea, he did not return to bed. Instead, he sat at the table, brows furrowed, his dark eyes fixed unblinkingly on the raised curve of the quilt on the bed, as though deep in thought.

The faint cry of an eagle seemed to drift through the night sky.

He lifted his eyelids and slipped out of the room almost without a sound. He climbed over the Wang family's courtyard wall and only after reaching a more secluded alley did he raise his knuckles to his lips and blow a sharp whistle.

When a gyrfalcon carrying a message cannot find its recipient, it circles overhead, crying out as it searches. Only upon hearing the whistle will it follow the sound and dive down.

Before long, the pure-white Gyrfalcon swept out of the night. Xie Zheng extended his right arm, and the bird's iron-hook-like talons gripped his forearm securely. After flapping its wings to steady itself, it folded them neatly against its body.

Xie Zheng retrieved the letter from the Gyrfalcon's leg. After reading it by the light of the moon, the paper crumbled into fragments between his fingers.

The lights in the Jizhou Prefectural Office also remained burning late into the night.

When Zheng Wenchang emerged from the prison and presented He Jingyuan with the confessions obtained during interrogation, he lowered his head and said, “Just as **Daren** suspected, it was Prince Changxin's men who ambushed and killed our people before disguising themselves as grain requisition officers in Qingping County. Those dozens of lives lost in Majia Village were also the work of the rebels. **Xia Guan**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. this lower official"></sup> suspects that the incident in Taizhou Prefecture where people were beaten to death during grain requisition may also have been connected to the Chongzhou rebels.”

He Jingyuan stood with his hands behind his back, gazing at the row of warm yellow lanterns beneath the eaves and the heavy snow drifting through the night.

Instead of answering, he asked, “Wenchang, tell me. That 200,000 shi of grain—after passing through the hands of the merchant surnamed Zhao, where do you think it will be sent?”

Zheng Wenchang did not understand why his superior—who was also his teacher—had suddenly brought up the grain again, but answered truthfully, “At first, **Xia Guan** suspected the merchant was simply pursuing profit. But during the grain requisitions in Taizhou and Jizhou, that merchant never sold the 200,000 shi at an inflated price. Given the circumstances now, it does seem possible that the rebels were manipulating matters behind the scenes. **Xia Guan** believes that if we confiscate the Zhao merchant's assets, we will surely uncover several rebel strongholds.”

He Jingyuan shook his head. “You've underestimated your opponent. Just wait until tomorrow and see how much of the Zhao family's property can still be found throughout Jizhou.”

Zheng Wenchang lowered his head in shame. “If only **Xia Guan** had realized sooner and confiscated the Zhao merchant's household, the Qingping County incident would never have grown into such a major disaster.”

He Jingyuan said, “You are not to blame. The rebels were able to exploit this opening partly because **Laofu**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="老夫 - lit. 'old father', but it means I when spoken by an old man; “this old man”."></sup> bear responsibility. Had **Laofu** not fallen for their trap and become so intent on forcing the person who purchased the grain to reveal himself, allowing Wei Xuan to forcibly requisition grain, then no matter how many informants the rebels planted throughout Jizhou, they would never have been able to stir up such great turmoil.”

Zheng Wenchang did not understand what he meant. Perplexed, he said, “How can **Daren** take all the blame upon yourself? From what **Xia Guan** can see, the grain purchase was a trap laid by the rebels from the very beginning. Wei Xuan is obsessed with claiming glory, and the fact that he seized **Daren**’s official seal by abusing his authority the Northwestern Military Commissioner was not something **Daren** could control either.”

He Jingyuan let out a long sigh and said nothing.

His disciple had many good qualities, but he was simply too upright and rigid as a person—whatever he saw, he believed.

There were many things he ultimately could not explain to him too plainly.

Had that merchant surnamed Zhao not deliberately left a trail for him to follow, allowing him to deduce that the 200,000 shi of grain had been purchased by Marquis Wu’an, how could he have mistakenly thought that Marquis Wu’an had bought the grain merely to trip up Wei Xuan?

In struggles among those in power, it was always the common people at the bottom who suffered.

He had allowed Wei Xuan to requisition grain because he wanted Marquis Wu’an to see clearly what the common people would have to pay for his pursuit of a personal vendetta. He also wanted to find out whether Marquis Wu’an was the sort of person who would stop at nothing to achieve his goal.

It was precisely his decision to relinquish that authority that had given the rebels an opportunity to exploit.

The people had been driven to such desperate straits that Marquis Wu’an had been forced to “reveal himself”, having his old subordinates in Yanzhou Prefecture send an order to mobilize troops, thereby transferring Wei Xuan away and putting a stop to the grain requisitions.

Though He Jingyuan had remained behind the scenes, whatever his intentions, he had ultimately become an unwitting hand in advancing the rebels’ scheme.

Today, when he went to Qingping County and saw the man in green ghost mask who had single-handedly turned the tide of the situation, He Jingyuan suddenly thought of a question. What if he had been wrong from the very beginning?

What if Marquis Wu’an had never intended to use the people of Taizhou and Jizhou Prefectures as bargaining chips to bring down Wei Xuan?

Then why had he requisitioned those 200,000 shi of grain?

His eyes, which had remained tightly shut for a long while, suddenly snapped open. “Jinzhou!”

Zheng Wenchang was completely at a loss. “**Daren**, what about Jinzhou?”

He Jingyuan hurried back to his desk, took out a map of the northwestern territories, and spread it open. Pointing to Jinzhou, his expression was unusually grave. “Prince Changxin has rebelled in Chongzhou, plunging the northwest into internal turmoil, while Marquis Wu’an has fallen in battle. What does this mean for the Beijue people beyond the passes?”

Zheng Wenchang grasped the implications, and his scalp nearly prickled with alarm. “This is the best opportunity they could have to attack Great Yin.”

He Jingyuan paced back and forth before the desk with his hands behind his back. “Jinzhou is Great Yin’s gateway. Behind it lie Huizhou and Yanzhou Prefectures, forming a triangular defensive position that stabilizes Great Yin’s frontier. But all their grain and military provisions must be supplied by the imperial court. With Chongzhou in rebellion, the grain routes have been cut off. Huizhou Prefecture has yet to receive any grain, so where would Jinzhou get its provisions?

“I’ve been a fool! That 200,000 shi of grain that was bought—how could it have been intended to trap Wei Xuan? It was preparation for Jinzhou’s future crisis!”

Hearing this, Zheng Wenchang was equally stunned. Putting it together with what He Jingyuan had said earlier, he finally understood the key point. “*You* mean those 200,000 shi of grain were bought by **Houyé**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. Marquis Lord"></sup>? **Houyé** had already thought of the danger Jinzhou would face in the future when he was defeated on the Chongzhou battlefield?”

He Jingyuan slowly nodded.

Zheng Wenchang said, “**Houyé**’s foresight is beyond anything people like us can match. Now that the rebels’ scheme has been thwarted, Huizhou is holding firm, and Jinzhou has grain. That should be cause for celebration. Why, then, does **Daren** still look so troubled?”

He Jingyuan sighed. “If external threats and internal turmoil were to converge at the same time, how could this situation possibly be resolved?”

Those words left Zheng Wenchang caught in the same dilemma.

There were some things He Jingyuan did not say aloud.

Wei Yan would surely never allow Marquis Wu’an to survive. He had been able to interfere with matters on the Chongzhou battlefield last time. If the Beijue and the Chongzhou rebels were to attack Marquis Wu’an from both sides this time, while the imperial court deliberately restricted his military provisions, He Jingyuan truly feared that the Jinzhou disaster of seventeen years ago would happen all over again.

He Jingyuan stood with his hands behind his back for a long while before finally saying to Zheng Wenchang, “Continue sealing off Qingping County. Do everything you can to root out every one of the rebels’ informants. The waterways used for grain transport are at low water levels in winter, which also makes this a good time to dredge them. Wenchang, once the matter in Qingping County is settled, take some men and clear the waterways from Ji Prefecture to Chongzhou.”

If they could transport supplies by water, they could transport a great deal of supplies.

Zheng Wenchang’s heart gave a start. He accepted the order and withdrew.

Once Zheng Wenchang had left, He Jingyuan was alone in the study. Only then did the door to the side room open, and an elderly man with white hair and deeply wrinkled skin stepped out. “You tell me—if that man surnamed Wei were to find out how thoroughly you’ve been acting against his wishes while pretending otherwise, how many days do you think you’d have left to live?”

He Jingyuan merely said, “When one holds a position, one must attend to its duties; when entrusted with a responsibility, one must fulfill it. He **Mou**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="when added to the speaker's surname, it means “this certain He [surname]”."></sup> has nothing to be ashamed of before the people of this realm. That is enough.”

The old man shook his head with a laugh. “Next time **Lao Touzi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="same meaning with Laofu; this Old Man"></sup> comes looking for you to drink and play chess, I hope you’ll still be alive.”

He Jingyuan said, “One awaits **Taifu**’s visit at any time. May this one ask where **Taifu** plans to go next?”
<div class="author-note">
Taifu (太傅) or Grand Tutor is a high-ranking court title traditionally associated with tutoring and advising members of the imperial family. Usually, they are the teacher of the emperors.
</div>

The old man was dressed in tattered clothes. His head of white hair was sloppily tied up with a wooden hairpin, and a wine gourd hung from his waist. He stretched lazily and said, “That little brat Prince Changxin keeps sending people to my thatched hut every few days to disturb my peace. It’s getting rather annoying. **Lao Touzi** will wander around for a while and see what there is to see.”

He Jingyuan lowered his eyes. “I had thought **Taifu** had come out of retirement after hearing that **Houyé** had fallen on the battlefield.”

The old man gave a scoff. “**Lao Touzi** may not have much ability, but in this entire life, I’ve only ever taken one disciple. The person in this world who could take his life hasn’t even been born yet. Otherwise, he’d have another *junior disciple* by now.”

  <p>师弟 (shīdì) — junior male disciple / younger martial brother</p>

Hearing this, He Jingyuan merely smiled without saying anything.

Grand Tutor Tao had retired from officialdom and lived in seclusion for many years. After Prince Changxin rebelled, he repeatedly sent people to seek him out, claiming that he wanted to invite him to serve as an advisor. In truth, he wanted Grand Tutor Tao to teach his two sons.

That old man’s final remark meant that if he were to take another disciple, he would only accept someone whose natural ability surpassed that of Marquis Wu’an.

Apparently, neither of Prince Changxin’s two sons had caught his eye.

Knowing full well what the answer was, He Jingyuan asked deliberately, “After the battle of Chongzhou, Prince Changxin’s Shizi has earned the reputation of being the ‘Little Marquis Wu’an’. Even he failed to catch **Taifu**’s eye?”

Grand Tutor Tao’s expression darkened. “That volume of chess manuals I taught that brat (Xie Zheng) when he was ten—it somehow ended up in the hands of Prince Changxin’s youngest son.. Tell me, what do you think Prince Changxin is really plotting?”

He Jingyuan’s expression grew more solemn. *Little Marquis Wu’an*… Is Prince Changxin raising his youngest son to emulate Marquis Wu’an?

In Qingping County.

At the first crowing of the rooster, Fan Changyu woke.

It was only just beginning to grow light. Still half-asleep, she rolled over toward the other side of the bed, only to realize with a start that the mattress was shockingly cold. The chill jolted her fully awake.

Fan Changyu sat up, her hair tousled from sleep. Remembering that she had shared this bed with Yan Zheng the night before, she looked toward the table and, sure enough, saw Yan Zheng asleep there, his head propped on one hand.

Judging by how cold this side of the bed was, he probably hadn’t slept on it at all the entire night.

Fan Changyu couldn’t quite put a finger on what she was feeling. Perhaps it was the irritation of having her good intentions treated as though they meant nothing?

Then she wondered why she was even angry. He was being so proper and respectful; she ought to be pleased and think him a true gentleman.

While she was still wrestling with that thought, the man resting with one hand propping up his forehead woke at the sound of the rooster crowing. Their eyes met, and after a brief flicker of surprise, he said calmly, “You’re awake?”

Fan Changyu nodded and scratched at her hair. “If I’d known, I would’ve just gone straight back to town last night. I’ve caused you to go another whole night without sleep.”

Xie Zheng said, “I got up once during the night. When I saw it was nearly dawn, I simply didn’t go back to sleep.”

Fan Changyu gave a vague response, not wanting to dwell on the matter.

It was only a matter of catching up on some sleep. He could do whatever he wanted. After all, she wasn’t the one who had spent the whole night freezing and sleepless.

After breakfast at Head Constable Wang’s house, Fan Changyu took Yu Bao’er and returned to town with Xie Zheng.

Changning had slept with Auntie Zhao the previous night. When she saw Fan Changyu return, she nearly burst into tears. But when she spotted Yu Bao’er, she was afraid of embarrassing herself and forcibly held the tears back.

With the two children keeping each other company, they were so energetic that they might as well have been climbing onto the roof and tearing off the tiles. The one thing that reassured Fan Changyu was that Yu Bao’er had stopped asking about finding his mother, while Changning seemed to have forgotten about the Gyrfalcon as well.

Qingping County remained under a citywide lockdown as the authorities continued searching for the remaining accomplices of the bandits. But Head Constable Wang sent someone to Fan Changyu’s home, and she was surprised to learn that the magistrate had secretly rewarded her with fifty taels of silver.

That day at the magistrate’s residence, she had claimed to be one of Head Constable Wang’s people. Presumably, after taking credit for her achievement, the magistrate had given her the money to win her over.

Fan Changyu understood the wisdom of quietly making money. Fame or recognition was useless to her; if anything, it would only invite trouble. Real silver and gold, on the other hand, was far more practical.

After seeing the yamen runner out, Fan Changyu went into the room with a beaming smile to hide the silver. When she ran into Xie Zheng, she generously offered, “Want half?”

The fact that this fellow wanted to keep his distance from her was one thing. But he had been the one to come up with the plan that lifted the siege of Qingping County, and he had also saved her on the city wall that day. They still needed to settle the accounts properly.

Xie Zheng had the distinct feeling that Fan Changyu had become much more distant toward him over the past two days since returning.

Whenever she saw him, she still greeted him with the same smile as before, but there was an unmistakable difference from the way she had treated him previously.

He suppressed the inexplicable displeasure stirring in his heart and asked, “Does the authorities know my identity?”

Fan Changyu shook her head. “I didn’t tell anyone who you are. The magistrate wanted to take all the credit for himself and didn’t even mention Head Constable Wang’s name, so I doubt he’d voluntarily bring you up.”

She herself had no desire to reveal her identity either, fearing that those people might hold a grudge against her. When Yan Zheng had appeared on the city wall, he had even been wearing a mask, so Fan Changyu had guessed that he didn’t want his identity exposed either.

After all, if they offended those officials, nothing but endless trouble awaited them.

Xie Zheng said, “That reward money was all given to you. Why would you split it with me?”

Fan Changyu said, “Wasn’t the idea yours?”

Xie Zheng lowered his eyes. “The magistrate gave you this reward money not because you defended the city gates, but because you rescued him from danger and captured the rebels. It has nothing to do with me.”

Fan Changyu couldn’t out-argue him. She carried the silver back into the room. A short while later, she came back out carrying a pile of things. “You said before that you were leaving. It so happened that the county was unfortunately placed under lockdown, so you ended up staying a few extra days. I’ve been getting some things ready for you little by little. Take these two sets of clothes to change into them along the way. These shoes have double-stitched soles, so they’ll last. Oh, right, I also exchanged fifty taels of banknotes for you. They’ll be more convenient to carry…”

She went on and on, like an old mother sending her son off on a long journey. “I’ve written the divorce agreement too. All that’s left is for you to stamp your fingerprint on it.”

A *repudiation letter* only needs to be written by one party. A divorce agreement, however, is different; it is an amicable end to the marriage, so both parties must sign and affix their fingerprints.
<div class="author-note">
  <p>In Chinese historical culture, there were two types of marriage separation. One is 休妻 (Xiūqī), refers to a husband's unilateral dismissal of his wife (or in their case, the wife as the head of the household would dismiss the husband), typically through a xiūshū (休书, repudiation letter). This was often based on the "Seven Grounds for Dismissal" (七出), such as barrenness, jealousy, or disobedience. A woman (or man) who was dismissed carried a significant social stigma, making remarriage difficult, and the act itself reinforced the husband’s (or wife’s) authority while disgracing the wife (or husband). </p>
  <p>The other one is 和离 or Hélí, was a mutually agreed separation, allowing both parties to part ways without the shame associated with being cast aside. It was a more dignified form of divorce, particularly among couples of equal standing or those with irreconcilable differences. Because Hélí did not carry the same stigma as Xiūqī, it provided the woman (or man) with a better chance of securing his/her future, whether through remarriage or independence.</p>
</div>

The stifled frustration that had been lodged in Xie Zheng’s chest these past few days only grew worse when he heard her talking about all this.

He folded his arms and leaned against the doorframe, watching her for a moment. Then he suddenly smiled and said sarcastically, “How kind of you to think of everything so thoroughly for me.”

Fan Changyu didn’t trade barbs with him. She merely said, “Traveling outside is nothing like staying at home. It’s best to prepare as thoroughly as possible. If you run into any difficulties out there, there won’t be anyone around to lend you a hand…”

Some inexplicable emotions churned in Xie Zheng’s chest. The trace of sarcasm on his face could no longer hold. He turned his gaze toward the snow piled along the courtyard wall and suddenly asked, “What about you? What are you planning to do from now on?”

Fan Changyu found the question amusing. “Didn’t you ask me that before? As long as Qingping County remains peaceful, I’m going to get that pig farm started…”

Xie Zheng half-lifted his phoenix eyes. “I mean, are you planning to marry (into a husband’s family), or are you going to continue taking in a live-in husband?”

The question left Fan Changyu momentarily at a loss. She placed the pile of things on the table, then walked to the steps by the doorway and sat down. Looking at the leafless pear tree in the courtyard, she thought for a while before saying, “I’ll definitely marry eventually. As for whether I take in a husband or marry into someone else’s family, I’ll decide when the time comes.”

Xie Zheng rolled a small pebble between his fingers and casually tossed it at the pear tree, startling several sparrows perched on its branches into flight. “What kind do you like? If no one wants to marry you in the future, and no one is willing to marry into your family either, I’ll find someone for you.”

Hearing him mock her, Fan Changyu couldn’t help getting annoyed. “Well, it certainly wouldn't be someone with a foul temper like yours! With a mouth as nasty as yours, you’d better worry about whether you’ll ever find a wife!”

Xie Zheng sat down with one leg bent, a smile playing at the corner of his mouth, neither quite mocking nor quite sincere. “I wouldn’t marry someone like you either. I’d have to marry someone gentle and virtuous who knows how to manage a household.”

The last pebble in his hand flew especially far, sailing over the courtyard wall before disappearing from sight.

Fan Changyu glanced at his finely chiseled profile. Lowering her eyes, she tugged faintly at the corner of her mouth and said frankly, “I like someone refined and good-looking. Ideally, he’d have read plenty of books, be learned, humble, even-tempered, and fond of smiling. When my mother was still alive, she used to say that I was too boisterous by nature, so I needed someone more refined to keep me in line. That was the only way we’d be able to live together for the long haul.”

A faint, inexplicable ache stirred in Fan Changyu’s chest. She supposed it was because she had been reminded of her mother.

She said, “We’ve shared hardship and difficulty together, after all. You’re leaving soon, so don’t curse me by saying no one will ever want me. I’ll wish that you marry a gentle and virtuous wife, and you wish that I find a refined and good-looking husband!”

Xie Zheng said, “All right.”

His smile was genuinely beautiful.

When he stood, he even kindly held out a hand to Fan Changyu. Her legs had gone a little numb from sitting so long, and when she saw his hand extended toward her, she took it without a second thought.

The change came in that very instant.

Fan Changyu was yanked forward by a tremendous force and crashed into his arms. The hand gripping her uninjured wrist was so powerful that it nearly seemed intent on wrenching that wrist apart as well.

He seized her chin and lowered his head, covering her lips with a kiss that was almost brutal in its force.
