After Gongsun Yin departed, Xie Zheng acted as though he hadn't seen the large bundle at all. He picked up a military treatise from the table and began flipping through it.

When his personal guard entered to bring tea, he said coldly, “Take that to the rear tent.”

The guard froze for a moment before realizing he meant the large bundle Gongsun Yin had brought. He picked it up and carried it to the rear tent.

Though the bundle was no longer before his eyes, Xie Zheng's brows remained drawn together. His fingertips rapidly flipped the pages, yet he still couldn't suppress the restlessness churning inside him.

A moment later, he tossed aside the military treatise.

Hearing the noise, the personal guard stationed outside was just about to enter and ask whether he had any orders. But after lifting the tent flap a crack, he saw Xie Zheng already getting up and heading toward the rear tent himself.

The guard quickly withdrew his hand, returned to his original position, and continued standing watch, eyes fixed straight ahead.

Only Gongsun Yin and a handful of trusted confidants in the Yanzhou army knew Xie Zheng's true identity. The military tent he occupied was an ordinary commander's quarters, divided into a front tent and a rear tent. The front was used for discussing affairs, while the rear served as living quarters and a place to rest.

The bundle the guard had carried to the rear tent earlier now sat on a small table beside the military bed.

Xie Zheng lowered his eyes and studied it for a while before undoing the knot.

Inside were two new outfits and a pair of shoes, all of which Fan Changyu had packed for him that day.

When he spotted the two extra packets of dried tangerine-peel candies, the tight line of his lips relaxed slightly. The stifling pressure in his chest, which had been troubling him for so long, seemed to sink suddenly into warm water, and all that inexplicable agitation ebbed away.

Xie Zheng's fingertips brushed over the two new outfits. He picked them up, intending to put them away in his trunk, but the movement caused the banknotes and the divorce agreement folded among the clothes to tumble out as well.

The moment he saw those square, glaring characters spelling out “Divorce Agreement", the faint curve of his lips turned cold.

So she truly was... determined to leave them owing each other nothing!

Xie Zheng's thin lips curled coldly. His innate pride made him want to summon someone at once and have them throw this bundle as far away as possible.

He closed his eyes and took several breaths to compose himself. In the end, however, he merely gathered everything from the bundle and locked it all inside the trunk beside him.

He sat down nearby, lowering his eyes to the trunk at his feet, his face utterly expressionless.

If he threw it away now, he would only find himself thinking about it later.

Let it stay for now. When the day comes that looking at these things no longer stirs even the slightest ripple in his heart, that would be the time to throw them away.

Wei Yan had personally trained him. Of all the things he had learned from Wei Yan, the most useful was undoubtedly how to face his own desires—and, at the same time, learn to control them.

It was true that he had fallen for her, but that was all.

Meanwhile, after leaving the Yanzhou encampment, Gongsun Yin simply couldn't stand the feeling of *a cat paw scratching at his heart*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="A vivid metaphor for unbearable curiosity or restlessness.
"></sup>. He wandered over to the new recruits' camp in Jizhou once more.

Xie Zheng was tight-lipped. Even after *wearing his tongue raw*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="an idiom meaning to talk endlessly or exhaust every possible argument in an attempt to persuade someone to speak or agree."></sup>, Gongsun Yin had managed to pry out very little about his marriage. But he figured that since the girl had gone through her own uncle to send things to Xie Zheng, she hardly seemed devoid of feeling for him. So why had Xie Zheng claimed that she didn't want to be with him?

Carrying this bellyful of questions, Gongsun Yin went to ask the young officer overseeing the craftsmen in Jizhou. He had little trouble finding out about Carpenter Zhao.

There was only one carpenter who knew medicine and had treated a colonel's rheumatism with a few applications of medicinal plaster. He was easy enough to track down.

The newly conscripted soldiers still needed to undergo training and had not yet been sent into actual combat. The craftsmen recruited from among the common people, meanwhile, had been assigned to manufacture defensive equipment for the city.

Since there was no need for him to tend to warhorses, Carpenter Zhao had been assigned to the carpentry camp.

When the military overseer in charge of the craftsmen led Gongsun Yin to meet him, Carpenter Zhao was busy planing a piece of wood.

The overseer called out, “Is Carpenter Zhao here? Someone's looking for you!”

Carpenter Zhao set down his plane and raised his old eyes toward the entrance, “**Xiao Lao’er**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. little/humble old one"></sup> is here.”

The overseer beckoned him over. Carpenter Zhao briefly asked the foreman supervising the work for leave, then headed outside.

They were craftsmen, and the military camp did not issue them standardized uniforms. Carpenter Zhao was still wearing his own dusty gray clothes. His back was hunched, and his frame looked wiry and thin.

The overseer remained quite courteous toward him. “This **Daren** is looking for you.”

Carpenter Zhao hadn't been in the military camp long, but he had already mastered a set of survival rules. Anyone wearing armor was addressed as **Jiangjun**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="general"></sup>, ordinary soldiers received a respectful **Junye**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. soldier lord"></sup>, and those without armor but possessing an impressive bearing were called **Daren**, regardless of their actual identity.

The moment Carpenter Zhao saw Gongsun Yin, he hurriedly bowed, “**Xiao Lao’er** pays respects to **Daren**.”

Gongsun Yin made a courteous gesture helping him up, his smile warm as spring sunshine, “There's no need for such formalities, **Lao Renjia**. I heard **Lao Renjia** have a nephew-in-law  named Yan Zheng?”

老人家 （Lǎorénjiā) is a respectful form of address for an older person, roughly equivalent to “Elder” or “Old Sir”. Unlike 老头子 (laotouzi), which can sound blunt or familiar (spoken by Grand Tutor Tao), 老人家 carries warmth and courtesy, acknowledging the person's age and experience.

During his time in the army, Carpenter Zhao had been constantly trying to find out news of Yan Zheng. But with tens of thousands of newly conscripted soldiers, how could he hope to track someone down in a matter of days?

As luck would have it, he had unexpectedly treated a colonel. The officer was a straightforward man who told him to come to him if he ever encountered any difficulties. Afraid that saying he wanted to find a neighbor's family would not be taken seriously, Carpenter Zhao had lied and claimed he was looking for his niece's husband, asking the colonel to help make inquiries.

The Colonel was a man who kept his word. He really did take the matter to heart, and after discovering that Yan Zheng was among the thousand men loaned to Yanzhou, he immediately informed Carpenter Zhao. Like most of the craftsmen, Carpenter Zhao had been placed under guard to prevent them from fleeing. They were not permitted to move freely around the encampment, so he had asked the Colonel to deliver the bundle Fan Changyu had prepared to Xie Zheng.

After handing over the bundle, the Colonel had brought Carpenter Zhao a reply. Only then did Carpenter Zhao finally feel the weight lift from his heart, believing he had not failed Fan Changyu.

Now, with a richly dressed young gentleman suddenly coming to seek him out, Carpenter Zhao couldn't quite figure out what this was about. Could it be that he was about to be punished for lying about Yan Zheng being his niece's husband?

His lips moved a few times. In the end, he nodded, his heart twisting with unease.

The moment Gongsun Yin saw that he had found the right man, his fox-like eyes crinkled into a smile. He even brazenly asked the military overseer for a tent, inviting Carpenter Zhao inside for a little chat.

Carpenter Zhao had never encountered such a situation before. Once inside the tent, he felt as though sitting on a bed on needles.

Gongsun Yin's smile was both refined and amiable. He even took the initiative to pour him some tea, “I heard **Lao Renjia** cured Colonel Hu's rheumatism. With such medical skills, why not serve as a military physician instead of coming to work in the craftsmen's camp?”

Carpenter Zhao replied somewhat awkwardly, “**Xiao Lao’er**'s medical skills are meager. One used to treat livestock. How could One presume to serve as a military physician?”

Learning that the man was a veterinary doctor, Gongsun Yin smiled, “Then Colonel Hu was the first person **Lao Renjia** has treated?”

Carpenter Zhao answered honestly, “Not exactly. **Xiao Lao’er** worked as a veterinary doctor for over a decade before changing trades and becoming a carpenter. The first person this One ever saved was my niece's husband. He had suffered severe injuries at the time, and none of the medical halls in town dared take him in. That was why **Xiao Lao’er** risked using medicine to save him.”

Gongsun Yin was initially taken aback. Then he suddenly let out a snort of laughter. When Carpenter Zhao looked at him in confusion, he coughed several times before finally managing to suppress his amusement. “To have met **Lao Renjia** when he was at the end of his road... That was good fortune indeed.”

Carpenter Zhao hurriedly shook his head. “No, no. My niece carried him back from the wilderness. If she hadn't found him and brought him home, he might not have died from those wounds, but he surely would have frozen to death in that bitter snow and ice.”

Gongsun Yin thought to himself, A beauty rescuing a hero. Now that's a tale worth telling. Suppressing his curiosity, he asked, “And later, he married your niece?”

Seeing how relentlessly the young man was probing, Carpenter Zhao couldn't help giving him another look. Why is this official asking so many questions about Yan Zheng's marriage?

Gongsun Yin also realized that he was making his intentions a little too obvious. He had no choice but to casually come up with an excuse, “Your niece's husband has greatly impressed our general. When the general takes someone under his command and intends to make good use of him, he naturally needs to know the man's background thoroughly. That's why he sent me to pay a visit.”

Though Carpenter Zhao had never studied much, he had lived several decades and seen all manner of things. The war hadn't even begun, yet Yan Zheng had already caught the eye of a general.

Oh no. Could it be that Yan Zheng is so handsome that some general has taken a fancy to him and wants him for a son-in-law?

What would that mean for Changyu? Surely she wouldn't have to suffer through another ordeal like the one with Song Yan?

Carpenter Zhao thoughts raced in every direction as he replied, “To answer the question **Daren** just asked, that child later married into my niece's family as a live-in husband.”

Gongsun Yin was in the middle of drinking his tea. At those words, he promptly spat it out. A man usually so silver-tongued suddenly found his tongue tied. “Live… live-in husband?”

Even the young Emperor's wouldn't dare make such a declaration—have Xie Zheng marry into the household of some commoner girl? What kind of joke was this?

Seeing him lose his composure so completely, Carpenter Zhao became even more convinced that his suspicions were correct.

He hurriedly explained, “That child was carried back from the snow by my niece, who saved his life. He was covered in injuries and illness, unable even to get out of bed,  yet my niece didn't turn him away. She kept him there to recover, earning money by slaughtering pigs to buy his medicine and pay for his treatment... After spending so much time together, wouldn't feelings naturally develop?”

Gongsun Yin had just wiped the tea from the corners of his mouth when he heard this. His expression turned exceedingly strange. “*Your* niece... is a pig butcher?”

He had already been wondering how an ordinary woman could have carried Xie Zheng back so easily.

Afraid that Gongsun Yin might look down on Fan Changyu, Carpenter Zhao said, “That lass has had a hard life. Her family made their living slaughtering pigs, and even ran a butcher shop in town. They had been getting by fairly well, but who could have imagined that her parents would die at the hands of bandits? In the end, only she and her five-year-old younger sister were left. With a family to support, she had no choice but to make a living slaughtering pigs.”

As he spoke, he secretly glanced at Gongsun Yin's face. Seeing the young man's expression turn rather difficult to describe, Carpenter Zhao felt a small, private thrill of satisfaction.

That was precisely why he had told him all this: to make it clear to this official that Fan Changyu had shown Yan Zheng a kindness as weighty as a mountain. If they intended to force Xie Zheng to marry some general's daughter, that would be utterly immoral.

Even assuming the absolute worse—if Yan Zheng actually agreed to marry a general's daughter—then his character would be nothing short of despicable. After all, what sort of man could abandon the wife who had saved his life? Any official hoping to marry off a daughter would do well to weigh that matter carefully.

Little did he know that after hearing Carpenter Zhao's account, Gongsun Yin had conjured up an image of a broad-shouldered, thick-waisted woman wielding a pig-slaughtering knife, her face covered in slabs of flesh.

He drew a sharp breath through his teeth. Then he remembered Xie Zheng's words: “She's the one who doesn't want to be with me.”

Gongsun Yin hurriedly rubbed his arms.

No wonder that fellow never shows any interest in women. So this is the type he preferred?

Gongsun Yin asked with complicated feelings, clinging to one last sliver of hope, “So Yan Zheng married into your niece's family to repay a debt of gratitude?”

Carpenter Zhao immediately bristled, puffing out his whiskers and glaring, “How could it be merely to repay a debt? This young husband and wife are deeply in love! When the local ruffians came to my niece's home to cause trouble, it was my nephew-in-law who beat them away. He can read and write, and when he saw my niece rising early and returning late to slaughter pigs and earn money for his medical treatment, he wasn't even fully recovered before he begged me to go to the town's bookshop and find him work writing model essays. During the New Year, he even wrote New Year couplets for all the neighbors in the lane! When my niece was too busy at the butcher shop to manage everything, he had recovered a little and went to help her sell pork...”

Carpenter Zhao continued enthusiastically recounting the loving everyday life of the young couple. Meanwhile, Gongsun Yin imagined Xie Zheng selling pork and couldn't help shuddering again, gooseflesh rising along his arms.

What on earth had that fellow been through during these days of hardship?

Marrying into the family of a woman who slaughtered pigs?

Ss!<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="嘶- an onomatopoeia; the sound of air sucked between the teeth, indicating hesitation or thinking over"></sup> Utterly horrifying.

Knowing Xie Zheng as he did, Gongsun Yin was certain that if Xie Zheng refused to do something, even the Heavenly King himself descending to earth to force him wouldn't make it happen.

Which meant Xie Zheng had probably married into that family of his own accord.

And it was precisely because he understood this that Gongsun Yin found the whole thing even more outrageous.

Could it be that the fellow really did have a thing for burly, strapping women?

Gongsun Yin felt that if the noble ladies of the capital ever learned of this, they would probably weep themselves heartbroken...

Seeing the official's expression shift through a bewildering array of emotions, Carpenter Zhao feared they might still have designs on Xie Zheng. He added hurriedly, “By the time this war is over and my nephew-in-law returns home, they might even have a little one old enough to run around.”

The expression on Gongsun Yin's face could only be described as horrified. “Your... your niece is with child?”

Carpenter Zhao stammered, “That, we can't say for certain. There have been men from our village who were conscripted, only for their wives to discover not long afterward that they were pregnant.”

What he was thinking, however, was that when the daughters of wealthy, prestigious families were married off, their parents might tolerate the future son-in-law having concubines or *bedchamber maids*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="通房 - A tongfang, a maid or attendant who also served as a man’s sexual partner."></sup>, but they certainly would not tolerate him already having concubine-born children before the wedding.

Gongsun Yin, who was always refined and composed in front of others, had truly lost all composure this time. A storm was raging in his heart.

The notoriously high-and-mighty Xie Zheng had actually fallen into the hands of a butcher’s daughter?

Unable to restrain himself, Gongsun Yin gave his own thigh a vicious pinch. The pain twisted his mouth askew. Once he confirmed that this was not a dream, the expression on his face became even more devastated. After exchanging a few strained pleasantries with Carpenter Zhao, he left with an expression that seemed to question the very meaning of life.

Watching his stunned and bewildered retreating figure, Carpenter Zhao drank a cup of tea in exceptionally good spirits.

At long last, he had helped shield that young couple from a *rotten peach blossom*.

When Gongsun Yin left the military tent, he happened to run into the same Colonel from the Jizhou army, who had come again to ask Carpenter Zhao for medicinal salve.

The man recognized Gongsun Yin and respectfully clasped his fists. “Gongsun **Xiansheng**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="Master (used for a well-respected scholar)"></sup>.”

Gongsun Yin was still in a daze. After returning his greeting with a nod, he asked, “Is that carpenter, the one who used to work as a veterinary doctor, the one who cured your rheumatism?”

Colonel Hu was a straightforward man. He did not care in the slightest that Carpenter Zhao had once been a veterinarian. Now that the rheumatism in his waist no longer hurt, he had been feeling quite comfortable these past two days. He immediately grinned and nodded, “That’s him. Did Gongsun **Xiansheng** need something from him?”

It seemed he had not found the wrong man.

The niece’s husband that carpenter had mentioned was undoubtedly Xie Zheng.

Gongsun Yin said, “Just asking.”

After returning to the Yanzhou camp with an expression that questioned everything he knew about life, he summoned one of his personal guards and gave him a series of whispered instructions. Then, with a complicated look on his face, he said, “Don’t disturb that woman. Just keep an eye on her movements.”

The personal guard accepted the order and withdrew.

Gongsun Yin stared at Xie Zheng’s military tent for a while. Recalling the despondent, bereft expression Xie Zheng had worn in the tent earlier, he shuddered violently and muttered, “Could that fellow have simply gone too long without seeing a woman?”

Xie Zheng, who had returned from a horseback patrol around the camp in a foul mood, Xie Zheng happened to hear the latter half of his sentence upon returning.

Leading a tall black steed whose nostrils were still puffing clouds of white breath, he stood not far away and said coldly, “Gone too long without seeing a woman? Shall I have someone throw you into the Yihong Brothel tonight?”

Back in the day, Gongsun Yin would never have dared answer back. But after his meeting with Carpenter Zhao today, he had suffered one shock after another. Now, meeting Xie Zheng’s cold, somber gaze, he actually considered it for a moment before looking at him and said, “Jiuheng, neither of us has ever been to a *green tower*. How about we go take a look?”
<div class="author-note">
青楼: Literally “green tower,” but historically a euphemistic term for a high-class pleasure house or brothel.
</div>

More than anything, he wanted to confirm whether something was wrong with this fellow’s eyes.

Xie Zheng’s hand paused as he rolled up the riding crop. When he looked up again, all traces of his earlier languidness had vanished from his eyes. “If you were a military officer under my command, a hundred strokes of the military rod would be a lenient punishment.”

Gongsun Yin knew he had misspoken. But if he accepted the punishment now and simply went along with Xie Zheng’s words, there would be no friendship left between them. He shrugged with a grin and said, “Too bad I’m not.”

Xie Zheng handed his warhorse to a personal guard and walked past him toward the military tent, leaving only a final warning behind: “Do not violate my military’s rules.”

Watching him walk away, Gongsun Yin gave a soft tsk. “Now that you’ve finally *wised up*, I’m even more curious what kind of extraordinary woman that butcher’s daughter is.”

In Lin’an Town.

Under the cover of night, another thick layer of snow had settled over the eaves, roof tiles, and treetops. The entire town lay in silence. Not even a single bark could be heard.

“Bandits are here!”

“They’re killing people! Run!”

People fleeing from the county seat toward the surrounding towns screamed in panic, their cries tearing through the stillness of the snowy night. The townsfolk were jolted awake from their sleep, hurriedly throwing on whatever clothes they could find and scooping up their children as they prepared to flee.

But the moment they opened their doors, a gleaming blade was thrust straight into their chests.

The man, dead with his eyes still open, was kicked aside by the bandit outside. Inside, a woman grabbed her child and scrambled toward a corner, only for the child to be violently thrown aside by a bandit who forced his way in. With a feral grin, he seized the woman by the hair and dragged her toward the bed…

Before long, all of Lin’an Town was engulfed in blazing firelight. The cries of children and the bandits’ savage battle cries were shrill and horrifying.

Amid the flames, a man sat astride a tall warhorse, watching the bandits slaughter and plunder with cold indifference. Lowering his gaze to the Qingping County Magistrate dangling limply from his grip like a dead dog, he asked lazily, “That woman, where does she live?”

After County Magistrate Liu learned that the bandits had taken advantage of the conscription seizing all the able-bodied men from the county and had begun attacking Qingping County, he had fled with his entire family without a second thought. He had assumed that these men would be satisfied with slaughtering the people in the county seat.

He had never imagined that after his carriage had traveled more than ten li<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="approx. 5 km"></sup>, this man would still catch up to him on horseback.

Now covered in blood and having been jostled atop a horse for the entire journey, Magistrate Liu was already scared witless. He could only beg over and over, “**Xiaoren**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. this small person"></sup> doesn’t know. **Xiaoren** truly doesn’t know…”
