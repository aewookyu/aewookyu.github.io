The next day, Fan Changyu rose early to slaughter pigs as usual.

During these first few days after the New Year, most people in town were busy visiting relatives. With meat gracing nearly every meal, their bellies already well supplied with rich food, there was little demand for fresh pork. The braised meat business, however, was thriving. Every household was willing to buy ready-made braised meat to serve guests at home, and setting it out as a substantial dish at the dinner table was a fine way to show hospitality.

Fan Changyu’s shop had usually sold the fresh meat of two pigs a day, but these past two days she had been slaughtering only one.

As for the braised meat supplied to Yixiang Restaurant, she purchased that meat from elsewhere. On the street where pork was sold, she had practically cornered the market on pig heads and trotters.

She was no longer merely a competitor to those butchers. She had become one of their biggest customers. Eager to secure such a long-term business arrangement with her, every butcher on the street greeted her with a broad smile whenever they saw her, their greetings considerably warmer than before.

Whenever Fan Changyu ran into some difficulty at her shop, all she had to do was mention it, and a whole crowd would eagerly come rushing to lend a hand.

Fan Changyu suddenly began to understand why, after Song Yan passed the provincial examination, certain people in town had gone so far out of their way to curry favor with the Song family by trampling her underfoot.

It really was as Yan Zheng had said: when she had nothing, no matter how good-natured she was, others could always find fault with her.

But now that she had merely brushed against a little wealth and influence, the goodwill she received was already several times what it had been before.

Everything was moving in a better direction. It was just that Fan Changyu now had to deliver meat to both Yixiang Restaurant and the Fat Shopkeeper, while also keeping an eye on her own pork shop. She found it difficult to be in two places at once.

Finding a helper on such short notice was easier said than done.

At breakfast, Fan Changyu kept glancing at Xie Zheng, looking as though she wanted to say something but couldn’t quite bring herself to.

Xie Zheng had slept poorly the night before, and the dark circles beneath his eyes were even more pronounced. After noticing Fan Changyu’s repeated glances, he set down his bowl of porridge and asked, “What is it?”

Only then did Fan Changyu get a proper look at the shadows beneath his eyes, which had grown noticeably darker since before. She stared in disbelief. “Did you not sleep at all last night?”

Xie Zheng lowered his eyes. “I did. There were mice in the room last night. Spent some time looking for them.”

There had indeed been a mouse. He had killed it by hurling a bamboo skewer straight through it, then tossed it to the Gyrfalcon.

The moment Fan Changyu heard about the mice, she remembered the cured meat still hanging above the fire pit at home and immediately grew concerned. She hurried to check. Only after finding no signs that the mice had nibbled at anything did she relax.

“We never used to keep this much braised meat and cured meat at home,” she said. “We sold fresh pork directly, and there weren’t many mice around either. I was careless. I’ll have to find a cat to raise here.”

Changning had already finished eating and gone to check on the Gyrfalcon in the chicken coop. Suddenly, she burst into tears. “Sunsun is gone again!”

Fan Changyu was puzzled too. “It flew away again?”

The two sisters turned to look at Xie Zheng in unison.

Someone had sent the Gyrfalcon out to deliver a letter in the middle of the night. The someone in question fell silent for a moment before saying, “That creature is wild by nature and difficult to tame. Perhaps I still haven’t trained it properly.”

*Golden beans*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="金豆子 (Jīn dòuzǐ) A playful metaphor for tears"></sup> immediately began rolling down Changning’s cheeks, one after another.

Fan Changyu sighed helplessly. “There, there, don’t cry. When spring comes, I’ll raise a whole brood of chicks for you, all right?”

Changning continued crying, “I don’t want chicks! I want Sunsun!”

She wiped her eyes with her sleeve, “Sunsun will come back!”

Then she looked up at Xie Zheng with hopeful eyes.

This time, Xie Zheng did not give her a definite answer. He only said, “Perhaps it will come back.”

Changning’s lips immediately crumpled, and she began crying even harder.

Fan Changyu coaxed her, “How about we go catch another one in the wild?”

Changning shook her head. “I don’t want another one. I only want Sunsun.”

Fan Changyu knew how much a child’s stubborn streak could test one’s patience. She said, “The gyrfalcon has already flown away. It’s a bird that belongs in the wild, and even **Ah Jie** can’t find it. All we can do is catch another one for you out in the wild if that’s what you want. But you can’t just keep crying when you don’t want that either. Ningniang, tell **Ah Jie**, what is **Ah Jie** supposed to do?”

Changning sniffled pitifully, then raised her chubby arms and hugged Fan Changyu. “Sorry, **Ah Jie**. Ningniang isn’t being naughty. Ningniang just can’t bear to part with Sunsun.”

Fan Changyu patted the child’s back.

Changning buried her face against Fan Changyu’s shoulder and mumbled, “When spring comes, raise chicks.”

Fan Changyu agreed.

Changning straightened up, her eyes still rimmed with red. “When the chicks grow up, Sunsun can see them while flying and come down to eat.”

Fan Changyu, who had thought she’d finally managed to comfort the child: “...All right.”

At any rate, the child had finally stopped crying.

Fan Changyu returned to the table and sat down again, finishing the half-bowl of porridge she had left with mixed feelings. Remembering how short-staffed her pork shop was, she scratched her head and asked Xie Zheng, “Are you going to catch up on sleep later?”

Even back when Fan Changyu had been hesitating to speak earlier, Xie Zheng had already realized she likely had something she wanted his help with. “If there’s something you need, just say it.”

Fan Changyu then swallowed her embarrassment and broached the subject. “My pork shop opened for business today, but I still have to deliver braised meat to Yu **Zhanggui**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="manager"></sup>’s restaurant. If you’re free, could you help me look after the shop for half a day? I’ll be back as soon as I finish the delivery.” 

He had only said last night that he intended to leave soon, so asking him for help now seemed somewhat inappropriate. But Fan Changyu truly couldn’t manage everything on her own. For now, she could only squeeze a little more labor out of him.

Xie Zheng nodded, and Fan Changyu immediately let out a breath of relief.

If he had refused, even Fan Changyu’s thick skin would probably have been unable to spare her some embarrassment.

She had made some progress when it came to navigating social relationships. She no longer immediately brought up payment for his labor. After all, his willingness to help was a matter of personal goodwill. If she responded by offering him wages, it would undoubtedly trample on that goodwill.

If she truly wanted to thank him, she was better off preparing more supplies for him before he left. Gratitude quietly expressed through actions afterward was what truly repaid a favor—not making promises of various benefits from the outset, as though they were negotiating a business deal.

Both she and Xie Zheng would be heading out, and Fan Changyu wasn’t comfortable leaving Changning home alone. Just as before, she took Changning to stay with Auntie Zhao next door.

Only then did she go outside the alley and flag down an ox cart, sending the fresh meat to the Fan’s pork shop first.

The meat alone was already heavy enough. Fan Changyu and Xie Zheng therefore didn’t ride in the cart, but simply walked alongside it all the way to the shop.

Though Xie Zheng had been in this town for quite some time, this was his first time seeing its morning market. It could not compare to the prosperity of the capital, but it was livelier than he had expected.

In front of the shops selling breakfast, steam billowed from every stove and boiler. Vendors’ cries and hawkers’ calls mingled with the bustle of traffic. People came and went, their steps hurried.

It was the warmth and vitality of everyday life—and the very pulse of this little town.

When they reached the shop, Fan Changyu had barely set down a basin of braised meat before Xie Zheng carried down all the fresh pork.

Fan Changyu glanced over and couldn’t help reflecting that having someone around to lend a hand really did make things considerably easier.

After putting away the basin of braised meat, she began arranging the fresh pork on the chopping board, explaining to Xie Zheng which cut each piece came from and how much it sold for.

The butcher’s wife from the shop diagonally across the street caught sight of Xie Zheng’s handsome face and teased her, “Changyu, you finally decided to bring your husband out and let the rest of us have a look! What a handsome young man! No wonder you’ve been keeping him hidden at home all this time!”

Fan Changyu had grown accustomed to being teased by Yu Qianqian, so her skin was no longer quite so thin when others teased her and Xie Zheng. She replied, “**Shenzi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="auntie"></sup> is joking. He’s been recuperating at home all this time. His injuries have gotten better recently, and I’ve been too busy to manage everything myself, so he came to the shop to lend me a hand.”

The butcher’s wife knew that Xie Zheng was Fan Changyu’s live-in husband, so she had dared to make such a joke. She was a full twelve years older than Fan Changyu and knew that many men who married into their wives’ families were sensitive about their status. A joke like hers could easily end up causing the young couple to quarrel once they got home.

Hearing Fan Changyu give such a straightforward explanation, she immediately changed her tone. “**Shenzi** was only joking. Young man, don’t take it to heart.”

Xie Zheng said, “I won’t.”

The butcher’s wife continued, “Before, Changyu had to handle everything in and out of this shop all by herself. Now that she’s married, she finally has someone to lend her a hand.”

Xie Zheng helped arrange the pork on the counter. He glanced at Fan Changyu beside him as she hoisted a slab of pork rump and scraped it clean against the iron hook, but said nothing.

Though it was the dead of winter, and she was bundled up in thick winter clothes, after only this short while, a fine sheen of sweat had already appeared on her forehead.

Before, whenever she came to the pork shop herself, she had generally handled everything on her own.

“Pork rump is thirty-five wen per jin<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="1 jin =  500 g"></sup>. If someone tries to haggle, don’t go any lower than thirty wen...” Fan Changyu was going over the prices with him. After hanging up the pork, she turned around and saw Xie Zheng looking at her. She frowned. “Didn’t catch that?”

Xie Zheng withdrew his gaze. “I’ve got it.”

Fan Changyu was a little skeptical. Still not reassured, she asked, “What did I just say?”

A faint smile touched Xie Zheng’s lips. “Pork rump is thirty-five wen per jin. Don’t go below thirty wen if they haggle.”

Fan Changyu nodded. “Exactly.”

Just then, an older woman passing by after buying groceries spotted Xie Zheng standing inside the pork shop. His appearance was hard to miss, so she asked, “Young man, how much are you selling this hind leg for?”

Fan Changyu said nothing, deliberately wanting to see how Xie Zheng would go about selling meat.

When Xie Zheng looked toward the older woman, his answer was perfectly composed. “Thirty-three wen per jin.”

The older woman muttered, “That’s rather expensive...”

Xie Zheng merely half-lowered his eyes and said nothing further, giving off every indication that she was welcome to buy it if she wanted, but he had no intention of persuading her if she didn’t.

Fan Changyu’s eyelid twitched at the sight. She quickly said, “You can take a look around the other shops first. If you still think ours is the best, you can come back and buy from us.”

The older woman’s little remark had only been an attempt to haggle. Seeing that the price wouldn’t budge, and that the meat did indeed look excellent, she said, “You two young people look like honest folk. Surely you wouldn’t cheat an old woman like me. Cut me two jin.”

Fan Changyu was just about to pick up the knife when she saw that Xie Zheng had already taken it. He estimated the size of the cut, sliced off a piece, and somehow got it to exactly two jin—no more, no less.

Fan Changyu wrapped up the meat and handed it to the older woman. As the woman counted out her copper coins, she kept glancing at Xie Zheng’s face and asked, “Young man, are you married yet? If you’re not, I have a granddaughter who’s only seventeen this year. She’s a lovely girl, and her temperament is good too...”

Xie Zheng’s expression remained indifferent. “This pork shop belongs to my wife. I’m only here to lend her a hand.”

The older woman immediately looked a little sheepish. “Oh... I see.”

She glanced at Fan Changyu. After all, she had lived for decades and knew how to smooth over an awkward moment. With a smile, she said, “You two young spouses are quite a handsome pair, one handsome and one pretty. At first glance, I thought you were brother and sister! Turns out you have *the look of a married couple*. That means you’re blessed with good fortune!”

Fan Changyu could only manage a perfunctory curl of her lips.

As soon as the older woman left, she couldn’t help scolding Xie Zheng. “No matter what, you have to smile when you’re doing business. You sit there with that expression like someone owes you money. Who’s going to come buy your meat?”

As she spoke, another young woman who had come out to buy vegetables glanced at Xie Zheng and, blushing, asked, “How much are the ribs?”

There still wasn’t a trace of unnecessary expression on Xie Zheng’s face. “Thirty-nine wen per jin.”

Pork ribs were the most expensive cut of fresh meat.

The young woman didn’t even dare look at Xie Zheng. Lowering her head, she shyly murmured, “I’ll take three jin. Please chop them into small pieces.”

Xie Zheng picked up the bone cleaver, chopped the ribs into pieces in a few swift strokes, wrapped them up, and handed them over.

Fan Changyu stood to the side, utterly dumbfounded.

For convenience when counting, people in the common market would also string together one hundred copper wen coins with a thin cord. This made transactions easier for both buyer and seller.

Xie Zheng took the one hundred and seventeen wen and handed them to Fan Changyu. Fan Changyu was still standing there in a daze.

She slowly came to terms with the reality: other people sold pork with their silver tongues; this fellow sold pork with his face.

She pressed a hand to her forehead and joked half-seriously, “I should have had you come help at the shop sooner. Who knows, maybe business would have been even better before the New Year.”

Xie Zheng glanced at her but did not reply.

It was still early, and there were few people at the market buying vegetables. The other pork shops had hardly any customers, while the Fan family’s pork shop had already made two sales.

The other butchers were certainly envious, but Fan Changyu also bought pig heads and trotters from their shops for her braised-meat business, and occasionally bought pig offal as well. Since she was still supporting their businesses, none of them begrudged her the good fortune.

But Butcher Guo had long-standing grievances with the Fan family. Fan Changyu would never buy meat from his shop, so the two families had become increasingly hostile toward each other.

As he cleared the snow in front of his own shop, he deliberately flung the snow he had shoveled onto the main street and said sarcastically, “Now that’s what I call selling meat. Why come to a place like this? Wouldn’t it be better to take that face to a brothel and sell it there?”

He was, of course, mocking Xie Zheng for drawing customers to the Fan family’s shop with his looks.

Fan Changyu’s expression darkened on the spot.

She was fiercely protective of her own people. Xie Zheng had pretended to marry into her family in order to help her protect her family’s property. She could tolerate people privately making fun of his status as a live-in husband, but now they were openly slinging filth about him right in front of her. How could she possibly stand for that?

And on top of that, his uncle had recently helped Fan Da plot to seize a share of her family’s property. With old and new grievances piled together, it was high time they settled the score!

Fan Changyu walked out of her family’s pork shop and planted herself right in the middle of the street, crossing her arms as she stared at Butcher Guo, “Say that again.”

Her shout drew the attention of everyone running a shop along the street, as well as the handful of pedestrians out buying vegetables.

Butcher Guo had suffered at Fan Changyu’s hands before, so he did not dare confront her head-on. Instead, he resorted to his sharp tongue.

“What did I say? Oh, right. There was a *whore*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="窑姐儿 or yáojiěr; a degratory term for prostitute"></sup> walking down the road just now, cozying up to some *tu’er ye*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="兔儿爷 a derogatory term for a male who is perceived as effeminate or associated with male same-sex relationships."></sup>. I was talking about that whore and that *tu’er ye*. Why are you, Fan **Da Guniang**, so eager to come over and claim the insult for yourself?”

The moment he finished speaking, a heavy blow struck him squarely under the jaw. The force sent Butcher Guo staggering backward several steps until he crashed into the cabinet inside his shop and finally managed to steady himself.

He clutched his jaw with one hand, feeling as though his upper and lower rows of teeth had been smashed together. The taste of blood filled his mouth, and for a good while he could not get a single word out. He pointed at Fan Changyu with his other hand, but before he could speak, he looked up and met her icy gaze.

She said coldly, “Apologize.”

Butcher Guo endured the wave of pain shooting through his jaw, spat out a mouthful of bloody spittle, and immediately flared up again, “I never said anything about you and that *little white face* husband of yours! You’re the one who eagerly claimed the insult for yourself. Why should I apologize?”

Fan Changyu couldn’t be bothered to waste words on him. She thrust the long stick in her hand forward again.

Butcher Guo ducked in fright, and although the stick had no spearhead, the sheer force behind her strike drove it straight through the wooden panel of the cabinet door.

It was enough to make one wonder whether, had that been his forehead instead, she might have driven the stick clean through his skull and left a bloody hole behind.

Butcher Guo’s legs trembled with fear, yet he still tried to put on a threatening front, “You dare lay a hand on me? My uncle is the magistrate’s private advisor! If this turns into a lawsuit, he can make sure you spend the rest of your life in prison!”

Fan Changyu said, “Believe me or not, but before your advisor uncle can even get here, I can twist your head off and use it as a dog’s feeding bowl.”

When it came to throwing his weight around, Butcher Guo truly couldn’t hold a candle to her. His face immediately fell.

Fan Changyu shouted once more, “Apologize!”

Butcher Guo was extremely reluctant, but with the long stick pointed directly at his face, he could only grit his teeth and apologize in front of everyone. “Sorry.”

Fan Changyu withdrew the stick and gave a cold snort. “People say eunuchs are the ones who love stirring up gossip and making mischief. But you’re even more skilled at running your mouth than a eunuch! So what if you’re jealous of the little bit of business my pork shop gets? With a talent like yours, you really ought to go into the palace and make yourself the Chief Eunuch. Otherwise, it’d be a waste of that tongue of yours, which can turn black into white and white into black!”

The onlookers burst into laughter.

The butchers at the other pork shops were also struggling to hold back their laughter.

“Eunuch? Come to think of it, with that Guo fellow’s whole tough-on-the-outside, weak-on-the-inside act, maybe he really can’t perform as a man!”

“I heard his son looks exactly like his Biao cousin<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="cousin on maternal side; cousins that does not share the same surname."></sup>. Maybe the boy might not have been sired by him!”

“Didn’t everyone say behind their backs that his wife was sleeping around? Heaven help us—his wife has been blamed and cursed behind her back for all these years, but it turns out she wasn’t some loose woman. He’s just useless!”

“He’s built like a bull. How could that thing of his not work?”

“I heard it happened when he was slaughtering a pig. The pig struggled, he couldn’t hold it down, and it fell on the ground and stepped right on his manhood!”

Listening to the onlookers piling one comment on top of another, Butcher Guo’s entire face turned crimson with rage, the veins beneath his neck bulging, “What nonsense are you spouting about? Believe it or not, I’ll cut every last one of you down with my knife!”

The onlookers kept their distance, but the murmurs did not stop.

“Look at him. He clearly got a nerve struck. Could it actually be true?”

“I always wondered why a grown man was so foul-mouthed, always calling handsome young men *tu’er ye*. Turns out he’s the *tu’er ye* himself!”

The gossip grew more and more outrageous. The angrier Butcher Guo became at the onlookers, the more elaborate and convincing everyone made their stories sound.

In the end, Butcher Guo could only glare resentfully at Fan Changyu. “You just wait!”

Fan Changyu couldn’t even be bothered to spare him a glance. She merely said, “Your mouth is as filthy as it gets when you’re talking about other people’s affairs. Now that people are making things up about you, you know how unpleasant it feels?”

With that, she carried her stick back to her own pork shop.

After listening to the crowd’s endless chatter, Butcher Guo had no heart left for doing business that day. He simply shut up shop and retreated home.

Once Fan Changyu returned to the shop, she said to Xie Zheng with some remorse, “I’m sorry. You’re leaving soon, yet I still let you get dragged into that Guo fellow’s gossip.”

He had seen how fiercely she had defended him outside. Xie Zheng merely said, “It’s fine.”

Yet there was a complicated look in his eyes.

Fan Changyu said, “He’s only throwing his weight around because he has an uncle who works as the magistrate’s private advisor. Once the magistrate’s term is up and he’s transferred elsewhere, his uncle won’t amount to anything!”

The scuffle just now had loosened the cloth band tied around her cuff.

Fan Changyu frowned, untied it, and wrapped it again. To secure it more tightly, she held one end of the band between her teeth while awkwardly winding the other end around her sleeve with one hand.

Though winter clothing had narrower cuffs than summer garments, they were still inconvenient when working. And because she often wielded a cleaver to chop through bones, she tied a cloth band around the sleeve at her wrist to keep the cuff from getting in the way and protect her wrist.

Seeing this, Xie Zheng took the band from her hand with his long fingers, “I’ll help you.”

He seemed to be informing her rather than asking for permission. Before Fan Changyu could respond, his other hand had already taken hold of the end of the cloth band between her teeth. “Let go.”

Fan Changyu did not understand at first, then obediently loosened her bite.

By the time she came to her senses, Xie Zheng had already begun methodically rolling her sleeve up in neat folds. He pressed it in place at her wrist with just the right amount of force, then slowly wound the cloth band tighter and tighter around it. The sensation against her wrist was unusually clear.

Her fingertips curled slightly of their own accord.

The cloth band was slate blue. His slender fingers, wrapped in the dark fabric, were pale yet sharply defined with tendons and bones. There was something indescribably pleasing about the sight.

He looked quite focused, yet still found the attention to ask her, “When does your county magistrate’s term end?”

Fan Changyu had already thought the atmosphere felt a little strange, but once he started speaking, the awkwardness seemed to ease. “By my reckoning, once the New Year is over, he’ll have completed his three-year term.”

Xie Zheng said, “Then that advisor’s good days should be coming to an end.”

Under the Great Yin Code, county magistrates appointed to serve outside the capital were replaced every three years. Usually, they would be transferred to another post. Only those who had rendered significant service could be promoted, while a magistrate could also remain in the same place if the local people jointly petitioned for him to stay.

Fan Changyu asked why, and after Xie Zheng explained the relevant regulations, she suddenly understood. Then she grinned. “Then I’m even less afraid of that Guo fellow!”

A magistrate’s advisor was merely a private aide hired by the county magistrate. They were not on the imperial payroll at all.

Since they were the ones helping the magistrate devise strategies, they would inevitably know many of the magistrate’s private and underhanded dealings. Thus, whenever a magistrate was transferred or promoted, he would either take his advisor with him to his new post or give the aide a sum of money and forbid him from serving as anyone else’s aide thereafter.

Considering everything the magistrate of Qingping County had done during his years there, there was no chance the local people would write a petition signed by thousands to beg for him to remain.

So whether the magistrate was promoted or demoted, he would not be staying in Qingping County. And even if Butcher Guo’s uncle continued serving as his advisor, his new post would not be in Qingping County. Naturally, he would no longer be able to throw his weight around there.

After Xie Zheng finished tying the cloth band securely around her cuff, he looked up and caught sight of the bold, unrestrained smile on her face.

His eyes narrowed slightly before he looked away. “Done.”

Fan Changyu rolled her wrist experimentally, the smile still on her face. “It really is tighter than when I tie it myself. Thanks!”

The lingering tightness around her wrist felt almost as though his hand were still resting there. It was only after she rubbed the spot that the strange sensation gradually faded.

Xie Zheng said, “It was no trouble at all.”

Fan Changyu glanced at the sky outside. “I need to hurry and deliver the meat to Yixiang Restaurant. I’ll leave things at the shop to you.”

Xie Zheng said, “Don’t worry.”

Fan Changyu reached the doorway, then turned back to remind him, “If anyone comes to buy meat and we sell out, but they want to place an order for more, make sure you write it down for me too.”

Xie Zheng nodded in agreement.

Only then did Fan Changyu leave with peace of mind. Once she climbed onto the ox cart, she couldn't help rubbing that same wrist again, though she couldn't quite say what felt so strange about it.

The roads were slippery with snow. It took Fan Changyu half an hour of driving to reach Yixiang Restaurant in the county town. From a distance, she could already see a crowd gathered around the entrance. There was even the faint sound of wailing, as though someone were mourning at a funeral. The people were packed so tightly that she could barely get through, let alone bring the ox cart in.

Fan Changyu had no choice but to climb down and ask one of the bystanders gathered outside to watch the commotion, “What happened at Yixiang Restaurant?”

A middle-aged woman who had been watching the spectacle turned to glance at her.

“Someone died after eating the food at Yixiang Restaurant. The dead man’s children brought the coffin straight to the entrance and set it down there. They’re demanding an explanation!”

Fan Changyu’s heart lurched.

She had worked at Yixiang Restaurant herself and knew that the ingredients they purchased were all of the highest quality. When it came to the quality of the food, Yu Qianqian had never been careless. How could someone suddenly have died from eating there?

She grabbed the woman’s arm, “When did this happen?”

Seeing how agitated she was, the woman said, “From what I heard, they came to eat here yesterday at noon. While the person was eating inside, he suddenly started foaming at the mouth. They called a physician right away, but he still couldn’t be saved. Then first thing this morning, the family came here to demand an explanation.”

Two men nearby glanced at Fan Changyu and clicked their tongues, shaking their heads.

“They charge such exorbitant prices, and now their food has actually killed someone! The owner of this restaurant deserves to be punished!”

“If the authorities don’t investigate this properly, who’ll dare eat at a restaurant anymore?”

“I heard long ago that the female owner of Yixiang Restaurant dabbles in all sorts of crooked tricks. Supposedly, she puts something in the food that makes people addicted to it. Otherwise, how could she suddenly open two restaurants in just a few years and have business booming like this? Maybe she put too much of that stuff in the food this time, and that’s what killed the man!”

“If you ask me, it should be a life for a life! Just arrest that female owner and execute her. You can tell from her face that she’s not the sort to *stay within the bounds of propriety*! She’s definitely no good!”

Fan Changyu listened to the two men in felt hats (毡帽), both of them looking shifty and rat-like as they spoke about Yu Qianqian with undisguised contempt. Her anger was so great that her lips pressed into a thin line.

After making her way out of the crowd, she asked the old man driving the ox cart to wait for her on a section of road that wasn't congested, then went to the alley behind Yixiang Restaurant.

She entered through the restaurant's back door and found that there were hardly any people in the kitchen. The manager and the attendants who normally handled distinguished guests were all at the front entrance, arguing with the deceased man's children.

After some difficulty, Fan Changyu spotted an attendant and hurriedly called out to him, “Where’s Yu **Zhanggui**?”

The attendant assumed she had come to deliver braised meat and hurriedly waved his hands. “Fan **Laoban**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="Boss (as in owner of a shop)."></sup> has seen the situation here. We can’t accept your braised meat today.”

Fan Changyu said, “If I’m looking for Yu **Zhanggui** at a time like this, naturally it isn’t about that. What exactly happened to the person who died here yesterday?”

The attendant looked thoroughly put out, “Who knows? Yesterday, one of the guests suddenly fell ill. **Zhanggui** saw him and said it was probably *sheephorned-wind*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="羊角风 (Yángjiǎo fēng) - a historical term for epilepsy."></sup>, so she immediately had someone fetch a physician. At first, the family was extremely grateful. But after they took the man home, he suddenly died during the night. Then first thing this morning, they carried his coffin to the restaurant entrance and started making a scene, demanding that the restaurant pay with their old man's life! Isn't it obvious they're just trying to extort money?”

“No matter how **Zhanggui** tried to reason with them, they wouldn't back down. She was even prepared to pay up and put an end to the matter, but they refused that too. They clearly came here specifically to cause trouble. **Zhanggui** was worried that some other restaurant had set her up, so she reported it to the authorities, but the officers still haven't come. She went to the authorities herself to pull some strings, but she's been gone for quite a while now and still hasn't returned.”

Although Fan Changyu had never studied much, she understood the principle that *a tree which grows too tall attracts the wind*.
<div class="author-note">
树大招风 (shùdà-zhāofēng) an idiom that describes how someone or something that becomes prominent naturally attracts jealousy, hostility, or unwanted attention.
</div>

Yu Qianqian's banquet a few days ago had gone splendidly, and Yixiang Restaurant had made quite a name for itself in the county town, taking away business from quite a few major restaurants. Naturally, that would make others jealous. But for them to use such underhanded means against Yu Qianqian was truly despicable.

The reason so many people had gathered outside Yixiang Restaurant was partly because the family had brought the coffin there to cause a scene. But everyone was condemning Yu Qianqian, and not a single person had stood up to speak in her defense. Some were even claiming that Yixiang Restaurant had added addictive drugs to its dishes.

Fan Changyu immediately thought of the two shifty, rat-like men.

Those two men, clearly *one sings and the other harmonize*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="一唱一和 an idiom means two people deliberately backing each other up."></sup>, deliberately saying those things where people who didn't know the truth could hear them, fanning the flames and stirring up trouble.

If they couldn't persuade the people carrying the coffin to leave, then they could start by taking out the ones manipulating public opinion.

After thinking it over, Fan Changyu said to the attendant, “Find a few more people. Change out of your restaurant uniforms and come outside with me.”

With Yixiang Restaurant caught up in this mess, the attendants were already overwhelmed. One of them said apologetically, “Sorry, Fan **Laoban**, but we really can't spare any people today…”

Fan Changyu said, “There are people in that crowd deliberately smearing Yixiang Restaurant. Bring some men with you and come with me. We'll pick them out.”

The attendant immediately went to gather some people.

A quarter of an hour later, Fan Changyu led seven or eight Yixiang Restaurant attendants dressed in plain clothes out through the back door and once again squeezed into the crowd of onlookers.

She watched for a while. Most of the passersby who had stopped to watch would stay for a bit, then leave once they saw that the matter wasn't being resolved anytime soon and remembered they had other things to attend to.

Only a group resembling the two men in felt hats remained stationed outside Yixiang Restaurant. They cursed louder than anyone else, and whenever an uninformed passerby stopped to ask what was going on, they would immediately launch into that same story about Yixiang Restaurant adding addictive drugs to its food.

Fan Changyu was now fairly certain that these were the troublemakers behind it all. She gave the Yixiang Restaurant attendants a look.

These attendants treated Yixiang Restaurant like their own home. How could they tolerate outsiders slandering it like this?

Following Fan Changyu’s instructions, they pretended to push toward the center of the crowd, forcing the troublemakers toward the outskirts. The attendants behind them then grabbed them by the shoulders and started hauling them away.

These men were already guilty enough to be nervous. The moment someone got a hold of them, they immediately tried to shout and make a scene. Fan Changyu was quick on the draw, landing several hard punches to their stomachs and successfully making them swallow the cries that had already reached their throats.

A few bystanders nearby turned to look. Fan Changyu snarled, “What are you looking at? Never seen debt collectors from a gambling house before?”

As she spoke, she kicked one of the men in the felt hats. “You bastard! Run, then! You think you can *get away on the first day of the month and still escape on the fifteenth*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="a saying meaning that escaping trouble temporarily does not mean one can escape it forever"></sup>?”

The attendants of Yixiang Restaurant stared for a moment as their usually amiable Boss Fan suddenly transformed into a street thug, kicking someone in broad daylight. Then they quickly stepped forward to help, grabbing the two men by their collars and dragging them toward a corner.

Taking the opportunity to settle some personal scores, they punched and kicked them while cursing, “Paying your debts is only right and proper! Run again and we’ll break your legs!”

When the nearby people heard that these were gambling-house debt collectors, and saw that the men looked shifty and clearly weren't decent sorts, they hurriedly moved aside, not daring to meddle in other people's business.

The men still tried to shout, but a filthy rag was quickly stuffed into each of their mouths. All they could do was mumble incoherently as they were dragged into the courtyard in the back alley behind Yixiang Restaurant. There, they were tied together like livestock, staring in terror at Fan Changyu and the group of Yixiang Restaurant attendants disguised as street thugs standing before them with their arms crossed.

Fan Changyu sat in a chair that the Yixiang Restaurant attendants had brought over, looking like a bandit chieftain. She toyed with a boning knife in her hand.

The instant she raised her eyes, the sharp boning knife flew through the air, piercing straight through the top of one of the felt hats. Its momentum carried it onward, pinning the hat to the tree trunk behind the man.

This was the very man who had cursed Yu Qianqian most viciously earlier.

Fan Changyu raised her eyes, ready to deliver a threatening line, but froze for a heartbeat.

Beneath the felt hat was a completely bald head!

No wonder he wore a hat!

Without the felt hat to protect him, the bald man's scalp felt terribly cold. The wind slicing across it stung like a knife. Realizing that a blade really had just flown right past his scalp, his face went white.

Fan Changyu quickly recovered from her momentary surprise and put her fierce expression back on. “Who put you up to causing trouble outside Yixiang Restaurant?”

The shifty-looking man beside the bald man sneered, “Nobody put us up to anything. Someone died after eating at Yixiang Restaurant, and we're not allowed to demand justice? You tied us up and brought us here, and now you want to kill us and silence everyone too? This Yixiang Restaurant isn't a restaurant at all. It's a black-hearted den of murderers and bandits!”

Fan Changyu was thoroughly irritated by the man's endless blathering. He was exactly the same kind of disgusting nuisance as Butcher Guo!

She grabbed the wooden mallet leaning against the wall and brought it down hard on his forehead three times. The crisp “bonk, bonk, bonk” was certainly much more pleasant to listen to.

The man was clearly stunned by the beating.

Fan Changyu barked, “Did I give you permission to speak?”

The other ruffians who had been tied up swallowed hard. They awkwardly shuffled themselves farther away from the man without making it obvious, shrinking into themselves as much as possible to minimize their presence.

The man still wanted to keep running his mouth, but when he caught sight of the wooden mallet in Fan Changyu’s hand, his forehead still throbbing with a dull pain that felt as though his skull had been split open, he sullenly shut his mouth.

Fan Changyu gave a cold snort. “That tongue of yours is useless anyway. Take him away. Chop his tongue to pieces and feed it to the dogs!”

The attendants of Yixiang Restaurant first exchanged glances, then immediately sent two men forward. They dragged the bound ruffian into the courtyard at the back.

The sound of knives being sharpened soon followed. Then came the heavy thud of a blade striking a chopping block, accompanied by the man's screams. A short while later, even the screams stopped, leaving only muffled whimpering.

The several ruffians tied up in the courtyard were so frightened that their faces turned ashen.

Fan Changyu herself nearly slid off her chair. She had only been putting on an act and trying to frighten these men, just as the heroes in storybooks did. Surely the attendants at Yixiang Restaurant hadn't misunderstood her and actually cut the man's tongue out?

Before long, an attendant came over carrying a basin. Inside was a short, bloody strip of tongue. “That bastard struggled too much. We couldn't pull out the whole thing, so we only managed to chop off this section.”

The ruffians were already so terrified by the bloody mass of flesh that they nearly wet themselves. They didn't dare take another look. Fan Changyu, however, slaughtered pigs regularly and recognized it at a glance as a short piece of pig tongue. It wasn't even particularly fresh anymore, and someone had smeared it with what was probably chicken or duck blood. Set on a plate like this, though, it was certainly convincing enough to fool someone.

She breathed a sigh of relief. The attendants at Yixiang Restaurant really were quite quick-witted. Maintaining her fierce expression, she ordered, “Bring a dog. Feed it to the dog.”

An attendant immediately led over a wolfdog and tossed the piece of pig tongue from the plate. The dog wolfed it down at once.

Several of the ruffians watched in horror and couldn't stop dry-heaving. A few were even so frightened that they wet themselves.

Fan Changyu figured that after frightening them this badly, they could probably be made to tell the truth. She kept her fierce expression and continued questioning the bald man.

“Speak. Who sent you to cause trouble at Yixiang Restaurant? If you tell a single lie, we’ll cut out your tongues and feed them to the dogs too!”

The bald man gagged so hard that tears streamed from his eyes. He hurriedly said, “I’ll talk! I’ll talk! It was the servant of Advisor He who came to find us.”

Fan Changyu couldn't help freezing for a moment at that answer.

How was that troublemaker, Advisor He, involved again?

She shouted, “You’re lying!”

Even with his hands tied, the bald man kept knocking his head against the ground in front of her. “**Gu Nainai**, this small one is really not lying! It really was the yamen advisor who came to find us!”
<div class="author-note">
姑奶奶 (gūnǎinai) is literally “grand-aunt” but in colloquial speech it can be used to acknowledge a woman is superior.
</div>

Fan Changyu said, “Advisor He has no grudge against Yixiang Restaurant. Why would he have you do something like this?”

The bald man burst into tears, “We don't know either!”

The other ruffians also began crying pitifully, all pointing the finger at Yamen Advisor He.

“Let them go,” came a woman's voice from the moon gate.

Fan Changyu looked up and saw Yu Qianqian. She rose from her chair. “Yu **Zhanggui** is back?”

Yu Qianqian nodded. There was a smile in her eyes as she looked at Fan Changyu, along with a touch of gratitude. “I just got back. I happened to hear you helping me interrogate these men. Thank you, Changyu **Meizi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="妹子 is a familiar form of address for a younger woman; lit. younger sister."></sup>.”

Fan Changyu said, “I didn't really help much, Yu **Zhanggui**.”

Yu Qianqian said, “This is more than enough. Let them go.”

She gestured to the attendants nearby to untie the ruffians.

The ruffian whom Fan Changyu had previously ordered the attendants to take away was brought out as well. His tongue had not been cut out; his mouth had merely been gagged. Clearly, the screams they had heard earlier had been produced by Yu Qianqian using some method of her own.

Fan Changyu was puzzled and asked Yu Qianqian, “Aren’t you going to take them to court and confront them there?”

Yu Qianqian merely shook her head, weariness showing on her face. Only after the attendants had taken the ruffians away did she say, “You found out just now as well. It was Advisor He who instructed these men.”

Fan Changyu frowned. “Did one of the restaurants competing with Yixiang Restaurant get in touch with Advisor He through his connections and try to use him to bring down Yixiang Restaurant?”

Yu Qianqian gave a bitter smile, “It’s worse than that.”

Fan Changyu had thought the worst that could happen was what she had just guessed. When Yu Qianqian said it was even worse, she genuinely couldn't imagine what could be beyond that. “What exactly happened?”

Yu Qianqian's bangs, which she normally kept sleek and perfectly smooth, had long since been mussed up from how much she'd run her fingers through them. She closed her eyes, “Yixiang Restaurant can't be saved. And it's my own fault. I pushed too hard. If only I hadn't rushed to open a restaurant in the county town last year…”

In Fan Changyu's impression, Yu Qianqian had always seemed to have everything firmly under control. It was rare to see her this helpless. “My relationship with Yu **Zhanggui** may not be especially deep, but **Zhanggui** has helped me time and again, and I've always kept that kindness in my heart. I don't know what trouble Yixiang Restaurant has actually run into, but if **Zhanggui** need it, my family still has some connection with Head Constable Wang at the county yamen. I can ask him for a favor and see if he can help Yixiang Restaurant.”

Yu Qianqian shook her head, “It won't help.”

She took Fan Changyu's hand and gave it a squeeze, managing a faint smile. “I appreciate the thought. I spent this entire half-day making use of every connection I could. If there were any way out, I wouldn't just sit here and wait for disaster. And don't go asking Head Constable Wang for a favor either. You'll only get him into trouble.”

Fan Changyu could feel how exhausted Yu Qianqian was. Even she hadn't imagined that something like this could happen to Yixiang Restaurant overnight. She said, “I still can't figure out what kind of trouble Yixiang Restaurant has gotten into. According to the attendants, the old man who ate at the restaurant yesterday had an *sheep-horned-wind* and started foaming at the mouth. How can the restaurant's food possibly be blamed for that? And if it really went to court, there'd be a physician who could testify to it, right?”

Yu Qianqian said, “Do you know who Advisor He works for?”

Fan Changyu answered with two words, “County magistrate?”

Yu Qianqian nodded wearily. “The most powerful official in all of Qingping County wants to seize my family's property. When it comes to right and wrong in court, isn't it all up to him to decide? What ordinary commoner would dare oppose an official?”

“Then take him to court in Jizhou Prefectural Office,” Fan Changyu said. “The county magistrate may be the most powerful official in Qingping County, but once you leave Qingping County, what power does he have?”

Yu Qianqian still shook her head, a trace of anguish appearing in her expression. “I heard the rumors from some of the noblewomen and learned that this was the county magistrate's doing, so I sent my guards by carriage to Jizhou Prefectural Office. But just as I came through the door earlier, someone sent something over…”

Yu Qianqian’s voice trembled. “It was one of my guards’ severed fingers. The authorities and the bandits are working together. Every road leading to Jizhou Prefectural Office has been blocked by bandits.”

Fan Changyu finally understood what it meant for someone to have power that reached everywhere. What Yu Qianqian was facing now was even more desperate than when Fan Da had tried to seize her family’s property.

The authorities had already spread the rumor that Yixiang Restaurant was putting something in its food. Now that an old man had happened to fall ill and die while eating there, the authorities could simply claim that there was something wrong with Yu Qianqian’s food. They could confiscate everything she owned and might even have her arrested and thrown into prison.

In the blink of an eye, Fan Changyu remembered what Xie Zheng had previously said about the military grain being requisitioned in Jizhou Prefecture.

She said, “On your own, you’re certainly too weak to fight them. But if the people of the entire Qingping County rise up against the county magistrate, then whether the authorities block the roads to the prefectural seat or send constables to suppress us, neither would be a problem!”

Yu Qianqian asked, “What do you mean?”

Fan Changyu said, “Jizhou Prefectural Office is requisitioning grain for the army. In our county, they’re collecting one shi of grain per person, or silver in lieu of the grain if people can’t pay. Qingping County has more than a hundred thousand people. Just from our county alone, they could forcibly collect over a hundred thousand shi of grain. There’s no way the authorities in Jizhou would drive the common people into a dead end like this. Clearly, the county magistrate is using the opportunity to line his own pockets!”

Yu Qianqian’s expression changed drastically as she listened.

She murmured, “The county magistrate isn't lining his pockets. At the very moment he's about to be transferred, he suddenly squeezes so much silver out of the common people, then sets his sights on my Yixiang Restaurant as well. Even if he can conceal it for a while, he can't hide it forever. Someone will eventually expose him, and even after his transfer, he won't escape punishment. Perhaps… Yixiang Restaurant is merely *the chicken he's killing to scare the monkeys*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="an idiom meaning to punish one person as a warning to others"></sup>! The wealthy merchants throughout Qingping County are his real target!”

She looked at Fan Changyu, her expression grim. “Chongzhou borders Jizhou. The county magistrate is planning to defect to the rebel prince!”
