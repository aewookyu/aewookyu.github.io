Seeing that Sui Yuanqing was clearly at odds with the bandit chief—and that he seemed to already be betrothed—Fan Changyu could not help cursing him inwardly for his *treachery*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="奸诈 (jiānzhà) - treacherous, sly, cunning, or deceitful"></sup>.

The bandit chief wanted him to kill her. If he refused, the chief would do it himself, and then her having him at knifepoint would be exposed.

For a moment, cold sweat seeped into the palm gripping the boning knife. Fan Changyu forced herself to remain calm.

The worst that could happen was that they discovered she was holding him hostage. As long as she took his life before he could reveal that there were people hiding in the dry well, Changning and the others would still be safe.

She was alone, with nothing to lose. Once she got hold of a horse, escaping was not entirely impossible.

Sui Yuanqing was standing extremely close to Fan Changyu, so he could naturally feel how tensed up she was. She was like a hunting beast, ready to explode at any moment and tear her prey to pieces.

If she really struck with that knife, he would undoubtedly be the first one to die.

Sui Yuanqing did not tease her at a time like this. He merely chuckled and said, “**Da Ge**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. Eldest Brother, or in this case a term to address the big boss"></sup> wants me to keep myself chaste for Shisan Niang, to have eyes for no one but her for the rest of my life?”

They were all bandits. How could they not know a man’s baser instincts?

The scar-faced man barked, “She’s the only younger sister I have. What happens after you’re married is none of my business, but the two of you aren’t even married yet, and you’re already bringing another woman back to the stronghold. What do you take Qingfeng Stronghold for?”

Sui Yuanqing frowned. His tone, surprisingly, held a trace of genuine sincerity. “I joined Qingfeng Stronghold and became sworn brothers with **Da Ge**. It was never because I needed a woman to advance my standing. I, Qin Yuan, am a born playboy. I truly am not worthy of Shisan Niang. Why doesn’t **Da Ge** find Shisan Niang another husband? From now on, I’ll treat her as my own sister. If anyone dares bully her, I’ll be the first to refuse to let it go.”

The scar-faced man gritted his teeth. “Do you really look down on my sister that much?”

Sui Yuanqing lowered his eyes. “It’s because I, Qin Yuan, am naturally frivolous and unfit to be Shisan Niang’s husband. Today, **Da Ge** can force me to kill this woman. But what about tomorrow? If I take a fancy to another woman, will **Da Ge** force me to kill her too? If this continues, all it will do is damage the bond between us brothers. Better to make things clear now.”

Though the scar-faced man was furious, he knew there was some truth to what Sui Yuanqing said.

It was precisely this rakish nature of his that had nearly made Shisan Niang lose her soul over him. But that was simply his nature. He could force Sui Yuanqing to behave for a while, but could he force him to do so for the rest of his life?

Still, he could not help feeling indignant on his sister’s behalf. He shouted, “Shisan Niang rescued you when she found you by the river. Setting aside that debt of gratitude, the two of you have already shared *intimacy*. If you refuse to marry Shisan Niang, where does that leave her?”
<div class="author-note">
肌肤之亲 (jīfū zhī qīn) — Literally “skin-to-skin intimacy”. Here it is a euphemism for having had sexual relations.
</div>

Sui Yuanqing raised his eyes. “So, for the sake of making me marry Shisan Niang, **Da Ge** is going to resort to the conventions of society?”

The scar-faced man’s expression darkened. He knew perfectly well that his own argument did not hold up.

These people of *Jianghu* were not bound by the finer points of propriety. If he really tried to make an issue of the fact that his sister had saved Sui Yuanqing and the two had shared intimacy, it would only make him a laughingstock if word got out.
<div class="author-note">
江湖 - jianghu refers to the wider world of wandering martial artists, outlaws, bandits, and other people living outside ordinary official society.
</div>

In the end, he did not press the matter further. With a dark expression, he turned his horse around and barked, “Back to Qingfeng Stronghold!”

The group of underlings below him also dispersed in a clamor, with only a few remaining behind to flatter Sui Yuanqing.

“**Wu Dangjia**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="Fifth Chief"></sup> really is a true man. There used to be people saying **Wu Dangjia** only caught **Da Dangjia**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="First Chief"></sup>’s eye because of Shisan Niang. Clearly, **Da Dangjia** recognizes **Wu Dangjia**’s abilities and wants to marry his little sister to him. The day Heilong Stronghold<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="黑龙寨 - Literally Black Dragon"></sup> attacked us, it was all thanks to **Wu Dangjia**s’s strategy that we defeated them…”

The wound beneath Sui Yuanqing’s ribs was still being held at knifepoint by Fan Changyu, so he had no mind to listen to these underlings singing his praises. He cut them off, “Don’t spout nonsense. You’ll only damage the bond between **Da Ge** and me. **Da Ge** treats me like a real brother; he was merely being overprotective of his sister. Back to the stronghold.”

The few men took the hint. They looked sheepish and did not dare continue deliberately saying things they thought would please him.

Fan Changyu remained silent. From their exchange, she now understood why Sui Yuanqing had ended up mixed in with these bandits.

So after being wounded by Yan Zheng that day, he had fled into the river and been rescued by the people of Qingfeng Stronghold.

She noticed that among the few underlings who had been flattering Sui Yuanqing, one had quietly moved ahead. She wondered if that man was actually one of Qingfeng Stronghold’s First Chief’s men. Perhaps Sui Yuanqing knew this and had deliberately said those words because of him?

Fan Changyu thought to herself that this fellow really did have more schemes than an ear of lotus root had holes.

With a knife pressed against his chest, Sui Yuanqing acted as though nothing was wrong as he led Fan Changyu to his horse. Only when they reached it did he lower his head slightly and ask her in a hushed voice, “With you holding this knife against me, I can’t get on the horse. What are we going to do?”

Whether he mounted first or Fan Changyu mounted first, she would no longer be able to keep him hostage.

The few underlings assumed Sui Yuanqing was merely flirting with the woman. They stole a few itching glances, but did not dare overstep. They muttered a couple of crude jokes to each other, laughed, and headed on ahead.

One of them said, “This time, we really hit the jackpot. Once we get back to the stronghold, we can take it easy for a good while. Last time, when **Da Ge** took us to ambush those escort guards, looking for some damn treasure map, it turned out to be a fake…”

Fan Changyu had been concentrating solely on dealing with Sui Yuanqing. The moment she heard those words, however, the blood in her entire body seemed to surge backward.

Ambushing escort guards, treasure map…

When the authorities had closed the case, hadn't they said that her parents had died at the hands of bandits because of a treasure map?

Could it really be such a coincidence that her parents had also been killed by this very group?

She could barely suppress the murderous intent surging from her.

Seeing that she had fallen silent while the murderous aura around her only grew stronger, Sui Yuanqing immediately became wary as well. He assumed Fan Changyu intended to kill him with a single stroke here, then flee on horseback alone.

He said, “Killing me here isn't exactly a wise choice, unless you want to face several hundred people by yourself.”

Fan Changyu tightened her grip on the knife. She knew this was not the time to act on impulse. There would be other opportunities to avenge her parents. Changning, Auntie Zhao, and the others were still hiding in the dry well. Drawing these bandits away was what mattered most right now.

After a brief calculation, she said, “Unfasten the sword at your waist and give it to me. Get on the horse first, then pull me up.”

Sui Yuanqing thought he had talked her down. The corners of his mouth lifted slightly, and he did as she said.

But the instant he turned sideways to grab the saddle, Fan Changyu suddenly swung her knife at the back of his neck.

Sui Yuanqing had already suffered from this trick once before, and this time he had been prepared. As the force of Fan Changyu’s strike came down, he twisted aside and dodged it, then moved with lightning speed to intercept the hand holding the knife. He wrenched it hard.

Fan Changyu immediately let out a muffled groan. Her entire arm went limp, and she knew at once that it had been dislocated.

Seeing the cold sweat beading at her temple, Sui Yuanqing said unhurriedly, “I won't fall into the same trap twice…”

Fan Changyu's slightly round almond eyes fixed fiercely on him, filled with a hard, ruthless determination.

When Sui Yuanqing pulled her dislocated arm toward him, intending to catch her other hand, Fan Changyu simply drove her foot viciously between his legs.

This time, it was Sui Yuanqing who let out a muffled groan. His face contorted with pain as he doubled over, gritting his teeth. “You…”

He had clearly never expected Fan Changyu to resort to such a low trick.

Fan Changyu had no concern whatsoever for whether it was fair. As he doubled over, she slammed her elbow hard into the back of his neck twice.

Sui Yuanqing's vision went black in waves, and in the end, he staggered and collapsed.

The commotion also drew the attention of the few underlings walking ahead.

Holding torches, the men stared in stunned silence at Fan Changyu, who was dragging the unconscious Sui Yuanqing around like a dead dog.

Fan Changyu had no time to think. She quickly hauled Sui Yuanqing onto the horse's back.

She would have liked nothing more than to finish him off with a single stroke right there, but what Sui Yuanqing had said earlier was indeed reasonable. Even at full strength, she might not have been able to handle this band of bandits. Now that one of her arms was dislocated, the situation was even more dangerous. It would be better to keep him with her. At a critical moment, she could still use him as a hostage to save her own life.

Everything happened in the blink of an eye. The underlings finally realized what had happened and shouted, “That woman has **Wu Dangjia** hostage!”

A group of them raised their torches and drew their swords, rushing forward to stop Fan Changyu. But Fan Changyu had already mounted the horse. She dug her heels into its sides and charged out of the alley. The underlings were afraid of being trampled to death by the horse, so some dodged out of the way while others were knocked against the walls. In the end, none of them managed to stop her.

The commotion made the scar-faced man, who was riding at the very front, turn around as well. All he saw amid the blazing torchlight was a sturdy horse charging out of the alley with two people on its back. The person holding the reins was unmistakably a woman.

The woman had evidently spotted him too. After a moment of shock, she hurriedly wheeled the horse around and fled in the opposite direction down the main street.

The underlings chasing out of the alley shouted, “**Wu Dangjia** is on that woman’s horse!”

Remembering how, when he had first seen Sui Yuanqing, the woman had spent the entire time leaning bonelessly against him, hardly looking like a respectable woman at all, the scar-faced man immediately realized something was wrong. He spurred his horse after her. “Stop that woman!”

Fan Changyu led the group more than ten li out of Lin’an Town. Some of the bandits were skilled riders and archers, and they fired cold arrows at her the entire way. Fan Changyu simply moved the unconscious Sui Yuanqing behind her on the horse and used him as a human shield. With him in the way, the bandits were forced to hold back, and their arrows no longer struck her.

But the horse beneath her was carrying two people, so it could not run as fast as the mounted bandits behind them. The longer the chase continued, the more tightly the bandits closed the distance. The thunderous pounding of hooves coming from behind on the official road seemed to fall into the same rhythm as Fan Changyu's heartbeat.

Fan Changyu estimated that Auntie Zhao and the others should have already escaped Lin’an Town with Changning. Once they reached the bend in the road ahead, as long as she let the horse continue running forward and drew the bandits after it, she could jump off and temporarily evade their pursuit.

She glanced at the man she was using as a human shield behind her. Thinking of the unrest in Qingping County that day and all the innocent people who had died tonight, she drew the boning knife from her waist.

But before she could bring the knife down, the man who had been jolted about the entire ride happened to regain consciousness at that very moment. Seeing the flash of steel before him, he instinctively seized Fan Changyu's arm and twisted it hard.

Having already learned her lesson from the time he dislocated her arm, Fan Changyu reacted extremely quickly this time. Using his own force against him, she threw herself backward and slammed her elbow hard into the wound on his chest.

Sui Yuanqing cried out in pain and released the hand clamped around her wrist. Fan Changyu immediately reversed the knife and struck again, but he had no time to dodge, so he kicked the back of the knee supporting her in the stirrup.

Fan Changyu's balance gave way, and she was about to fall from the horse. The knife in her hand changed direction and plunged toward the horse's rump.

As Fan Changyu fell from the horse, the horse let out a pained whinny and then bolted like mad.

Sui Yuanqing had only just dodged the knife when he nearly got thrown from the horse. Falling from a horse running at this speed could leave him crippled even if it did not kill him outright. He could only curse inwardly and first seize the reins, steadying himself on the horse's back.

The ground was covered in snow nearly a foot thick. After landing, Fan Changyu rolled several times before finally bleeding off her momentum, escaping any additional injuries. But her dislocated arm had been crushed beneath her as she rolled, sending a stabbing pain through it. The shoe on one of her feet had also been torn off by the stirrup when she fell.

Fan Changyu had no time to worry about the cold or the pain. She quickly scrambled to her feet, picked up her shoe, and threw it toward the riverbank below the official road. Then she plunged headfirst into the snow-covered woods on the inner side of the road.

Before long, the thunderous sound of hooves drew near, racing straight past without stopping as they pursued the horse that was still running wildly ahead.

Heavy snow continued to fall thickly from the sky. Fan Changyu slowly exhaled a breath.

She had thrown her shoe toward the riverbank so that when the bandits came back searching for her, they would mistake it for evidence that she had escaped through the river.

She herself headed deeper into the pine forest. Snowy weather was the worst possible condition for hiding: everywhere she walked, she would leave footprints in the snow. Fortunately, it was nighttime. Without torchlight, those tracks would not be easy to spot.

Even so, Fan Changyu still broke off a pine branch and swept away all the footprints she had left in the snow.

The snow was falling heavily now. Once she swept the tracks away, the fresh snowfall would soon cover the traces completely.

Only after she had dealt with the stretch of footprints leading into the forest did Fan Changyu discard the pine branch. She used the Big Dipper overhead to determine her direction and continued onward.

One foot was bare of a shoe, covered only by a felt sock. She trudged through the snow, sometimes sinking deep and sometimes shallow. The felt sock was soon soaked through with melting snow, and an icy coldness seeped up from the sole of her foot. Her entire leg was nearly numb from the cold, leaving her lips pale and her body trembling uncontrollably.

But Fan Changyu did not dare stop for even a moment.

Changning was still waiting for her.

A squad of scouts reached the borders of Qingping County. From afar, they saw the entire county town blazing with fire, and every one of them was startled.

The well-trained scouts dismounted and were about to investigate the enemy's movements when they suddenly spotted more than a dozen elderly people, women, and children supporting one another as they made their way toward them along a rugged path…

In Lucheng City

At daybreak, a courier on a swift horse raced toward the Yanzhou military camp.

“Qingping County was completely massacred?”

Inside the central command tent, the usually gentle and even-tempered Gongsun Yin wore a rare look of grim severity.

The scout who had ridden through the night to deliver the report lowered his head. “When we went to Qingping County on **Xiansheng**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. Master, traditionally often used for scholars who were held in high regard."></sup>’s orders, the entire place was already a dead city. It was only when we went to investigate what had happened that we unexpectedly came across a few households with survivors.”

Gongsun Yin hurriedly asked, “Where have they been taken?”

The scout replied, “I returned first to report. The remaining troops are escorting those dozen or so survivors to Jizhou Prefectural Office.”

Gongsun Yin paced around the tent with his hands behind his back before asking, “Was that butcher's daughter surnamed Fan among them?”

The scout said, “No. But her younger sister was. The dozen or so survivors said that the butcher's daughter hid them in the cellar of her home and entrusted them with taking care of her younger sister. When the bandits discovered that people were hiding in the cellar, the butcher's daughter somehow managed to lure them away. After the survivors came out, they could not find the butcher's daughter's body. They believe the bandits took her back to their stronghold.”

Gongsun Yin had never met Fan Changyu, but hearing that she had allowed herself to be captured by the bandits in order to protect those people, he could not help thinking that, whatever else one might say about her, her courage and sense of righteousness were no less than any man's.

He dismissed the scout and summoned one of his personal guards. “Where is **Houyé** now?”

The guard cupped his fist, “**Houyé** went to inspect the terrain along the river valley first thing this morning.”

Gongsun Yin naturally knew why Xie Zheng had suddenly gone to inspect the river valley. The grain routes to Jinzhou and Huizhou depended on Chongzhou, but Jizhou also had a waterway that could be used to transport grain. It had simply become unusable since the rivers fell with the onset of winter. Once spring arrived, the waterway could be reopened.

If Jizhou fell as well, they would truly have been seized by the throat by Prince Changxin.

To hold Jizhou, they could not afford to lose Lucheng City's defenses.

He and Xie Zheng had already discussed it. The most effective way to deal with Prince Changxin's army of fifty thousand would be to make use of the spring floods after winter ended.

Gongsun Yin said, “Send someone to find **Houyé** immediately!”

He had barely finished speaking when a personal guard outside called, “**Houyé**.”

Gongsun Yin was already at his wits' end. Hearing the voice, he hurried out to meet him.

Dawn was breaking. Outside the tent, a man approached in dark military armor. Fine snow and morning frost clung to his shoulder guards and cloak. His features were cold and severe, and the handsome, peerless face beneath them carried the chill of frost and snow, making him almost impossible to look at directly.

The moment Gongsun Yin saw him, he got straight to the point. “It looks like someone took revenge on Qingping County. The entire county town was massacred.”

Xie Zheng paused as he removed his cloak. “When did this happen?”

Gongsun Yin said, “The scouts just brought back the news. They say it was the work of bandits. The butcher's daughter named Fan from Lin’an Town was also taken by them. I find it rather strange. Prince Changxin’s Shizi has yet to be found. Could this be his retaliation?”

Xie Zheng took a sword from the weapon rack and strode outside. “Prepare the horses. Take a hundred light cavalry with me to Qingping County!”

By the time dawn broke, Fan Changyu had finally reached the main road. The bandits had long since vanished from sight.

She had spent half the night walking through the snow with one bare foot. Now, utterly exhausted, she found her head throbbing in waves, perhaps because she had caught a chill.

Fan Changyu cursed Sui Yuanqing a thousand times in her heart, thinking that the next time they met, she would absolutely take that bastard’s life.

Jizhou Prefectural Office lay south of Qingping County. After leaving Qingping County, Auntie Zhao and the others would surely go to Jizhou Prefectural Office to report the incident to the authorities. If she headed there too, she would eventually run into them.

Hearing the rumble of carriage wheels in the distance, Fan Changyu knew that bandits who robbed travelers either lay in ambush in groups or rode on horseback. They would not be operating with just a single carriage, so she made no effort to avoid it.

When the carriage approached, she cast it a glance. It was not particularly ornate, but its wheels appeared a full circle larger than those of ordinary carriages, making for a remarkably steady ride through the snow. The carriage shafts looked sturdy too, and its canopy was made of a thick material Fan Changyu had never seen before.

She guessed it belonged to some wealthy household. After taking only a glance, she lowered her head and continued on her way.

Seeing that Fan Changyu was walking with one bare foot, the coachman remarked to the person inside, “That young lady truly doesn't feel the cold. Walking along the road barefoot in weather like this.”

A fair, slender hand lifted the heavy curtain. The man's pale eyes reflected the snowy landscape along the official road—and the woman walking barefoot through it. “Something must have happened to her. Ask where she lives and offer her a ride.”

Once the gentleman had spoken, the coachman naturally did not dare refuse. He stopped the carriage and called out to Fan Changyu, “**Xiao Niangzi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="literally “Little/Young Miss”. "></sup>, where do you live? My master took pity on you having to travel in the snow and is willing to give you a ride.”

Fan Changyu knew her physical condition was not looking good. Her dislocated arm had swollen from going untreated, and her bare foot had grown so cold and painful that she could no longer feel it.

She did not insist on toughing it out and said, “I want to go to the Jizhou Prefectural Office.”

To report the incident.

The coachman said, “That's on our way. Come on up.”

Fan Changyu thanked him and climbed into the carriage.

The moment the curtain was lifted, a wave of warmth washed over her.

The young man seated on the daybed was dressed in a snow-blue robe without a single decorative pattern or embroidered flourish. Yet it conveyed a certain sense of *the greatest truths are found in simplicity*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="大道至简, a classic philosophical concept"></sup>.

At first glance, Fan Changyu could tell that he was truly a scholar.

Unlike Song Yan's self-righteous aloofness, or Yan Zheng's carefree irreverence and wild arrogance, this man's brows and eyes held a gentle, even-tempered warmth. He was like a ray of sunlight suddenly breaking through the ice and snow, making people feel inexplicably at ease in his presence.

The young man did not show any impatience or mockery when he noticed Fan Changyu staring at him. He merely gave her a polite little nod. Seeing that snow clung to her collar and the ends of her hair, he pushed the charcoal brazier closer to her and offered her a cloak made of some material she could not identify, but which felt exceptionally soft to the touch.

“**Guniang**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="young lady"></sup>’s shoes and socks are soaked through. Come, warm yourself by the fire.”

Fan Changyu knew how wretched she looked at that moment. The furnishings inside the carriage appeared simple, yet possessed a certain refinement she could not quite put into words. She tried to sit only in the small space near the carriage door and shook her head. “Thank you, **Gongzi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="young master/gentleman"></sup>, but I'm not cold.”

The frost and snow on her head and eyelashes melted in the warmth of the carriage, forming tiny droplets that clung to them. She looked like a little leopard that had emerged from the mountain forest at dawn, its fur covered in morning dew.

With her aggression stripped away, she instead revealed a somewhat bewildered, guileless, and a pitiable air.

The young man assumed she was uncomfortable with his presence in the carriage. He closed his book and smiled warmly. “It's a little stuffy sitting in the carriage for too long. I'll go outside for some fresh air.”

As he spoke, he lifted the carriage curtain and went to sit beside the coachman outside.

Fan Changyu stared at the heavy curtain swaying in his wake, momentarily stunned.

The warmth from the charcoal brazier finally brought some feeling back to her limbs after they had been numb with cold for so long. Even so, Fan Changyu still declined the cloak. She folded it neatly and placed it on the daybed.

She only used the brazier to dry the clothes that had been soaked by melting snow.

The pair of deerskin bracers on her arms grew rather hot from the heat. Through her clothing, the warmth spread throughout her wrists.

With one arm dislocated, Fan Changyu had difficulty undoing the bracers, and even if she did, fastening them again would be troublesome. So she simply left them on and continued warming herself by the fire.

When she felt the bracers growing hot, she lifted her hand and pressed it against her cheek.

Remembering what Yan Zheng had said on the day he left, she felt a strange tightness in her chest.

When her clothes were nearly dry, Fan Changyu was just about to invite the young man back inside when the carriage suddenly came to a halt.

She heard the coachman's muffled groan from outside, followed by the thud of something heavy hitting the ground. Instantly, Fan Changyu tightened her grip on the boning knife concealed against her body.

Hoofbeats sounded outside the carriage, followed by laughter and casual conversation. “We didn't find the woman who injured **Wu Dangjia**, but we happened to stumble upon a much bigger ‘fish’ along the way.”

The young man had clearly never encountered anything like this before. There was a hint of panic in his voice, but his words remained fairly composed. “Gentlemen, please spare my servant's life. You may take whatever valuables are in the carriage. If that isn't enough, I can write a letter home and have more money sent over. That can be arranged.”

Seeing how cooperative he was, the bandits burst into laughter. “Good lad, you know how to read the situation!”

Several bandits immediately stepped forward to lift the carriage curtain and inspect what was inside. Afraid they might recognize her, Fan Changyu had no choice but to shake out the cloak she had placed on the daybed and drape it over herself.

She could only hope that, in the darkness of the previous night, with her face concealed beneath Sui Yuanqing's cloak for most of the time, these men had not remembered her features clearly.

But before they could even lift the curtain, Fan Changyu heard the sound of a sharp weapon piercing flesh.

Outside, the young man demanded furiously, “You… why did you kill him?”

The bandits roared with laughter. “You're the only one worth keeping alive for ransom. Why should we go to the trouble of dragging your servant along too? If there's a woman in the carriage, though, we might just take her back to the stronghold.”

When one of the bandits used his knife to lift the carriage curtain, the person inside immediately kicked him flying more than ten feet away.

The remaining bandits were startled by the sudden turn of events and failed to react in time.

Fan Changyu, cloak wrapped around her, lunged toward the front of the carriage. She slashed through the reins securing the horse to the carriage, then stepped onto the carriage shaft and landed on the horse's back.

With one hand gripping the reins tightly, she urged the horse forward. As she passed the young man, she reached down and scooped him up around the waist.

“It's the woman who injured **Wu Dangjia**! After her!”

The bandits, finally recovering, pounced after them like a pack of hyenas.

The young man had clearly never ridden a horse before. He was nearly thrown from the saddle by the jolting ride. Fan Changyu shouted, “Grab onto my clothes!”

The young man was truly a stickler for propriety. Even with his life hanging by a thread, he still showed not the slightest impropriety. When Fan Changyu told him to grab onto her clothes, he genuinely did nothing more than clutch tightly at the fabric around her waist, nearly being thrown from the horse several times.

Fan Changyu simply couldn’t spare the attention to hold on to him herself, so she grabbed him by the collar and laid him sideways across the front of the saddle. At least now he wouldn’t be thrown off the horse—but the jolting nearly turned his stomach inside out.

The bandits behind them pursued relentlessly. Then, at the three-way fork ahead, another group of bandits came charging in to block their path. Their leader was none other than the scar-faced man.

When the two groups collided, both sides froze in surprise.

Fan Changyu noticed that most of the bandits in this new group were covered in blood, their expressions disheveled, as though they had just emerged from a fierce battle.

For the moment, she couldn’t figure out who they had fought. Instinctively, she chose the only road that remained open and fled down it.

The bandits who had been chasing Fan Changyu soon caught up as well. Upon seeing the other group, one of them asked, “**Da Dangjia**, what are you doing here too?”

The scar-faced man said bitterly, “Qingfeng Stronghold has been wiped out by the government troops!”

The pursuing bandits stared blankly. “Then what do we do?”

“Capture that woman!” the scar-faced man ordered. “The government troops are looking for the woman who injured **Wu Di**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="Fifth (younger) Brother; he's referring to Sui Yuanqing who is the Fifth in command"></sup> last night!”

As the two groups joined forces and resumed the chase, Fan Changyu cursed inwardly: She hasn't even dug up their ancestors’ graves, do they really have to chase her like their lives depend on it?

The official road stretched straight ahead, ending at a ferry crossing.
<div class="author-note">
渡口 (ferry crossing): This refers to the place where people and boats cross a river.
</div>

In this freezing weather, only a small boat was moored at the ferry point, and there wasn’t a boatman in sight.

Fan Changyu dismounted and, using her uninjured arm, hauled the young man onto the only boat at the crossing. Unfortunately, she didn’t know how to row. She could only brace a bamboo pole against the riverbed and push off from the bank, sending the boat several yards into the water—where it promptly stopped moving altogether.

When the bandits arrived, they didn’t care how cold the river was in the depths of winter. They plunged into it one after another, like dumplings dropped into a pot.

Fan Changyu used the bamboo pole to fend them off, but there were simply too many of them. Before long, several managed to seize an opening and reach the side of the boat.

After Xie Zheng destroyed Qingfeng Stronghold, he found no sign of Fan Changyu there. Having interrogated several bandits and learned that she had never actually been captured the previous night, he led his cavalry in hunting down the bandits who had escaped.

When they pursued them all the way to the ferry, he spotted a woman’s figure on the river in the distance. It looked strangely familiar.

As they drew closer, he realized it was indeed Fan Changyu!

The burning anxiety in his chest had not yet eased when he saw her desperately shielding a scholar on the boat—a young man utterly incapable of defending himself. Xie Zheng’s lips pressed into a thin, tight line.

His personal guards caught up with him. Seeing that Xie Zheng had reined in his horse, they glanced toward the man and woman by the river, who were being besieged by a crowd of bandits.

“**Houyé**, it seems those bandits are trying to seize the boat.”

Xie Zheng said coldly, “Bring me a bow.”

Yet his gaze, cold enough to freeze the air, remained fixed on the young man Fan Changyu was shielding behind her.
