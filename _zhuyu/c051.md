When the several Jizhou soldiers whose horses had been stolen finally caught up, they saw the man wearing a green ghost mask standing by the roadside, a long spear slung over his shoulder as he gazed down at the surging river below.
<div class="author-note">
For clarity, the author used different terms to describe XZ’s sword.

Mu Shi had injured one leg in his fall from the horse. Now he lay sprawled among the loose stones by the roadside, staring at the river and crying out through his tears, “**Shizi**!”

The Jizhou soldiers had no idea what had happened. They raised their weapons, eyeing the green-ghost-masked man with considerable wariness. Suddenly, the man turned around and gave them a detached glance. “The fugitive fled into the river. He has a wound around his waist and cannot swim far. Search for him downstream.”

With that, he mounted his horse, flicked the reins, and rode away. None of the soldiers dared step forward to stop him.

Only one sharp-eyed soldier recognized the horse beneath Xie Zheng. In a low voice, he said, “That’s Colonel Xu’s horse.”

Colonel Xu was the young officer whose horse Xie Zheng had stolen earlier.

The soldiers exchanged bewildered looks. Not one dared say a word. After a moment, their commanding officer finally ordered the injured Mu Shi bound. He divided the men into two groups: one to search for Sui Yuanqing downstream, and the other to escort Mu Shi back and report what had happened.

At the gates of Qingping County, the uprising among the commoners had been brought under control.

When He Jingyuan led his troops into the city, the County Magistrate had smeared two handfuls of blood across his face and rushed forward, wailing as though the heavens themselves had collapsed. “He **Daren**, thank heaven **you** have come! Otherwise, even if **Xia Guan**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="下官 — A humble self-reference used by a lower-ranking official when addressing a superior. Literally meaning “this lower official”."></sup> had laid my very bones across the city tower, One still couldn’t have stopped those rebellious commoners from entering the city…”

He Jingyuan remained seated on horseback. Seeing the magistrate covered in blood from head to toe, his expression softened despite his already unfavorable impression of the man. “The people of Qingping County have survived this calamity. Liu **Daren** deserves no small share of the credit.”

The moment County Magistrate Liu heard this, he felt certain that a promotion was within reach. His tears flowed even more freely as he said, “**Xia Guan** has served in Qingping County for three years, yet my record has been nothing remarkable. Now, just as One is about to be transferred, the military grain requisition drove the local people to the brink of rebellion. Terrified. One had no choice but to lead the yamen constables in blocking the city gates before the rioters could enter, even dared to bind the officers who had come to oversee the grain requisition, *an act of insubordination*, in order to quell the people’s fury. Only then did we manage to hold out until **Daren** arrived with reinforcements. One hopes **Daren** will not take offense.”
<div class="author-note">
以下犯上 (Yǐxià fànshàng) - Literally, “using those below to offend or defy those above.” The phrase describes violating the established hierarchy by challenging or acting against one’s superiors. The Magistrate uses it to emphasize how boldly he claims to have acted against the military officers, portraying himself as someone willing to risk punishment for the sake of the people.
</div>

He Jingyuan had already heard from the man in green ghost mask that the disturbance had been incited by the Shizi of Prince Changxin. Now, hearing Magistrate Liu mention the soldiers overseeing the grain requisition, he sensed that there was more to the matter than met the eye. He glanced at the magistrate and said, “What was the matter with those grain-requisition officers? Tell me everything.”

Magistrate Liu then truthfully recounted how the soldiers had arrived in the county several days earlier and demanded grain according to the quota of one shi per person.

He Jingyuan barked, “Foolishness! How could Jizhou Prefectural Office possibly have ordered grain to be requisitioned at one shi per person?”

Cold sweat poured down Magistrate Liu’s face. “That group of soldiers claimed they were acting on the orders of the Military Commissioner, Wei **Daren**. **Xiaoren**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. this small person"></sup>… **Xiaoren** would never have dared obstruct them. Later, they even placed me under house arrest… When **Xia Guan** learned that the farmers in the countryside had been driven to revolt, One feared this would lead to a great catastrophe. That was why **Xia Guan** ordered my men to bind those soldiers.”

Afraid that his own contribution would be overlooked, Magistrate Liu said nothing whatsoever about Head Constable Wang or Fan Changyu, offering only a vague account of what had happened.

He Jingyuan’s expression darkened, and Magistrate Liu’s heart began to climb into his throat once more.

From the magistrate’s account, He Jingyuan had already pieced together roughly seventy to eighty percent of the truth. Prince Changxin’s Shizi had led men to ambush and kill the soldiers who came to Qingping County to requisition grain, then impersonated Jizhou troops and produced a forged grain-requisition order. The attack on the villagers of Majia Village was likely another part of their scheme, intended to stir up the people of Qingping County and drive them into rebellion.

But the County Magistrate of Qingping still had no idea of those soldiers’ true identities. How, then, had that masked man recognized Sui Yuanqing?

Could it be that the masked man had known Sui Yuanqing all along?

He Jingyuan recalled his earlier suspicions, and his gaze grew even more complicated.

He asked the County Magistrate, “Earlier, I saw a black-clad man wearing a green ghost mask fighting with exceptional valor atop the city tower. Do you know who he is?”

Magistrate Liu waited anxiously, only to receive such a question. He shook his head, “This… **Xia Guan** does not know either. Perhaps he is simply a righteous man from the city.”

Just then, the soldiers who had pursued Sui Yuanqing and his men returned to the city.

The moment their commanding officer passed through the gates, he dismounted and cupped his fists toward He Jingyuan. “**Daren**, the rebel ringleader fled into the river. **Mojiang**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="A humble military self-reference used by a military officer when addressing a superior. Literally, “this subordinate general”."></sup> has already sent men to continue searching downstream, and brought this survivor back in chains to report to **Daren** first.”

He Jingyuan glanced at Mu Shi, who was bound tightly from head to toe, and asked, “Did you see a man wearing a green ghost mask?”

The junior commander cupped his fists and lowered his head. “This man was captured by that brave hero. When we arrived, he told us the rebel ringleader had fled into the river, then headed downstream himself. Judging by the look of things, he was searching for the ringleader as well.”

The young officer whose horse had been stolen could not help muttering, “Then where’s my horse?”

He Jingyuan shot him a look, and he hurriedly shut his mouth.

He Jingyuan turned to Mu Shi. “Have him taken into custody and kept under strict guard. Under no circumstances are you to let him take his own life.”

The young officer acknowledged the order.

He Jingyuan then pointed to the young officer who had just spoken. “Colonel Xu, take a detachment of men and search along the river for the fugitive. Capture him alive if at all possible.”

The young officer immediately straightened, cupped his fists, and said, “**Mojiang** receives the order!”

After seeing Head Constable Wang off to the physician, Fan Changyu noticed that it was already nearly dark, yet Xie Zheng still had not returned. Concern began to creep into her heart. After informing Head Constable Wang, she prepared to leave the city in search of him.

By then, the city gates had been taken over by soldiers from the Jizhou Prefectural Office. Clad in armor and armed to the teeth, they looked imposing enough that ordinary civilians kept their distance.

Fearing that accomplices of the rebels might still be hiding within the city, the gates were now being guarded with extreme strictness. Even some of the rural folk who regularly came into the city to trade had temporarily been detained.

Fan Changyu hesitated for a moment, but still decided to go over and explain the situation. She also intended to ask whether they had seen Yan Zheng while pursuing the enemy. After all, with the green ghost mask covering his face, he ought to have been rather easy to recognize.

She had just about to step forward when the unhurried sound of hoofbeats came from outside the city gates. The soldiers stationed there poked their heads out and saw a *chestnut horse*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="枣红 literally means “jujube red,” referring to a rich reddish-brown coat color in a horse."></sup> returning alone.

At the same moment, a large hand suddenly shot out from an oblique angle, seized Fan Changyu by the wrist, and pulled her several steps backward.

The soldiers who gathered at the gates looked outside. Seeing no rider, they stared in bewilderment. “Colonel Xu’s horse came back on its own?”

Several paces away, Fan Changyu saw the man who had removed his green ghost mask standing before her, dressed in a dark robe. Her shock immediately gave way to delight. As he led her along, she was so caught up in his return that she completely forgot he was still holding her hand, merely chattering on, “Why were you gone for so long? The soldiers have already captured someone and come back. I was so worried something had happened to you…”

Xie Zheng listened to her ceaseless muttering, but his grip around her wrist did not loosen in the slightest. “I went after that scoundrel. The chase took me farther than expected.”

Fan Changyu immediately realized he must be talking about that particularly cunning military officer who had led the others. She hurriedly asked, “Did you catch him?”

Xie Zheng shook his head.

He had searched for more than ten li<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="approx. 5 km"></sup> along the river, yet had not caught sight of Sui Yuanqing. The man had fled into the river still wearing that suit of armor, and Xie Zheng had already wounded him at the waist. No matter how skilled he was in the water, his chances of survival were slim.

If Sui Yuanqing had truly managed to escape with his life, then all one could say was that fate had not yet decreed his death.

Hearing that Sui Yuanqing had not been caught, Fan Changyu was somewhat disappointed. Then she said, “They say a turtle (Wangba) can live for a thousand years and a soft-shelled turtle for ten thousand. If that little Wangba somehow managed not to die, I suppose he’s living up to the saying.”
<div class="author-note">
  <p>千年王八万年鳖 — “A turtle can live for a thousand years and a soft-shelled turtle for ten thousand”.</p>
  <p>This is a humorous saying about turtles and soft-shelled turtles supposedly having extraordinarily long lifespans. In everyday usage, 王八 (wangba) can also be an insulting term, functioning as “bastard” or “son of a bitch.”</p>
</div> 

Xie Zheng had yet to hear Fan Changyu say a single good word about Sui Yuanqing. Recalling what Sui Yuanqing had provocatively said before fleeing into the river, his gaze darkened slightly. “Do you have a grudge against him?”

Fan Changyu said, “I didn’t before. I heard you say the County Magistrate had been taken under control, so I wanted to go drag him out and get Wang **Shu**’s position as the head constable restored first. It would make things easier for Wang **Shu** to handle matters. Who would’ve thought that little Wangba was staying at the magistrate’s residence? I had no choice but to seize that Wangba while I was at it. So now we have a grudge.”

Xie Zheng lowered his eyes, concealing the emotions in them. “He’s quite skilled. How did you manage to tie him up?”

Fan Changyu felt a little embarrassed at the question. She felt that she had won through somewhat underhanded means. But she was an exceedingly straightforward person, so she told him everything honestly, “There were too many of them, and I was afraid I couldn’t beat them. I wanted to use some knockout powder to drug them, but there wasn’t any at the magistrate’s residence. So I disguised myself as one of the magistrate’s maids and brought that little Wangba a bowl of tremella soup laced with croton seeds.”

She was still wearing that same maid’s outfit, the sleeve leaving half of her fair wrist exposed beneath Xie Zheng’s hand.

Xie Zheng lowered his eyes to look at her. Thinking of how she had gone to deliver that soup to Sui Yuanqing while dressed in these very clothes, the grip around her wrist unconsciously tightened.

The pain in her wrist finally reminded Fan Changyu that Xie Zheng was still holding it.

She patted his hand and hissed, “Easy. That bastard<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="瘪犊子 - literally shrivelled calf"></sup> bullied me up on the city tower when I didn’t have a proper weapon. He came at me with a ring-pommel saber while I only had my boning knife, and later he dragged me over the edge of the tower as he fell. This wrist got hurt too, and it’s still aching.”

Xie Zheng released her. When he lowered his eyes, he saw a ring of bruised fingerprints around her frost-pale wrist, clearly not made by him. There were cracks across the webbing between her thumb and forefinger as well, the blood that had seeped from them already dried.

A flash of ferocity crossed his eyes.

Seeing him remain silent, Fan Changyu realized that what she had just said sounded rather like she was complaining. It felt a little too fussy, so she quickly added, “But I got my revenge, too. I stabbed him several times. And before that bastard ran off, I even kicked him in the face!”

Xie Zheng still said nothing as he listened to her recount it all, his eyes cold and dark.

Fan Changyu only felt that he had been unusually quiet throughout their journey. She guessed he was probably regretting that he had failed to catch that bastard, and spent several moments reassuring him.

Before returning to town, Fan Changyu first went to tell Head Constable Wang that they had returned safely, lest he worry when Yan Zheng failed to come back.

When Head Constable Wang learned that they intended to return to town, he said, “It’s already dark, and the snow is coming down heavily. After what happened today, there are bound to be bandits taking advantage of the chaos to rob people along the road. It isn’t safe to travel in the dark. We have an empty room at home. Stay here for the night and return tomorrow. There’s no need to rush.”

Fan Changyu thought about it. She and Yan Zheng had both been exhausted after such a long day, so she thanked him and agreed.

When Yu Bao’er saw Fan Changyu arrive, he came running out on his little legs and asked her, “Changyu **Gugu**, when is my mother coming to get me?”
<div class="author-note">
姑姑 (Gūgū) literally means “paternal aunt”, but it can also be used by children as a familiar form of address for an adult woman of the appropriate generation, even when she is not their actual aunt.
</div>

Only then did Fan Changyu remember Yu Qianqian. She looked up at Xie Zheng. “Is Yu **Zhanggui**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="manager/shopkeeper"></sup> still in the jail?”

Xie Zheng leaned against the chuihua gate with his arms folded and gave a languid shake of his head. His casual gaze settled on Yu Bao’er, with a trace of complicated emotion hidden within it. After a moment, he looked away and said, “The death at Yixiang Restaurant has yet to be resolved. I don’t know what disposition the authorities will make. Since she entrusted this little brat to you, you can look after him for her until the case is settled.”

Fan Changyu thought that Yu Qianqian had always treated her well, so helping look after Yu Bao’er for a while was only right.

Earlier, she and Yu Qianqian had discussed the matter and assumed that the county magistrate wanted to seize Yu Qianqian’s property and use it to curry favor with the rebel prince. Now, however, it appeared that was not the case.

If the authorities handled the case impartially and cleared Yu Qianqian’s name, then everyone would be happy.

But if the county magistrate was harboring some ulterior motive, Fan Changyu already had evidence of him taking credit for the grain requisition. She would not have to fear him making things difficult for Yu Qianqian.

Fan Changyu rubbed Yu Bao’er on the back of his head and said, “Your mother has run into a little trouble. Once she sorts it out, she’ll come pick you up. For now, come with me to town and play with Ningniang for a few days, all right?”

Back when Yu Qianqian’s business had been busy, she would often leave Yu Bao’er in the care of the stewardess. Sometimes, Yu Bao’er would go three or five days without seeing his mother.

Though he was still young, he was already an unusually steady child. Upon hearing this, he obediently nodded and asked curiously, “So, Changyu **Gugu** can slaughter pigs?”

Fan Changyu thought for a moment before saying, “Probably.”

The events of the day had left everyone in the county town on edge. It was still uncertain whether the market would even open over the next couple of days. It would probably take some time before the market returned to its usual bustle.

Yu Bao’er completely disregarded the word “probably”. Satisfied with Fan Changyu’s answer, he obediently let an older maidservant take him away to wash up and go to bed.

Fan Changyu had come to Yixiang Restaurant early that morning to deliver meat to Yu Qianqian, and she had not had even a sip of water since.

Wang Furen knew she must be starving, so she had one older maidservant prepare a meal in the kitchen.

Fan Changyu had been too busy all afternoon to even think about whether she was hungry. It was only when the smell of food reached her that she realized she was so hungry her stomach was practically stuck to her back.

She had spent the entire day doing strenuous physical work. With her stomach completely empty, she ate three bowls of rice in a row. When she reached for a fourth, Xie Zheng pressed down on her rice scoop.

He said, “If you’ve been hungry for a long time, don’t eat until you’re too full all at once. It’ll upset your spleen and stomach.”

Fan Changyu reluctantly set down her bowl and chopsticks.

After the meal, Xie Zheng went out for a while. Head Constable Wang had spent years handling cases, bumps and injuries were inevitable, so his household kept quite a stock of medicinal remedies for treating wounds.

Xie Zheng asked Wang Furen for some ointment for bruises and injuries, as well as a bottle of *golden-wound medicine*.
<div class="author-note">
金创药 — A traditional medicine used for treating wounds, especially cuts and other injuries caused by weapons.
</div>

By the time he returned to the room, Fan Changyu had just finished washing up.

He saw her wringing out a cloth in the basin, and his brows furrowed. “Has no one ever told you that you shouldn’t get your wounds wet?”

Fan Changyu glanced at the wounds on her hands and said unconcernedly, “It’s just a little injury. It’s nothing.”

Then she turned and spotted the medicine in Xie Zheng’s hand. “Oh?” she said. “You went to get medicine for me?”

Xie Zheng lowered his eyes slightly and said flatly, “Wang **Furen** gave it to me.”

Fan Changyu did not suspect a thing. “**Shenzi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="auntie"></sup> really is thoughtful. She even noticed such a tiny injury of mine.”

Xie Zheng said nothing. Leaning against the doorframe, he asked, “Are you going to put it on or not?”

Fan Changyu thought to herself that this man’s temper really did change from one moment to the next. But remembering that he had rescued her several times on the city tower, she decided not to hold it against him. Tilting her chin up, she said, “Of course I am. Why wouldn’t I? This is medicine **Shenzi** went out of her way to get for me, after all. It’s the gesture of her *goodwill*.”

At the words "goodwill", Xie Zheng raised his eyes and glanced at her before looking away again.
<div class="author-note">
Here, the word for is 心意 (xīnyì) which means someone's feelings, intentions, or the thoughtfulness behind something they do for another person. It can also refer to feelings towards someone.
</div>

Fan Changyu first sprinkled the golden wound medicine over the cut between her thumb and forefinger. When Xie Zheng saw her struggling to bandage it while biting down on one end of the gauze, he walked over, finished wrapping it for her, and tied it securely.

But when it came time to apply the ointment to her wrist, Fan Changyu realized she had done something foolish.

She should have applied the ointment to her wrist first. The ointment was oil-based and had to be spread little by little and rubbed into the skin. Now that both her hands were wrapped in gauze, she could only scoop up a little with her fingertips and slowly work it in with her finger pad, making the whole process quite troublesome.

Moreover, the oil-based ointment was extremely slippery. Rubbing it in with the pads of her fingers made it difficult to work into the skin.

Fan Changyu rubbed it halfheartedly a few times and was ready to be done. But just as she was about to close the ointment jar, a large hand intercepted her wrist.

Xie Zheng’s broad palm, callused with a thin layer of roughness, rubbed the undried medicinal oil into her wrist. His tone could hardly be called polite. “Is everything you do this halfhearted?”

Fan Changyu had been rebuked by him again, and she could not resist talking back. “Isn’t it because it’s inconvenient with both my hands like this?”

Xie Zheng seemed to pause for a moment. After that, he simply concentrated on spreading and rubbing the medicinal oil into her wrist, without saying another word.

Her frost-pale skin took on the warm luster of jade beneath the candlelight, while the ring of bluish fingerprints around her wrist stood out even more starkly. They looked almost alarming.

All at once, the provocative smile Sui Yuanqing had thrown at him after breaking through the crowd surfaced in Xie Zheng’s mind. A surge of inexplicable anger rose within him, and his thin lips pressed into a hard line.

A layer of medicinal oil lay between his palm and her wrist. While the oil was still wet, his hand moved against her skin with nothing but a slick, slippery sensation. But as the oil was worked into her skin, the feeling of his fingers kneading her wrist became increasingly distinct.

Perhaps from rubbing for too long, his palm had grown scorching hot, like an iron fresh from the fire.

Fan Changyu furrowed her brows and was just about to say that was enough when he withdrew his hand before she could speak.

The words at the tip of Fan Changyu’s tongue were forced back down.

Xie Zheng put away the ointment jar and went over to the washstand to clean his hands.

Fan Changyu lowered her eyes to her wrist, which had been rubbed red. Her entire wrist felt hot and itchy. She had to contort her face to resist the urge to rub it against her clothes.

She thought to herself that if she had known the ointment’s effects would leave her whole hand numb and itchy, she would never have bothered applying it. She might as well have waited until she got home and used medicinal wine instead.

When Xie Zheng turned around, he saw the conflicted expression on her face and asked, “What’s wrong?”

Fan Changyu shook her wrist. “The medicine’s taking effect. I’m just not used to it.”

Someone passed along the street, striking a wooden clapper. It was already the Zi hour<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="between 11 p.m. to 1 a.m."></sup>, and the entire Wang residence had fallen silent.
<div class="author-note">
梆子 — A wooden clapper struck by night watchmen while making their rounds, traditionally used to mark the passing of nighttime hours and signal that all was quiet.
</div>

Without needing Fan Changyu to say anything else, Xie Zheng went over and opened the cabinet in the room, only to find that there were no spare quilts.

Fan Changyu, sitting at the table, noticed it too.

At this hour, they could hardly wake Wang Furen and ask her for a quilt just so they could spread it on the floor.

After a moment, Xie Zheng turned back and said, “I’m not tired yet. You get some rest.”

Fan Changyu thought to herself that he might as well be trying to fool a ghost. He had barely slept properly for several nights in a row, and that very morning, he had forced himself to help her sell pork.

Besides, in the dead of winter, without even a brazier of charcoal, it would be cold enough to freeze a person to death. Was he really planning to sit in the room all night?

Fan Changyu glanced at the single thick quilt left on the bed and took the initiative. “How about… we sleep on the bed together and make do for one night?”

Xie Zheng’s heart gave a jolt. He turned to look at her, his handsome brows drawing together. Misinterpreting his reaction, Fan Changyu hurriedly raised her gauze-wrapped hand in reassurance. “Don’t worry. I absolutely have no improper intentions toward you!”
