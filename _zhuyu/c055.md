The bruising at the corners of Xie Zheng’s eyes had faded considerably. Leaning against the daybed, he sat with his eyelids lowered, lost in thought. Even Gongsun Yin entering the tent failed to draw his attention. It was only after Gongsun Yin’s teasing tone that Xie Zheng finally lifted his eyes. “If you’re too idle, go oversee the fighting in Jinzhou.”

Gongsun Yin found himself a place to sit, poured a cup of hot tea, and drank it as he said, “How am I idle? You sent Xueluan to deliver a letter, and I immediately brought the troops from Yanzhou all the way here to Lucheng, traveling several hundred li. I worked myself to the bone, only to get a line like that from you. Truly, my heart is broken.”
<div class="author-note">
雪鸾 (Xuě luán) is the name of the Gyrfalcon. 雪 means snow, and 鸾 refers to the luan, a mythical auspicious bird related to phoenix.
</div>

Xie Zheng was in a foul mood, his tongue was even more merciless than usual, “Giving that stupid beast such a name, are you not even embarrassed by how pretentious it sounds.? Since you’ve made yourself into a *beast of burden*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="牛马 (niúmǎ) literally means “oxen and horses”, referring to beasts of burden or animals used for labour."></sup>, I suppose you won’t be needing a carriage or horses for the journey back from Lucheng to Yanzhou either. You can walk.”

Gongsun Yin was momentarily speechless. Everyone said he had a sharp tongue, but that tongue had never managed to gain the upper hand against Xie Zheng.

Unable to resist, he said, “Why are you so irritable? If you don’t want Xueluan, then give him to me to raise!”

At the last sentence, his fox-like eyes could not help sliding toward the Gyrfalcon in the corner of the main tent.

For some reason, the Gyrfalcon had taken a liking to using a bamboo basket as its nest. The basket had originally been meant for holding dirty clothes, but the moment the Gyrfalcon spotted it, it simply climbed inside and claimed it as its nest.

Xie Zheng raised his eyes indifferently, “You really didn’t realize that I was saying I don’t like the name you gave him?”

Furious, Gongsun Yin flung his sleeve and turned to leave, “Xie Jiuheng, don’t push people too far!”

Xie Zheng let him storm away.

Gongsun Yin reached the entrance of the main tent, only to suddenly turn back. He sat down again and said, “I nearly fell for your trick. You goaded me into leaving. Whoever managed to injure you and put you in such a foul mood…”

His fox-like eyes narrowed, “I heard that the Shizi of Prince Changxin went to Qingping County some time ago. You were lured into a trap and defeated on the Chongzhou battlefield by him, and the whole army says he’s peerless in valor. Could that injury on your face have been caused by him?”

Xie Zheng gave a cold scoff. “You certainly think highly of him.”

The moment Gongsun Yin heard that, he knew it absolutely could not have been Sui Yuanqing.

He frowned. “Surely you didn’t incur some *romantic debt* and get beaten by a woman…”
<div class="author-note">
桃花债 literally means “peach-blossom debt”. It refers to romantic entanglements, especially troublesome ones.
</div>

The moment the words left his mouth, he burst out laughing himself. “That’s absolutely impossible. Never mind that you, Xie Jiuheng, were never blessed with the fate for romantic entanglements—even if you were, there isn’t a young lady in the world with the strength to hit you that hard.”

Xie Zheng’s expression stiffened slightly, and he spoke impatiently. “Did you come all this way just to tell me this?”

Seeing his displeasure, Gongsun Yin put away his teasing manner and said, “Of course there’s serious business to discuss as well. When the troops from Yanzhou went to meet that merchant surnamed Zhao to escort the grain, they didn’t leave the slightest trace behind. Instead, it was that merchant surnamed Zhao who left a loose end in Jizhou, which was how He Jingyuan traced the trail back to Yanzhou.

“When I sent men to raid the Zhao family’s hidden outposts, I deliberately let word slip, giving them enough warning to move their people beforehand. That was how we managed to find the even deeper hideouts they had concealed. After thoroughly investigating them, we truly uncovered quite a few interesting things.”

He gave a mysterious smile. Only when he saw Xie Zheng look over did he continue, “The Zhao family also has dealings with Prince Changxin’s side.”

Xie Zheng’s expression remained unchanged, “When Zhao Xun met with me, he repeatedly hinted that he was one of the people connected to the Imperial Grandson who supposedly perished in the fire more than a decade ago. With Prince Changxin in rebellion, it would hardly be surprising if the people behind Zhao Xun had some sort of bargain with Prince Changxin.”

At the mention of the Imperial Grandson, Gongsun Yin’s expression shifted slightly. He asked Xie Zheng, “How does **Houyé** see it?”

He addressed him as **Houyé**, rather than “you”. He was therefore not asking as a friend, but as a strategist asking his lord where he intended to stand next.

Xie Zheng said, “After the Battle of Chongzhou, Wei Yan and I are already as incompatible as fire and water.”

Gongsun Yin pondered for a moment before saying, “Prince Changxin is certainly no benevolent man either. If the Imperial Grandson truly still lives, who knows what sort of bargain he has struck with Prince Changxin.”

Xie Zheng sat with one leg half-bent, his long hair tied up, dark brows sweeping into his temples. His tone was casual yet cutting, “If the Imperial Grandson has dealings with Prince Changxin, yet Prince Changxin did not use the Imperial Grandson’s name when he rebelled, then either this so-called Imperial Grandson is nothing more than a smokescreen Prince Changxin put out, or…”

He paused.

“…Prince Changxin is simply too ambitious to remain beneath another man. Once he had gathered enough strength to rise, he was unwilling to submit to anyone else.”

Gongsun Yin was startled by the possibility Xie Zheng had raised.  “Even if Prince Changxin was unwilling to remain subordinate to another and used the Imperial Grandson’s name to justify his rebellion, that would still make his cause more legitimate. Once the realm is settled and he holds overwhelming power in his hands, whether the Imperial Grandson gets to sit upon that Dragon Throne would be entirely up to him. In that case, the matter of the Imperial Grandson really does look like a scheme devised by Prince Changxin.”

His brows drew tightly together, “But Wei Yan seems to have been investigating the Imperial Grandson’s whereabouts as well. These past few days, he’s already arrested quite a number of people based on nothing more than rumors and scraps of clues. Even you… when you reopened the investigation into that Jinzhou case from years ago, it was enough to make him set his heart on killing you. From the looks of it, the news that the Imperial Grandson is still alive doesn’t seem to be false either. Otherwise, why would Wei Yan be so alarmed?”

Xie Zheng thought back to what Zhao Xun had said that day. Zhao Xun had hinted that he was one of the Imperial Grandson’s people, yet he had known absolutely nothing about what had truly happened in the Jinzhou tragedy seventeen years ago. Even his claim that the Imperial Grandson had survived the fire in the Eastern Palace was merely his own word, with no evidence whatsoever to prove it.

It was precisely because he had found the matter rather suspicious that he had asked the person behind Zhao Xun to come and speak with him in person. But as the Zhao family’s businesses were sealed one by one by the authorities, that matter had been shelved as well.

He asked, “Before the authorities sealed the Zhao family’s outposts in Qingping County, did the men you sent discover anything?”

Gongsun Yin said, “Those outposts in Qingping County were all temporary. The shops, restaurants, and such had been purchased by the Zhao family less than two months ago. There was pitifully little to be found.”

Xie Zheng’s knuckles, which had been tapping against the tabletop, paused briefly. “When the authorities sealed the Zhao family’s shops, I had only been staying in Qingping County for a month. Clearly, those outposts weren’t established for my sake.”

Gongsun Yin also felt the matter growing increasingly shrouded in fog. “You mean that something must have happened in Qingping County during the month before your downfall, something that prompted the Zhao family to establish so many outposts there?”

What had happened in Qingping County during the month before his downfall…

A sharp light flashed in Xie Zheng’s eyes.

There was only one answer: the deaths of the Fan husband and wife.

Wei Yan had sent men to kill the husband and wife, then dispatched death soldiers to the Fan household several times in search of something. Anyone keeping an eye on Wei Yan’s movements could hardly have failed to notice such commotion.

So… the Zhao family had established those outposts for the sake of the Fan family?

Zhao Xun had not sought him out because he had read the model essays he wrote, developed admiration for him, and happened to discover his identity. Rather, the Zhao family had been keeping tabs on the Fan family all along—and had consequently stumbled upon his whereabouts.

Something was on the verge of revealing itself.

Xie Zheng spoke slowly, “It seems Wei Yan was played by Prince Changxin as well.”

Gongsun Yin was a man of exceptional clever. The moment Xie Zheng explained it, he immediately understood what he meant, “You’re saying Prince Changxin deliberately spread rumors about the Imperial Grandson and the Battle of Jinzhou all those years ago, hoping to throw Wei Yan into disarray?”

Xie Zheng voiced his own conjecture, “The Imperial Grandson may truly exist, or he may merely be a smokescreen. But Prince Changxin must know something about what happened back then. Lacking evidence, he deliberately spread these rumors to make Wei Yan believe he had failed to clean up every last loose end from those years ago. Then, when Wei Yan turned back to *cut the grass and remove its roots*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="idiom meaning to eliminate a threat completely so it cannot grow back."></sup>, he would inadvertently expose the evidence.”

Even Gongsun Yin was momentarily stunned. His brows drew tightly together. “If there is no Imperial Grandson at all, then that man surnamed Zhao purchasing 200,000 shi of grain for you was, from the very beginning, meant to lure Wei Xuan into requisitioning grain, forcing the people of Qingping County to rebel and join forces with those inside the city to attack Lucheng?”

He rose and paced back and forth inside the military tent. “And it just so happens that Beijue has begun attacking Jinzhou. With those 200,000 shi of grain, it would be no easy task for them to take Jinzhou. Because if Jinzhou falls, the northwest gateway will be wide open, and Beijue will drive straight south. A scenario that would spell disaster even for Prince Changxin. Those 200,000 shi of grain of his truly accomplish three things with one stroke!

“Wei Yan wants you dead, Prince Changxin controls the grain route through Chongzhou, and you hold back Beijue while Prince Changxin’s army continues south. Once you reach the end of your strength, you will either starve to death in Jinzhou, just as your father and Crown Prince Chengde did all those years ago, or be forced to ally yourself with Prince Changxin and surrender your military authority.”

When Gongsun Yin sat down again, his long brows remained tightly furrowed. “What an extraordinarily meticulous and vicious plan!”

Xie Zheng, on the other hand, looked rather unbothered, as though he had not taken the dead end Gongsun Yin described to heart. “I thought you would advise me to ally myself with Prince Changxin.”

Gongsun Yin’s expression darkened. “Don’t lump me together with those *useless gluttons*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="“酒囊饭袋” — Literally “wine sacks and rice bags,” a contemptuous term for people who are good for nothing except eating and drinking."></sup>. Never mind that you’re Wei Yan’s own *Wai nephew*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="the son of a sister"></sup>; the mere fact that you command a powerful army is enough to ensure that even if you went to Prince Changxin and pledged allegiance, he wouldn’t dare use you. In this world, no one can sleep soundly with a sharp sword hanging over their head.”

He was the most powerful blade in all the realm, its edge unmatched.

Wei Yan had been forced by circumstances to forge such a weapon, quite by accident. Yet the moment he heard the slightest stirrings of trouble, he had already resolved to break it.

If even a man who held power over the entire court feared him to such an extent, how could Prince Changxin possibly dare take up this blade?

If Xie Zheng were to rebel, it remained uncertain which of the two—he or Prince Changxin—would ultimately rule the realm. Prince Changxin was far too shrewd to leave such a tremendous hidden threat at his side.

Still, Xie Zheng’s words had given Gongsun Yin an idea. His fox-like eyes narrowed. “Does **Houyé** desire this realm?”

Xie Zheng gave a derisive scoff, “I want the truth behind the Battle of Jinzhou seventeen years ago.”

Those words brought a smile to Gongsun Yin’s face, “Now that is the Xie Jiuheng I know.”

Gongsun Yin straightened the hem of his robe and took his leave. When he reached the entrance of the main tent, he could not resist turning back, “I’m still rather curious, though. Just who was it that hit you?”

There were no other injuries on Xie Zheng’s body. For someone to have beaten his face into such a state and left the rest of him untouched was truly difficult to fathom.

When Xie Zheng’s gaze swept toward him, cold as a blade of ice, Gongsun Yin hurriedly flicked aside the tent flap and left.

Xie Zheng closed his eyes. The feelings he had deliberately ignored came surging back for no apparent reason, stirred up by just a few words from Gongsun Yin.

Perhaps he had experienced too few setbacks in this life. Alongside the bitterness, an inexplicable sense of resentment rose within him.

Never mind the noble ladies of the capital—even a princess could be brought home as his wife if he so desired. Yet that woman alone had nearly dismissed him as if he was nothing more than a weed growing by the roadside.

His chest felt heavy, tinged with irritation.

Just then, the tent flap was lifted. Xie Zheng glanced up impatiently, only to see Gongsun Yin returning with a large bundle in his hands. It looked somewhat familiar.

Meeting his unfriendly gaze, Gongsun Yin shrugged and said, “A colonel from the Jizhou army camp sent this over. Apparently, a carpenter cured his *rheumatism*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="风湿 (literally wind-dampness/fēngshī). It is often associated with joint or muscle pain."></sup> with a few applications of *gaoyao*. Grateful for the carpenter’s kindness, he decided to help the man find his niece’s husband. As it happens, that carpenter’s niece’s husband is named Yan Zheng. And of the thousand men I requested from He Jingyuan, you’re the only one using the alias Yan Zheng. I figured, well, surely he must be looking for you.”
<div class="author-note">
膏药 (gāoyao) refers to traditional Chinese medicinal plasters: sticky herbal preparations applied to the skin.
</div>

As he spoke, he couldn’t suppress the teasing glint in his fox-like eyes. “Niece’s husband? So you’re married?”

When Xie Zheng saw the bundle, he paused for a moment. Then his lips pressed into a thin line, and his expression turned somewhat cold, “Put it in the rear tent.”

At this, Gongsun Yin’s fox-like eyes widened in astonishment. He looked almost foolish, “Wait, you really are married?”

Xie Zheng pressed his lips tightly together and said nothing.

He indeed was married, but the marriage was a sham.

Seeing his reaction, Gongsun Yin frowned. Then, as though he had suddenly thought of something, he stared at Xie Zheng in disbelief, “Don’t tell me you’re planning to abandon her after having your way with her?”

His gaze fell upon Xie Zheng’s face, and his suspicion only seemed to grow, “Could that injury on your face be because you wronged the young lady and her family beat you up?”

Xie Zheng’s expression darkened, “Shut up.”

He was the one who had been cast aside like a worn-out shoe.

Gongsun Yin, however, had already begun to feel indignant on Fan Changyu’s behalf. He looked at Xie Zheng as though his heart were breaking. “Jiuheng ah, Jiuheng. I never imagined you were this kind of man…”

Xie Zheng impatiently raised his eyes, “Are you going to leave on your own, or should I have someone throw you out?”

Gongsun Yin frowned and adopted a solemn expression, “Jiuheng, even if her status is too low for her to be your principal wife, she did stick by you when you were at the end of your road. At the very least, you could bring her back and give her the position of a concubine. How could you be so heartless…”

Xie Zheng remained silent for a long while before finally saying, “She’s the one who doesn’t want to be with me.”

Gongsun Yin’s expression instantly became indescribably strange.
