Fan Changyu was stunned.

It was only when a sharp sting spread across her lips that she realized what had happened. Feeling both flustered and furious, she swung her other hand at his face. But as though he had been prepared for it, he caught her wrist with ease and yanked her harder against him, his solid chest and iron arms locking her in place.

Fan Changyu had never been treated this way before. She struggled with all her strength, but he effortlessly turned aside every attempt.

Driven to desperation, she decided to put all her strength into her teeth instead. When she bit down, Xie Zheng hissed softly. By the time they parted, there was blood on his lips. He frowned. “You—”

Before he could finish, Fan Changyu slammed her forehead into him, striking his nose. A sharp, throbbing ache shot through the root of his nose, and he had no choice but to pull one hand away to cover it. In the next instant, Fan Changyu’s freed hand landed a vicious punch at the corner of his eye.

Xie Zheng winced in pain but did not release the hand he still held. With a powerful tug, he wrenched both her arms behind her back and pinned them against the wall. He pressed his body firmly against her back, his voice turning cold, “Are you really that aggrieved<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="this could also mean “you feel that wronged?”"></sup>”

Fan Changyu was so furious she could have bitten him to death. With her wrist still injured, she found herself unable to break free of his hold for the moment.

“What the hell is wrong with you?” she cursed. “If you want to find a woman, there are plenty in the pleasure houses willing to do business with you. What kind of person do you take me for?”

Xie Zheng abruptly lifted his head, his dark eyes deep and shadowed, “That’s what you think of me?”

Pinned against the wall and unable to move, Fan Changyu’s eyes nearly sparked with fury and humiliation. “What did you think you were doing just now? Taking advantage of me when I was vulnerable!”

Xie Zheng seemed to have been driven to the brink of anger. To her surprise, he let out a low laugh. “Taking advantage of you? If I truly intended to take advantage of you, I wouldn't have waited until now.”

He released her and took a step back, the curve of his lips cold and mocking, “You really can’t let go of that former fiancé of yours? When you look for someone else in the future, you insist on finding a man who resembles him? Haven’t you learned a thing?”

Fan Changyu had only just been subjected to his unwelcome advances. Hearing him speak in that sarcastic, reproachful tone now made her even angrier. Before she could think better of it, she swung another punch straight at his face. “Whether I can let go of him or not, what does it have to do with you?”

Xie Zheng neither dodged nor flinched. He took the full force of her punch, his lip splitting from the impact. The flush spreading across one side of his face lent an unexpectedly striking beauty to his jade-like features.

Fan Changyu herself  was stunned after throwing the punch. She knew exactly how hard she had hit him—she was the one who had thrown it.

Why... hadn't he dodged?

Xie Zheng ran the tip of his tongue over the split corner of his mouth. After tasting the faint metallic tang of blood, he turned his head toward Fan Changyu, “Not continuing?”

Fan Changyu couldn't quite put into words what she was feeling. Her knuckles still ached faintly, and his face had surely fared even worse.

But after what he had done to her, she couldn't bring herself to apologize. Pressing her lips tightly together, she turned and headed inside.

She hadn't expected the man barely a step away to suddenly close the distance like a specter.

Fan Changyu only caught sight of those startlingly dark eyes before his hand clamped around the back of her head and he kissed her again.

Her scalp prickled with shock. Having lost the initiative, she found herself at a disadvantage at every turn. Amid the struggle, her entire body was pressed against the wall. He seized both her hands and raised them above her head, using his larger frame to pin her down. As he lowered his head, his breath—far heavier and more heated than its usual, restrained cadence—washed across her face.

This kiss was even wilder and more brutal than the last.

Fan Changyu, furious, bit him hard. He quickly clamped a hand around her jaw, using some deft maneuver she couldn't quite identify to keep her from biting down again. Yet he showed no intention of pulling away. Instead, he took advantage of the opening to force his way past her teeth, sweeping across the inside of her mouth several times.

By the time it ended, Fan Changyu was gasping for breath. Her mind briefly starved of air, she actually forgot to punch him again. She could only stare at him in disbelief.

Xie Zheng released her and wiped the blood from his lips with his index finger. “Now **this** is taking advantage of you.”

The fury of being violated and subjected to such indignity surged straight to Fan Changyu's head. As soon as Xie Zheng released his hold on her limbs and stepped back, she drew the bone-cleaving knife she carried at her side and pressed it against his throat. “Who do you think you are? You think you can humiliate me whenever you please?”

Xie Zheng leaned against a wooden pillar, the blade at his neck provoking not the slightest change in his expression. Only when he heard Fan Changyu's words did he raise his eyes, his expression turning unusually serious. “Rather than continuing to choose poorly and finding another ungrateful wretch in the future, you might as well come with me.”

The words left not only Fan Changyu, but Xie Zheng himself, momentarily stunned.

Then, once again, came that numb, intoxicating thrill of having forcibly shattered another piece of his own reason.

Yes. Rather than let her marry someone else in the future, wouldn't it be better to keep her by his side?

Having said that much, the words that followed seemed to come more easily. After a moment of silence, he slowly said, “I have a formidable enemy out there. I may die by his hand—or he may die while I survive. If you're willing, wait for me for two years. If I die, someone will come and deliver the news to you. You can marry someone else then; it won't be too late.”

Fan Changyu stared at him coldly, “You keep calling Song Yan an ungrateful wretch. How much better are you? You make improper advances toward me, then turn around and tell me you're interested in me?”

She lowered the knife. Her anger at being violated had temporarily overwhelmed everything else. Raising her sleeve, she roughly wiped her lips. “I hit you, so I suppose we're even. Your things are all on the table. Once the curfew at the city gates is lifted, you should leave.”

Xie Zheng watched her retreating figure as she went back inside. Not even the slightest trace of that cold, sardonic curve could lift at the corners of his mouth anymore.

So... he'd been rejected?

The man who had tasted defeat only once in his life—on the battlefield of Chongzhou—had now tasted it again, in an entirely different place.

He left the things on the table in the main room untouched. Instead, he leaned against a veranda pillar for a while before leaving the Fan family courtyard.

Because of the unrest in Qingping County a few days earlier, when rebellious commoners had surrounded the city and caused a disturbance, the authorities had imposed another security lockdown. The streets of Lin'an Town were bleak and deserted, with hardly any country folk coming into town for market day.

Xie Zheng wandered aimlessly until he reached the pine woods along the river outside town. The ground beneath lay more than a foot of accumulated snow. The river, fed by the undulating terrain, ran swift and turbulent. The thin sheet of ice that had formed across its surface just the night before had already broken apart. All that could be heard was the tinkling of mountain springs halfway up the slopes.

He lay down in the snow on a gentle incline, resting one arm behind his head as he stared blankly at the distant outline of Lin'an Town.

He hadn't panicked when his life had hung by a thread after being ambushed on the Chongzhou battlefield. He hadn't felt fear when he had narrowly escaped death and been pursued by death warriors for over a hundred li<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="1 li ≈ 0.5 km"></sup>.

When he'd fallen from the cliff and been carried by the river currents to Jizhou, he'd awoken on the riverbank. Enduring sword wounds all over his body, along with chills and a raging fever, he'd gone searching for a village, only to collapse in the wilderness and be brought home by that woman.

Back then, all he'd planned was how to stabilize the situation in the northwest, then gradually exact his revenge on the Wei father and son.

When had he begun to find himself unable to bear leaving?

That little house was always noisy and full of the warmth of everyday life. He had seen too many backs bent beneath the weight of suffering, but that woman—even if the sky itself came crashing down—would straighten her slender back and shoulder the burden.

Perhaps... it was simply that no one had treated him with such unreserved kindness in far too long?

The dried tangerine-peel candies she gave him when he took his medicine, the red envelopes from New Year's...

A self-mocking smile crept across Xie Zheng's lips. For a brief moment, the phrase “wagging his tail and begging for pity” came to mind.
<div class="author-note">
摇尾乞怜 (Yáowěiqǐlián) - An idiom describing someone acting servilely or ingratiating themselves to win another person's favor, like a dog wagging its tail to beg for affection or mercy.
</div>

She was probably simply too kindhearted. Even if it hadn’t been him who was rescued that day, but anyone else, she would have cared for them just as wholeheartedly—buying them sweets, preparing a New Year red envelope…

It was because she pitied him that she treated him well. It wasn’t because she had any feelings for him.

His remark—*come with him*—had truly become a joke.

A man who had spent half his life proud and unyielding was not particularly willing to admit to such a laughable defeat.

High in the sky, the Gyrfalcon circled overhead, crying out as though searching for someone.

This time, Xie Zheng did not blow his whistle for a long while. He turned his head slightly and saw that along the riverbank, where much of the snow near the water had already melted, a tender green blade of grass had pushed through the snow. Fresh and vibrant, it stood alone amid the expanse of white.

When ice melts, the springs begin to stir; 
when snow fades, the grass sprouts anew.

This was the New Year couplet he had written for her.

He watched it for a while before lowering his eyes and sitting halfway up. He plucked the tender shoot from the ground and tossed it into the swift current, silently watching as the river carried it away.

If it has taken root in his heart and unsettled him, then he need only uproot it.

At last, the Gyrfalcon circling overhead spotted him too. When it swooped down, Xie Zheng did not raise his arm to receive it. The Gyrfalcon stood there for a while. When Xie Zheng showed no sign of taking its message, it tilted its head at him, then walked closer and gently pecked the back of his hand with its beak.

Xie Zheng raised a hand and smoothed the feathers atop its head, his gaze still fixed on the distant current. Only after a long while did he remove the letter tied to its leg.

He read it in a glance, *three lines at a time*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="This is an idiomatic way of saying he reads extremely quickly."></sup>. The letter crumbled into scraps between his fingers.

He gave one last look toward distant Lin’an Town and said, “Let’s go. It’s time to return.”

In Jizhou Prefecture.

An urgent report from Jinzhou arrived at the Jizhou Prefectural Office. Every official in the office was stunned upon reading it.

“The Beijue have indeed attacked Jinzhou!”

“Thank heavens Marquis Wu’an survived Chongzhou! With Marquis Wu’an stationed in Jinzhou, surely those Beijue barbarians will lose their nerve the moment they hear his name!”

He Jingyuan sat at the head of the council chamber, his expression dark as a storm cloud. Before he could say a word, another guard announced from outside: “Lucheng City is under attack! Guo Xinhou, a senior general under Prince Changxin, has surrounded Lucheng with fifty thousand troops!”

The moment these words were spoken, the officials in the council chamber erupted into an even greater uproar.

How long had it been since Prince Changxin’s Shizi led a group of death soldiers disguised as farmers to incite the people of Qingping County into rebellion?

If the unrest in Qingping County had not been suppressed, and the common people had truly risen in revolt, Lucheng—Jizhou’s first major military stronghold bordering Chongzhou—would have Qingping County directly behind it. By then, Lucheng would truly be caught between enemies on both sides.

An official cursed furiously, “The rebels clearly planned this long ago! With Jinzhou sending an urgent appeal for aid, the elite troops Marquis Wu’an has stationed in Huizhou will inevitably be transferred to Jinzhou. There will be no strength left to hold back the rebels! They intend to use this opportunity to swallow up the northwestern territories!”

A military officer said, “The immediate priority is that the rebels are already at Lucheng’s gates. We must figure out how to defend Jizhou.”

Once Lucheng City fell, Jizhou Prefecture would lose its protective barrier.

Amid the uproar, He Jingyuan said, “Guo Xinhou is a veteran general skilled in the art of war. I shall go to Lucheng myself and oversee the defence.”

“**Daren**, absolutely not! Lucheng is extremely dangerous at present. The rebels have fifty thousand troops bearing down on it, while Lucheng has only twenty thousand soldiers. If anything were to happen to *you*, even if we died ten thousand deaths, we could never atone for it!”

Amid the chorus of objections, He Jingyuan raised a hand, signaling the officials below not to say another word. “If going there is dangerous for me, are the soldiers defending Lucheng not in danger as well? Once I arrive, the rebels will be wary of me, and Lucheng will actually be in less danger. It will also give all of you sufficient time to recruit more soldiers from among the populace.”

As soon as the council meeting ended, cavalrymen carrying conscription orders galloped toward the various commanderies and counties.

<br><br><br>

Lin’an Town.

Fan Changyu had spent the entire afternoon brooding over Xie Zheng’s reckless behavior.

She opened the book on the table, hoping to distract herself by reading, but the moment she saw the dense, tiny annotations covering its pages, another lump formed in her chest, neither rising nor sinking.

Those annotations had all been written by him during the nights he stayed up late.

As her anger gradually subsided, she thought of how he had said he might die at the hands of his enemies, and a strange, unpleasant feeling settled in her heart.

Had he always been so determined to leave because he was carrying some great vendetta?

She stepped out of the room and passed through the main hall, where she saw the pile of things she had prepared for him still sitting on the table. The divorce agreement were there too. Both sheets bore only her name; he had not signed or stamped them. Her feelings grew even more complicated.

Changning and Yu Bao’er had gone out to play with the children in the alley and had yet to return.

Fan Changyu walked to the door of the south room. After hesitating for a moment, she finally knocked.

There was no response from inside.

Fan Changyu pressed her lips together and knocked twice more before calling, “Yan Zheng, are you in there?”

The only answer was continued silence.

Remembering the harsh words she had thrown at him in the heat of the moment, Fan Changyu worried that Yan Zheng might have left without saying goodbye. She pushed the door open with force, and when she saw that he had not taken any of his belongings with him either, her heart immediately settled back into place.

Then he had probably gone out to clear his head?

Fan Changyu closed the door and was just about to return to her room when she heard a commotion outside the alley—cries and wails mixed with soldiers’ shouts and curses.

“**Junyé<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. Soldier Lord"></sup>**! **Junyé**! He’s my only son! Please, have mercy on us mother and son…”

“The rebels are about to attack Jizhou! If your sons don’t take the battlefield, are they going to wait until the rebels come and massacre the entire prefecture?”

Fan Changyu’s heart lurched. She opened the courtyard gate to look outside and saw armored soldiers carrying weapons barging into homes one after another to seize able-bodied men.

The woman sitting on the ground, crying and wailing, was none other than Granny Kang.

She clung to her son and refused to let go, but she still could not match the strength of several burly soldiers. Her son was dragged away under military escort.

Granny Kang wailed, “My son! Don’t be afraid! I’ll go to the Song family right now and find Song **Juren**. I’ll ask him to plead with the county magistrate and have them let you come home!”

The moment Fan Changyu saw that the soldiers were wearing the uniforms of the Jizhou troop, she knew that appealing to the county magistrate would be useless—unless the magistrate was willing to lower himself to curry favor with the officer in charge of conscription and offer him some benefits.

She immediately began worrying about Yan Zheng.

Once someone was seized for military service, they could not return home until the war was over. And most would die on the battlefield, likely without even a place where their remains could be buried.

The children who had been playing outside witnessed the commotion and no longer dared to misbehave. They each ran home.

Changning brought Yu Bao’er to their gate, and the two of them hurriedly hid behind Fan Changyu, peeking out timidly with half their heads to watch the soldiers storming into the alley.

Changning nervously tilted her head up at Fan Changyu and asked, “**Ah Jie**, Yanzi’s eldest brother was taken away by those soldiers. Will **Jiefu** be taken away too?”

Fan Changyu was uncertain herself. This was her first time witnessing conscription.

She had previously heard Auntie Zhao say that it was possible to pay silver to exempt one person from conscription, but judging from what she was seeing this time, that method did not seem to work.

She ushered the two children into the courtyard, saying, “Go inside first.”

She had barely closed the courtyard gate when the *Shizhang* from the alley arrived at her gate with the soldiers.
<div class="author-note">
什长 - The head of a shi (a group of ten) households. Here, the shizhang is responsible for the ten households in the alley and is required to report their household situation to the authorities.
</div>

Under the laws of the current dynasty, common households were organized into groups of five households, called a wu (wu=5), and ten households, called a shi (shi=10). Taxes and conscription were administered according to these neighboring groups of ten households. If anyone was found concealing a person from the authorities, all ten households would be held collectively responsible.

The Shizhang looked rather sheepish as he truthfully explained Fan Changyu’s situation to the soldiers: “This is the head of this household. Surname Fan, named Changyu. She took a husband into her family.”

The soldiers were surprised to hear that she had taken a husband into her household. Seeing that Fan Changyu was the only one outside and that the courtyard gate was still firmly shut, their expressions darkened. “Where is your husband?” one of them barked.

Fan Changyu pressed her lips tightly together. If she said that she and Yan Zheng had already divorced, while the divorce agreement inside still lacked Yan Zheng’s thumbprint, she would undoubtedly be putting the other nine households in danger.

But if she allowed Yan Zheng to be taken away, he would be suffering an entirely undeserved misfortune.

After thinking it over again and again, Fan Changyu answered truthfully, “He is not at home.”

The soldier seemed to have heard that excuse countless times before. His expression darkened as he raised his foot to kick the gate open.

The soldier beside him, who was holding the register and was apparently literate, had already found Fan Changyu’s entry in the Lin’an Town household register. He hurriedly called out to his companion, “Wait.”

He examined the register carefully once more, then looked at Fan Changyu. “Fan Changyu, is that right?”

Fan Changyu answered calmly and without servility, “Yes, it is **minnǚ**.”
<div class="author-note">
民女: A humble self-reference used by a woman of ordinary status when speaking to an official or someone of higher status. Literally “this common woman”.
</div>

The literate soldier said to his companion, “Her husband is already on the conscription register. He must have been among the group of men we seized on the road just now.”

Fan Changyu’s heart pounded wildly. She hurriedly asked, “My husband has already been taken away? **Junyé**, are you certain you didn’t mistake him for someone else?”

The literate soldier glanced at the register. “Isn’t your husband’s name Yan Zheng?”

The moment she heard that name, the last sliver of hope in Fan Changyu vanished.

In a hoarse voice, she said, “It is my husband.”

The Shizhang led the soldiers on to the next household to knock on their door, while Fan Changyu crouched down at the courtyard entrance, her hands and feet turning cold.

With Yan Zheng’s skill, if he wanted to leave, there was no way these soldiers could have stopped him.

He had read so many books and was well versed in the law. Was it because he was afraid of implicating those nine neighboring households that he had willingly allowed himself to be taken away?

Fan Changyu thought of the pile of things she had prepared for him on the table inside, as well as their recent unpleasant parting. The tightness in her chest grew heavier and more painful. She did not know whether it was guilt or something else.

She sat there in a daze for a while. Then, as though suddenly remembering something, she raised her head and asked the soldier who was knocking on the next door, “**Junyé**, where is my husband now? Can I still see him one more time? He was taken away outside, so I want to bring him some things.”

The soldier glanced at Fan Changyu. “The batch that was seized on the road has already been escorted to the county seat. They’re about to set out with the main army for Lucheng. Whether you can catch up if you hurry there now, I can’t say.”

Fan Changyu immediately thanked him. After entrusting Changning and Yu Bao’er to the neighboring auntie, she rushed inside, grabbed the bundle of things from the table, stuffed two more packets of dried tangerine-peel candies inside, and hurried toward the county seat.

Thinking the ox cart was too slow, she directly borrowed a horse from someone and rode to the county gates. Yet she was still a step too late. The first batch of conscripts from the county had already departed for Lucheng with the garrison troops.

Apart from those whose names were on the conscription register, ordinary people were still not permitted to freely enter or leave Qingping County.

The snow was falling heavily. Fan Changyu stood at the city gate, leading her horse with one hand and carrying the large bundle with the other, staring at the official road stretching into the distance beyond the gate opening.

The heaviness in her chest became unbearable. Without saying a word, she turned and led the horse back.

On the way, someone bumped into her, and the things in her bundle spilled all over the ground. Fan Changyu silently picked them up one by one. When she came to the two packets of dried tangerine-peel candies, she picked up one piece and put it in her mouth.

She thought, it’s a good thing that she didn’t catch up. These two packets of dried tangerine-peel candies she bought are too sour. They’re not as sweet as the ones before.

Even if she’d given them to Yan Zheng, he probably wouldn’t have liked them either.

After gathering everything up, Fan Changyu hung the bundle from the saddle, but then rested her forehead against the saddle for quite some time.

How did things end up like this?

She was angry with him, but he had been taken away for conscription without her even getting the chance to say goodbye. She could not shake the feeling that she owed him something.

On her way back to the town, she happened to encounter the soldiers escorting the second batch of newly conscripted men toward the county seat.

Family members followed along the road, weeping and sobbing as they saw their loved ones off. The men who had been conscripted also had red-rimmed eyes, repeatedly telling their families to stop following them.

Fan Changyu discovered that Carpenter Zhao—well along in years—was actually among them.

Unable to hold herself back, she called out, “Zhao **Shu**, why are you going to Lucheng too?”

Carpenter Zhao screwed up his old face, bitterness filling his voice. “This old man chose the wrong trade, that’s why. When I was young, I was a veterinary doctor; when I got old, I became a carpenter. Those military masters said that in the army, I can treat the warhorses and build defensive equipment.”

The soldiers drove the crowd onward with their whips, urging them to move faster.

Afraid that Carpenter Zhao, at his age, might simply drop dead from the journey, Fan Changyu hesitated only briefly before saying, “Zhao **Shu**, take this horse!”

The soldiers were about to drive Fan Changyu away when she approached, but the moment they heard that she was giving away a horse, they immediately turned a blind eye.

A horse was a valuable thing. It could carry both people and cargo, and if an attack occurred, someone on horseback could flee quickly and might even survive.

Carpenter Zhao refused. “This horse is worth a fortune! How could I possibly take it?”

Fan Changyu handed him the reins. “Take it with you. The things in the bundle are things I prepared for Yan Zheng. I couldn't catch up with him. Zhao **Shu**, if you go to Lucheng and see Yan Zheng, please give these things to him for me.”

Hearing this, Carpenter Zhao no longer refused. He felt sorry for the young couple as well and said, “Don’t worry. As long as these old bones of mine are still alive, I’ll make sure I get these things to him.”

Fan Changyu watched Carpenter Zhao disappear into the distance before walking back to town on foot. Once there, she took out her silver and paid the owner what she owed for the horse.

When Fan Changyu went to Auntie Zhao’s house to fetch Changning and Yu Bao’er, Auntie Zhao burst into tears upon hearing that Fan Changyu had bought Carpenter Zhao a horse, repeatedly thanking her through her sobs.

If a conscript brought his own horse with him, that horse would remain his personal property as a soldier. Once he arrived at the military camp, he would generally be assigned to the cavalry.

Those who were not physically fit enough for the cavalry would not be treated poorly.

After comforting Auntie Zhao for a while, Fan Changyu took Changning and Yu Bao’er home. The two children seemed to have noticed that someone was missing from the household and were unusually quiet. Wrapped in the silence, Fan Changyu felt more and more that the house had somehow become terribly empty.

How strange.

It wasn’t as though Yan Zheng had ever been a talkative person.

So why did everything suddenly feel different now that he was gone?

Fan Changyu went to the south room to tidy up and found that the writing desk he had used was already neat and orderly, barely requiring any work from her.

In one corner of the desk lay a pair of leather bracers, with tools such as files beside them. A sheet of paper was tucked underneath.

Judging by the size of the bracers, they did not look like they were meant for Yan Zheng.

Fan Changyu picked them up and looked at the paper. Only eight characters were written on it:

“Joy on your birthday; may you know lasting happiness and freedom from worry<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="in Chinese, 生辰欢喜，长乐无忧 (shēngchén huānxǐ, zhǎnglè wú yōu)."></sup>.”

The memory of Yan Zheng asking about her birthday came rushing back, and Fan Changyu suddenly felt as though the pair of bracers in her hands weighed a thousand jin.

She lowered her eyes and examined them closely. She noticed that one of them seemed to have been filed and reshaped. Once fastened around her wrist, the leather fit her perfectly.

When Fan Changyu tried to undo the clasp, she could not tell whether her hands were trembling slightly or whether the knuckles with which she had punched Yan Zheng hard in the face were still throbbing faintly. Either way, she tried several times without managing to undo the bracers.

In the end, she simply gave up. Leaning back against the chair, she stared blankly at the bracers on her hand, an inexplicable emptiness settling in her chest.
