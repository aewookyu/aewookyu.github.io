A disturbance arose below the city tower, while the sound of approaching hoofbeats drew nearer in the distance. The northern wind roared, whipping the tattered banners still clinging to the tower.

The embrace was brief, as though Xie Zheng had pulled her into his arms only to let the momentum of hauling her up dissipate naturally.

Before Fan Changyu could recover, Xie Zheng had already released her. His voice was icy as he left her with a single instruction, “Stay on the tower. Do not go down.”

Having said this, he took up his long-handled saber, seized a rope with an eagle-claw hook attached to its end, and slid down from the tower like a hawk skimming low over the ground.

Fan Changyu scrambled to her feet and braced both hands against the parapet<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="女墙 -the low protective wall along the edge of a city wall or tower, the low crenellated protective wall where defenders could take cover."></sup> to look down. She saw only Xie Zheng, saber in hand, pursuing Sui Yuanqing.

Many among the rebelling farmers were undercover agents Sui Yuanqing had planted in advance. Dressed just like ordinary farmers, they darted through the crowd, sowing chaos. Thousands of people were packed together in a disorderly mass, and with people constantly pushing and weaving their way through, the crush was so dense that Xie Zheng struggled to advance.

Standing atop the tower, Fan Changyu had a clear view of Sui Yuanqing’s movements. Pointing toward a particular direction, she shouted down at Xie Zheng, “That *bastard*’s running southwest!”
<div class="author-note">
The word she used here was 瘪犊子 (Biě dúzǐ) which is a strongly colloquial insult. It literally means a worthless or malformed calf.
</div>

Hearing Fan Changyu’s warning, Xie Zheng planted a foot on the shoulders of the farmers packed tightly together and vaulted into the air, heading southwest in pursuit of Sui Yuanqing.

The death warriors concealed among the farmers immediately swarmed toward him to intercept his advance. Xie Zheng forced several back with a single sweep of his saber. Taking advantage of the patched short hemp jackets they wore—identical to those of ordinary farmhands—some of the death warriors pretended to be farmers and shouted, “That guy in the green demon mask is killing people!”

“I wasn't one of those men who just stormed the tower! What are you swinging your blade at me for?”

Some farmers, unaware of what was really happening, saw Xie Zheng fighting men dressed in short jackets and assumed he was attacking ordinary civilians. Enraged, they too grabbed whatever weapons they had at hand and rushed over to surround him.

Xie Zheng could still strike with lethal precision against a group of death warriors, but when faced with a crowd of farmers who had been deceived into attacking him, he could only hold back. For a time, they kept him pinned down, leaving him unable to break free—and allowing Sui Yuanqing to reach the edge of the crowd under the protection of his followers.

The two men regarded each other from across the crowd. Sui Yuanqing flashed Xie Zheng a taunting smile.

Beneath the blue demon mask, Xie Zheng’s eyes turned icy cold.

From atop the city tower, Fan Changyu had witnessed Sui Yuanqing escape using such a shameless trick. In a fit of anger, she punched the crenellated wall.

The already dilapidated parapet crumbled a little further beneath her fist.

Fan Changyu froze. She looked at the wall, where clumps of mud and debris were pattering down, then at her own hand. She glanced at Head Constable Wang and the County Magistrate, both staring at her in stunned disbelief, before decisively backing away several steps and putting as much distance as possible between herself and the wall.

She absolutely could not get saddled with a repair bill!

<br>

He Jingyuan had already led his army to block the only official road outside Qingping County's city gate. Seeing the county's residents milling about in chaos outside the walls, he had no idea what was happening.

When he spotted soldiers wearing Jizhou military uniforms mixed in among the crowd, his aged eyelids drooped slightly. “Why are Jizhou troops here?”

He instructed one of his personal guards: “Use signal flags to summon the Jizhou soldiers mixed in with the crowd.”

On a battlefield, the din of combat was deafening, making shouted commands impossible to hear. Everything—from attacking and defending to advancing and retreating—depended on flag signals.

The guard who received He Jingyuan's order hurriedly fetched two small flags and began signaling to the Jizhou soldiers who had reached the outskirts of the crowd. The soldiers saw them, but instead of coming over, they promptly ran in the opposite direction.

The personal guard looked up at He Jingyuan, “**Jiangjun**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="general"></sup>, what do *you* make of this…?”

He Jingyuan replied in a low voice, “They are not soldiers of my Jizhou Prefecture army. For all we know, they may be affiliated with that unregistered force Wenchang went to suppress. Seize them!”

A young officer hurriedly led several dozen men in pursuit of Sui Yuanqing and his fleeing party.

Meanwhile, the death warriors concealed among the crowd began herding farmers into the path of the pursuing troops to impede them, all the while shouting:

“The soldiers are killing people!”

“The authorities never gave a damn about our lives!”

“This is a tyrannical court! If rebellion is what it takes, then rebellion it is!”

Amid the chaos, several death warriors took the opportunity to stab a few of the soldiers pursuing Sui Yuanqing to death. The surviving troops assumed their comrades had been killed by the rebelling civilians. Enraged, they too swung their blades without hesitation at the commoners who kept blocking their way.

When the civilians saw the soldiers begin killing indiscriminately, some shrank back into the crowd, fearing for their lives, while others, overcome with fury, raised their hoes and rakes and charged the soldiers in a desperate fight for survival.

He Jingyuan watched the two groups descend into chaos, his brows drawn into a deep, tight frown.

Another general under his command was gnashing his teeth at the sight. He stepped forward and said, “**Daren**, I'll take a thousand troops to suppress the rioters and reinforce Colonel Hu!”

As He Jingyuan deliberated, a man in black suddenly burst through the crowd. He carried a yanyue saber, his tall, slender figure concealed beneath a green demon mask. 
<div class="author-note">
偃刀 — yanyue saber. A broad-bladed weapon mounted on a long shaft.
</div>

In a rough, hoarse voice, he said to He Jingyuan, “The fugitive wearing Jizhou military uniform is Sui Yuanqing, the second son of Prince Changxin. His men have disguised themselves as rebels and infiltrated the crowd to sow discord.”

He Jingyuan thought, “so that's why.” Studying the young man before him, he couldn't help asking, “May I know who this brave hero is?”

Xie Zheng replied coldly, “Only a common wanderer, unworthy of offering my name before **Daren**.”

Even as he spoke, his gaze had already shifted toward the young officer who had spoken earlier. “Lend me your horse and bow.”

The young officer felt a sudden weight at his collar. Before he knew it, he had been yanked from his horse. He staggered several steps before regaining his balance, then looked up to see the man already riding away.

Indignant, the young officer shouted after him: “How great the audacity—!”

But when his gaze fell upon He Jingyuan, he immediately fell silent, lowering his head in shame.

The man had taken his horse right under his nose, and he had been utterly powerless to fight back. Clearly, he was simply outmatched.

He Jingyuan offered no words of reprimand. His expression complicated, he watched Xie Zheng ride away for a while before finally ordering the soldiers beneath him, “Sound the horns and *form ranks*<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="列阵: To arrange troops into battle formation."></sup>.”

The rebels were in complete disarray. Only by first bringing them under control could they minimize casualties as much as possible.

A mournful lowing rose from the ox horns. Shield-bearing soldiers formed ranks at the front, striking their thick shields with the sabers in their hands. At the same time, several thousand soldiers let out a unified battle cry—a thunderous roar that seemed capable of tearing the clouds from the sky.

The spectacle was undeniably imposing, and it successfully cowed every rebel present.

The farmers still held their farming tools pointed toward the soldiers with swords and shields, but their faces were filled with alarm, and they instinctively began shrinking back.

He Jingyuan spoke, “I am He Jingyuan, Governor of Jizhou Prefecture. Every one of you is a commoner under my jurisdiction. Why have you risen in rebellion?”

When the people heard that the man leading the army was He Jingyuan, a low murmur of discussion spread through the crowd. Though they still held their farming tools aloft, the sharpness had left their expressions. Some had even begun quietly sobbing.

After a moment, someone lowered his farming implement and fell to his knees, crying out bitterly: “He **Daren**, *you* must seek justice for us!”

Once the first cluster of people knelt, those behind them gradually followed suit, setting down their tools and dropping to their knees. Cries rose from every direction, “We were driven to this! We had no other choice!”

Even those who remained unwilling to submit understood that the tide had turned. These farmers, who knew little beyond swinging their hoes, could gain nothing by confronting a well-trained army. Rebellion was a grave offense punishable by the extermination of nine branches of one's family. Better to bow their heads and admit fault now, plead for mercy, and hope that with so many people involved, the law might let the matter pass without punishing them all.

For a time, the entire area beneath the city tower was filled with the cries of the common people—some genuinely pouring out their grievances, others putting on an act for fear of punishment.

But regardless, the riot had been brought under control.

The County Magistrate collapsed onto the city tower, gasping for breath. Thinking of how he had nearly lost his life to a blade, the rolls of flesh on his face were still trembling. He turned to Head Constable Wang and said, “Constable Wang, you saved **Ben Guan**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. this official"></sup>’s life. **Ben Guan** will certainly reward you handsomely afterward.”

Head Constable Wang himself had taken more blows from blades than he could count. Dragging his bloodied body along, he pushed aside the corpse of a dead death warriors and used his sleeve to wipe the blood from the face of a young constable. His eyes red, he forced a smile and said, “It was all in the line of duty. If **Daren** wishes to reward, then give the families of these young men a little more in the way of relief money.”

He looked at the dead young constable, “This is Xiao Wu<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="lit. means Little Wu/Little Five."></sup>, the youngest constable in the yamen. He was the most filial of them all. He has an eighty-year-old grandmother at home who’s blind. Every month when he received his wages, he would go to the butcher’s shop and buy a piece of meat to take home and cook into a meat porridge for her to eat. The one beside him is Li Da. His wife is still carrying their child—she’s due in another two months. Now the pillar of that household is gone. Who knows what that whole family will do…”

By the end, it was as though cotton had lodged in Head Constable Wang’s throat, and he could not force out another word. He merely covered his eyes with his hands, still smeared with blood.

Fan Changyu looked at the fallen constables, her lips pressed into a hard line.

She looked down from the city tower, but she could no longer see Xie Zheng or Sui Yuanqing and his men anywhere.

Sui Yuanqing and several of his personal guards had already fled in the direction they had planned beforehand by the time He Jingyuan sent men after them.

The death warriors brought up the rear, holding off the pursuing troops. Mu Shi protected Sui Yuanqing as they ran, firing a whistling arrow into the sky as a signal. Yet the thousand troops they had concealed at Banpo hillside were nowhere to be seen.

As Mu Shi saw more and more pursuers closing in, with more than half their death warriors already dead or wounded and no longer able to hold back the pursuing troops, he was about to fire a second whistling arrow when Sui Yuanqing said, “There’s no need.”

His lips curled into a cold smile as he forcibly suppressed his anger. “The one leading the troops here is He Jingyuan, not Wei Xuan. He is, after all, a famed general of Great Yin. It seems he has already discovered the thousand men we had lying in wait at Banpo hillside.”

Mu Shi realized the gravity of the situation, and his heart sank. “**Beizhi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="this subordinate"></sup> will protect **Shizi**<sup class="footnote-ref" data-footnote="title for the heir apparent"></sup> with my life and see **Shizi** safely back to Chongzhou.”

Sui Yuanqing merely smiled nonchalantly. He even slowed his pace. The cavalrymen behind them soon caught up, firing arrows at them as they urged their horses closer.

When Sui Yuanqing dodged an arrow, he caught it out of the air with his free hand. As a warhorse galloped past him, he seized its reins and swung himself onto its back.

The cavalryman atop the horse was startled and hurriedly slashed at him with his saber. Sui Yuanqing leaned back to evade the blow, then drove the arrowhead in his hand straight into the cavalryman’s throat.

The cavalryman died on the spot. Sui Yuanqing seized his corpse and flung it from the horse.

Mu Shi had also seized another cavalryman’s horse and ridden up alongside him. Sui Yuanqing flashed a roguish grin. “Four legs still get us back to Chongzhou faster than two.”

Having taken the cavalrymen’s horses, they no longer paid the Jizhou troops behind them any mind.

Whoosh!

A white-fletched arrow shot past Sui Yuanqing’s ear, so close that he could feel the wind it tore through the air. It buried itself viciously in the frozen earth several zhang ahead (1 zhang = 3.3 meter), its white feathers trembling at the end of the shaft.

Everyone was startled. Had that arrow been aimed at Sui Yuanqing, the arrowhead would have entered through his back and come straight out through his chest.

Even Sui Yuanqing lost the reckless look on his face as he stared at the arrow that had landed not far away, then turned to study the person who had fired it.

The official road had been churned into mud beneath countless hooves. Thin patches of snow still clung to the treetops in the mountain forests on either side. At the far end of the road stood the man wearing the green demon mask, his long-handled yanyue saber planted casually in the ground, a large bow held in one hand.

An arrow was already nocked on the bowstring, yet he was not taking deliberate aim. Beneath the mask, his eyes were cool and indifferent, almost languid.

At a single glance, Sui Yuanqing’s expression turned grim.

He shouted, “Scatter!”

Though the personal guards who had seized the horses did not understand why, they immediately split up and fled in different directions.

The corners of Xie Zheng’s lips curled coldly. The instant he released the bowstring, sending the arrowhead flying, a second arrow was already nocked and ready.

His movements were astonishingly fast. Arrows flew like a sudden shower of meteors. In the blink of an eye, more than a dozen arrows had been loosed, and every one of Sui Yuanqing’s personal guards had been knocked from their horses.

Seeing the guards on either side of him take arrows and tumble from their saddles, Sui Yuanqing had no time to worry about the man shooting from behind. He merely gritted his teeth, clamped his legs hard against the horse’s belly, and urged it forward, keeping his body as low and close to the horse’s back as possible.

The quiver on Xie Zheng’s saddle was already empty. As he rode after them, he passed a fallen cavalryman lying on the ground. Without breaking stride, he reached back and snatched away the man’s quiver, plucked out a handful of arrows with one hand, and tossed the empty quiver aside.

Of Sui Yuanqing’s personal guards, only Mu Shi remained, riding close behind him. Mu Shi glanced back, his eyes nearly splitting with fury, and shouted, “**Shizi**, look out!”

At the warning, Sui Yuanqing glanced back as well and was horrified to see that the man in the green demon mask was holding nearly ten arrows between his fingers. They were nocked onto the bow in a fan-shaped spread.

The instant he released the bowstring, that entire cluster of arrows came hurtling toward Sui Yuanqing like a swarm of flying locusts.

Sui Yuanqing was more shaken than he had ever been in his life. Never before had he witnessed such extraordinary archery, and he could not help wondering who might be beneath that green demon mask.

With no other choice, he twisted around on horseback and raised his sword to desperately fend off the incoming arrows. But an arrow struck the warhorse in the leg. With a cry of pain, it collapsed, sending Sui Yuanqing tumbling to the ground. He rolled several times before finally bleeding off the momentum.

The sound of hoofbeats drew closer. With only a few zhang between them, the man in the green demon mask did not hurry forward. Instead, he drew in the reins and let his horse approach at an unhurried pace, his posture utterly relaxed.

Sui Yuanqing’s face turned ashen. This method of toying with his prey like a cat playing with a mouse—wasn’t this exactly what he had done earlier to the little maid from the County Magistrate’s residence on the city tower?

What exactly was this blue demon mask-wearer’s relationship with that little maid?

When he had grabbed her and tried to drag her away, hadn’t that little maid seemed to be calling his name as well?

Fearing that Xie Zheng might harm Sui Yuanqing, Mu Shi charged over on horseback with his spear in hand and shouted, “Do not harm my general!”

Xie Zheng caught the shaft of the incoming spear with one hand. With a twist, he then drove his strength upward, sending Mu Shi flying from his horse while still clutching the spear. His grip had been so tight that the force nearly tore a layer of skin from his palm, and the intense pain made him let go.

Taking the weapon Mu Shi had delivered straight into his hands, Xie Zheng sat steadily atop his horse and held the silver spear against Sui Yuanqing’s throat. His tone carried a faint note of mockery. “Prince Changxin’s Shizi, Sui Yuanqing?”

Sui Yuanqing clenched his teeth so hard that he could taste the faint tang of blood. The veins at his temples bulged, but after a moment, he swallowed his humiliation and burst into loud laughter. “That’s right. It is **Ben Shizi**.”

The raging waters of the Nujiang River flowed directly below the official road. Even in the depths of winter, its swift current kept it from freezing.

Sui Yuanqing cast an inconspicuous glance below. Under the tip of Xie Zheng’s spear, he deliberately made his entire body look as relaxed as possible. “And who might you be? If you intend to take **Ben Shizi**’s life, surely you wouldn’t be afraid to at least state your name.”

Xie Zheng did not answer. If the army captured Sui Yuanqing, they would not kill him here. Taking him to the battlefield at Chongzhou and using him to bargain with Prince Changxin would be far more advantageous.

Sui Yuanqing’s question was deliberate. He was trying to coax information out of Xie Zheng.

Seeing that Xie Zheng refused to take the bait, Sui Yuanqing suddenly gave a roguish grin, “That little maid on the city tower—what is she to you? Her skin was awfully fair, and she tasted sweet when I kissed her.”

Xie Zheng’s gaze turned icy.

That was exactly the moment Sui Yuanqing had been waiting for. He abruptly knocked aside the spear pressed against his throat and dove headfirst toward the river.

Xie Zheng reacted with lightning speed, sweeping the spear horizontally at him and striking him in the waist.

Sui Yuanqing let out a muffled groan. The next instant, his entire body disappeared into the raging river, leaving behind only a spreading stain of blood upon the water.
